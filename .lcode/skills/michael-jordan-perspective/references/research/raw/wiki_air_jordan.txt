'''Air Jordan''' is a line of basketball and sportswear shoes produced by [[Nike, Inc.]] The shoes, related apparel and accessories are now marketed under '''Jordan Brand'''. The first Air Jordan shoe was produced for basketball player [[Michael Jordan]] during his time with the [[Chicago Bulls]] on November 17, 1984, and released to the public on April 1, 1985.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Randy Harvey |date=1985-04-26 |title=Clear The Dunkway, Michael Jordan Is . . .TAKING TO AIR : NBA Star Leaps Into Profitable Shoe Market |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1985-04-26-sp-20979-story.html |access-date=2023-05-10 |website=[[Los Angeles Times]] |archive-date=November 16, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116115724/https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1985-04-26-sp-20979-story.html |url-status=live }}</ref> The shoes were designed for Nike by [[Peter Moore (shoe designer)|Peter Moore]], [[Tinker Hatfield]], and Bruce Kilgore.<ref>{{Cite journal |title=Jordan - Air & Space |url=http://dx.doi.org/10.1163/2213-2996_flg_com_108010 |access-date=2023-05-10 |website=Foreign Law Guide|doi=10.1163/2213-2996_flg_com_108010 |url-access=subscription }}</ref><ref>{{Citation |last=Krasas |first=Jackie |chapter=She Must Have Done Something |date=2021-04-15 |chapter-url=http://dx.doi.org/10.7591/cornell/9781501754296.003.0003 |title=Still a Mother |pages=35–56 |access-date=2023-05-10 |publisher=Cornell University Press|doi=10.7591/cornell/9781501754296.003.0003 |isbn=9781501754296 |s2cid=244330266 |chapter-url-access=subscription }}</ref> The brand's logo, known as the "[[Jumpman (logo)|Jumpman]]", originated from a photograph by [[Co Rentmeester]] that was taken before Jordan played for [[United States men's national basketball team|Team USA]] in the [[1984 Summer Olympics]].

==History==
As [[Michael Jordan]] entered his rookie year in 1984, he was approached to sign a shoe deal with [[Adidas]], [[Converse (lifestyle wear)|Converse]], and [[Nike, Inc.|Nike]]. In their meeting with Jordan, Nike centered its presentation around a highlight video of Jordan's various [[slam dunk]]s, scored to "[[Jump (For My Love)]]" by the [[Pointer Sisters]]. Nike showcased the first design of the shoe, but Jordan criticized its colorway. While other companies saw Jordan as a figure for promoting preexisting shoe lines, Nike took Jordan's criticism into account to make him "a stand alone star and give him a signature shoe line."<ref name="Wertheim">{{Cite web |last=Wertheim |first=L. Jon |date=September 6, 2021 |title=The Birth of Air Jordans |url=https://www.sportsbusinessjournal.com/Journal/Issues/2021/09/06/Portfolio/Book-excerpt.aspx |access-date=2023-05-11 |website=[[Sports Business Journal]] |archive-date=May 5, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230505042722/https://www.sportsbusinessjournal.com/Journal/Issues/2021/09/06/Portfolio/Book-excerpt.aspx |url-status=live }}</ref>

On October 26, 1984, Jordan signed a six-year, $2.5 million deal with Nike, three times more than any other deal in the [[National Basketball Association]] (NBA) at the time. In addition, Jordan would get royalties from products sold by Nike with his name, which was at the time considered an industry disruptive move. Nike released the Air Jordan sneaker line in April 1985 with the goal of making $3 million in the first three years. Sales greatly exceeded expectations, earning $126 million in one year.<ref name="38years">{{Cite web |last=Benson |first=Pat |date=October 26, 2022 |title=Michael Jordan & Nike Celebrate 38 Years Together |url=https://www.si.com/fannation/sneakers/news/michael-jordan-nike-celebrate-38-years-together |access-date=2023-03-22 |website=[[FanNation]] |archive-date=March 22, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230322233305/https://www.si.com/fannation/sneakers/news/michael-jordan-nike-celebrate-38-years-together |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="EverythingAM">{{Cite web |last=Gupta |first=Manas Sen |date=2023-03-21 |title='Air': Deep-Diving Into The Legendary Partnership Between Michael Jordan And Nike |url=https://www.augustman.com/hk/amselect/hit-list/everything-to-know-about-michael-jordan-and-nike-association/ |access-date=2023-03-22 |website=[[August Man]] |archive-date=June 11, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230611175231/https://www.augustman.com/hk/amselect/hit-list/everything-to-know-about-michael-jordan-and-nike-association/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="TempleNow">{{Cite web |last=Baum |first=Brent |date=April 3, 2023 |title=How Michael Jordan revolutionized the sneaker industry—and our relationship to shoes |url=https://news.temple.edu/news/2023-04-03/how-michael-jordan-revolutionized-sneaker-industry-and-our-relationship-shoes |access-date=2023-05-11 |website=[[Temple University|Temple Now]] |archive-date=November 15, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251115084853/https://news.temple.edu/news/2023-04-03/how-michael-jordan-revolutionized-sneaker-industry-and-our-relationship-shoes |url-status=live }}</ref>{{Disputed inline|First year sales, $26 million vs $126 million|date=June 2023}}

NBA policy stated that the shoes must be 51% white and consistent with the shoes that the rest of the team wore. Failure to follow this policy resulted in a $5,000 fine per game ({{Inflation|US|5000|1984|fmt=eq|r=-3}}). Nike designed the Air Jordan I based on the [[Chicago Bulls]]' red and black team colors with only 23% white, which violated the NBA's policy. Nike agreed to pay each fine, garnering both controversy and publicity around the shoe.<ref name="TempleNow" /> Fines imposed by the NBA on Jordan for wearing the shoes bestowed upon them an iconic brand and was later regarded as groundbreaking in part due to its defiance of NBA regulations.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Petrik |first=Louis |date=2020-08-17 |title=How the Air Jordan 1 Became Iconic |url=https://bettermarketing.pub/how-the-air-jordan-1-became-iconic-79fd6164485a |access-date=2023-05-11 |website=[[Medium (website)|Medium]]}}</ref> Nike also took advantage of this marketing opportunity with the Air Jordan I "Banned" advertisement, which stated "On September 15th, Nike created a revolutionary new basketball shoe. On October 18th, the NBA threw them out of the game."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Taylor |first=David |date=2023-04-16 |title=How Air Jordan helped Nike fly high |url=https://thebrandgym.com/how-air-jordan-helped-nike-fly-high/ |access-date=2023-05-11 |website=brandgym |language=en-GB |archive-date=May 14, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230514021514/https://thebrandgym.com/how-air-jordan-helped-nike-fly-high/ |url-status=live }}</ref> The shoe sold out of the initial 50,000 pairs and ultimately generated more than $150 million in sales.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2021-10-19 |title="NBA Can't Stop You": Michael Jordan Destroyed The League in This Scathing Ad{{!}} NBA News |url=https://www.essentiallysports.com/basketball-nba-news-nba-cant-stop-you-michael-jordan-destroyed-the-league-in-this-scathing-ad/ |access-date=2023-05-11 |website=EssentiallySports |archive-date=May 5, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230505010405/https://www.essentiallysports.com/basketball-nba-news-nba-cant-stop-you-michael-jordan-destroyed-the-league-in-this-scathing-ad/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

In 1997, Jordan and Nike introduced Jordan Brand (originally called "Brand Jordan"). The brand has built a sustainable business model by releasing Air Jordan shoes and apparel, and collaborating with popular artists.<ref name="38years" /><ref>{{Cite web |title=Nike, Michael Jordan to introduce new brand Basketball shoes, sportswear to be sold under star's name; Apparel |url=https://www.baltimoresun.com/1997/09/10/nike-michael-jordan-to-introduce-new-brand-basketball-shoes-sportswear-to-be-sold-under-stars-name-apparel/ |access-date=2023-05-11 |website=[[The Baltimore Sun]] |date=September 10, 1997 |archive-date=February 3, 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260203071250/https://www.baltimoresun.com/1997/09/10/nike-michael-jordan-to-introduce-new-brand-basketball-shoes-sportswear-to-be-sold-under-stars-name-apparel/ |url-status=live }}</ref> In 2022 alone, Jordan Brand brought in $5.1 billion to Nike. Of that, a reported $150–256 million went directly to Jordan under his royalties deal with Nike.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Silva |first=Orlando |date=June 30, 2022 |title=The Jordan Brand Reached Over $5 Billion In Annual Revenue For The First Time, Michael Jordan Made $150M+ Just From Nike Last Year |url=https://fadeawayworld.net/nba-media/the-jordan-brand-reached-over-5-billion-in-annual-revenue-for-the-first-time-michael-jordan-made-150m-just-from-nike-last-year |access-date=May 11, 2023 |website=Fadeaway World |archive-date=October 6, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251006204731/https://fadeawayworld.net/nba-media/the-jordan-brand-reached-over-5-billion-in-annual-revenue-for-the-first-time-michael-jordan-made-150m-just-from-nike-last-year |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last1=Poindexter |first1=Owen |title=Nike, Jordan Score With Jordan Brand's Record Haul |url=https://frontofficesports.com/jordan-more-than-doubled-his-nba-career-earnings-in-2022-from-nike-deal/ |website=Front Office Sports |access-date=11 May 2023 |language=en |date=30 January 2023 |archive-date=March 11, 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260311133212/https://frontofficesports.com/jordan-more-than-doubled-his-nba-career-earnings-in-2022-from-nike-deal/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

==Logo==
{{Main|Jumpman (logo)}}
The "Jumpman" logo originated from a photo shoot Michael Jordan did for [[Life (magazine)|''Life'' magazine]] at the [[University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill]] campus before he played for Team USA in the [[1984 Summer Olympics]], photographed by [[Co Rentmeester]]. Jordan posed in a manner identical to the [[grand jeté]] ballet technique, while holding a basketball with his left hand, and wearing his Olympic jumpsuit and [[New Balance]] shoes.<ref name="Halfhill">{{Cite web |last=Halfhill |first=Matt |date=2022-08-16 |title=Jordan Logo Meaning, Designer, History |url=https://www.nicekicks.com/history-behind-jordan-jumpman-logo/ |access-date=2023-05-10 |website=Nice Kicks |language=en-US |archive-date=February 24, 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260224210526/https://www.nicekicks.com/history-behind-jordan-jumpman-logo/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine |url=https://www.life.com/arts-entertainment/the-amazing-story-behind-jumpman/ |title=The Amazing Story Behind "Jumpman" |author=Bill Syken |magazine=Life |date=2024-05-31 |access-date=January 24, 2025 |archive-date=February 24, 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260224221535/https://www.life.com/arts-entertainment/the-amazing-story-behind-jumpman/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Moore, who was in charge of the design team, came across this ''Life'' magazine issue and had Jordan replicate the pose, this time in Chicago and wearing his Bulls uniform and Nike Air Jordan shoes.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://footwearnews.com/business/legal-news/nike-lawsuit-michael-jordan-jumpman-logo-1202764580/ |title=Nike Prevails in Lawsuit Over Michael Jordan's Jumpman Logo |author=Samantha McDonald |website=[[Footwear News]] |date=2019-03-26 |access-date=January 24, 2025 |archive-date=April 11, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250411030910/https://footwearnews.com/business/legal-news/nike-lawsuit-michael-jordan-jumpman-logo-1202764580/ |url-status=live }}</ref> The "Jumpman" logo has developed and gone through different changes and can be seen on sneakers, attire, hats, socks, and other forms of wear. It has become one of the most recognizable logos in the athletics industry.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Air Jordan (Jumpman) Logo and symbol, meaning, history, PNG, brand |url=https://1000logos.net/air-jordan-logo/ |access-date=2023-05-10 |archive-date=April 26, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230426234626/https://1000logos.net/air-jordan-logo/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

| style="text-align:center" | [[File:Jordan II (cropped).jpg|center|150x150px]] Air Jordan II
| 1986
| The success of the Air Jordan I encouraged Nike to release a new Air Jordan in 1986 for the new basketball season. Designed by Peter Moore and Bruce Kilgore, the Air Jordan II was originally [[made in Italy]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Stonebrook |first=Ian |date=2021-12-14 |title=The Complete Guide to the Air Jordan 2 |url=https://footwearnews.com/feature/air-jordan-2-guide-1203207992/ |access-date=2023-02-05 |website=[[Footwear News]] |archive-date=February 5, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230205225408/https://footwearnews.com/feature/air-jordan-2-guide-1203207992/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="34years">{{Cite news |last1=Brinson |first1=Jonathon |last2=Berlin |first2=Jemal R. |date=April 22, 2020 |title=34 years of Air Jordans: A look at the sneaker institution that Michael Jordan and Nike built |work=[[Chicago Tribune]] |url=https://www.chicagotribune.com/sports/bulls/ct-cb-michael-jordan-air-jordan-shoes-timeline-20200422-scqnqnmomnhmxbxiahg55vurii-htmlstory.html |access-date=8 February 2021 |archive-date=April 22, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200422213354/https://www.chicagotribune.com/sports/bulls/ct-cb-michael-jordan-air-jordan-shoes-timeline-20200422-scqnqnmomnhmxbxiahg55vurii-htmlstory.html |url-status=live }}</ref> In early tests, Michael Jordan wore a prototype that fused the upper of the original Air Jordan with the cushioning being designed for the new model. The Air Jordan II featured a full-length Air-Sole unit and a polyurethane midsole. The shoe bore a similar silhouette to the Nike Air Python that would release the following year. It featured a faux lizard skin and swooping lines that resembled the detailing of a sports car. This design aesthetic would factor into the Air Jordan line later in the series. The Air Jordan II was the first Jordan not to have the [[Nike swoosh]] on the upper, though "Nike" was stitched across the heel counter.<ref name="34years" /> Michael Jordan wore the Air Jordan II for a shortened 18 games in the 1985–86 season due to a broken foot. It had several re-releases as the Jordan 2 Retro.

|In December 1988, Nike released the Air Jordan IV to the public. Designed by [[Tinker Hatfield]], it was the first Air Jordan released on the global market. It had four colorways: White/Black, Black/Cement Grey, White/Fire Red-Black, and Off White/Military Blue. Nike featured director and actor [[Spike Lee]] in ads for the shoe.<ref>{{cite web |title=Nike Air Jordan 4: A Complete Guide |work=Fastsole |date=October 22, 2021 |url=https://fastsole.co.uk/sneaker-news/nike-air-jordan-4-guide/ |access-date=January 25, 2022 |archive-date=May 18, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240518134326/https://fastsole.co.uk/sneaker-news/nike-air-jordan-4-guide/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> Lee had featured the shoe in his movie ''[[Do The Right Thing]]''.<ref name="34years" />

Michael Jordan wore the Air Jordan IV when he made "[[The Shot (Bulls–Cavaliers)|The Shot]]", a series winner in Game 5 of the 1989 NBA First Round between the [[Chicago Bulls]] and the [[Cleveland Cavaliers]]. In 2012 a Cavalier colorway dubbed the "Cavs" was released to honor "The Shot".

| style="text-align:center" | [[File:Air Jordan VII (Maillot N°9 de la Dream Team) (cropped).JPG|center|150x150px]] Air Jordan VII
| 1992
| The Air Jordan VII introduced "huarache" technology which allowed the shoes to better conform to the user's foot. A few things were no longer featured on the new model, such as the visible air sole, the Nike Air logo, and the translucent soles. This was the first Air Jordan in the line that did not have any distinctive "Nike Air" branding on the outer portions of the shoe, only on the in-soles.

When Jordan went to compete at the [[1992 Summer Olympics]] to play for the US Men's Basketball Team (also known as the "[[1992 United States men's Olympic basketball team|Dream Team]]"), Nike released a special Olympic color combo of the Air Jordan VII model which had Jordan's Olympic jersey number 9, instead of the usual "23" found on other colorways.<ref name="34years" />

Various models of the Air Jordan VII were re-released beginning with its 10-year anniversary in 2002.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Stonebrook |first=Ian |date=2021-12-15 |title=The Definitive Guide to the Air Jordan 7 Sneaker |url=https://footwearnews.com/feature/air-jordan-7-guide-1203207983/ |access-date=2023-02-14 |website=[[Footwear News]] |archive-date=February 14, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230214071555/https://footwearnews.com/feature/air-jordan-7-guide-1203207983/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

| style="text-align:center" | [[File:Air Jordan XI (cropped).jpg|center|150x150px]] Air Jordan XI
| 1995
| This model was designed by Tinker Hatfield. When the shoe launched, Michael Jordan (retired from basketball by then) was with the [[Birmingham Barons]] in baseball's minor baseball leagues. Hatfield designed the sneaker waiting for Jordan to come back and hoping he would play in them.<ref>{{Cite web |title=nickdepaula.com |url=https://nickdepaula.com/TINKER-HATFIELD-AIR-JORDAN-XI |access-date=2025-01-27 |website=nickdepaula.com |archive-date=January 27, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250127100132/https://nickdepaula.com/TINKER-HATFIELD-AIR-JORDAN-XI |url-status=live }}</ref>

The ballistic mesh upper of the sneaker was meant to make the Air Jordan XI lighter and more durable. Further changes came with the use of a carbon fiber spring plate in the translucent outsole, giving the shoe better torque when twisting on the court. The best-known aspect of the shoe is its [[patent leather]] mudguard. Patent leather was lightweight compared to genuine leather and also tended not to stretch as much – a property to help keep the foot within the bounds of the foot bed during directional changes on the court. The patent leather gave the XI a "formal" look. When this shoe released, some wore this model with business suits instead of dress shoes.{{citation needed|date=November 2026}}

The sneakers were only samples in 1995 when Jordan decided to come back to the NBA. Hatfield and Nike discouraged Jordan from playing in them, but once they were produced, he couldn't resist. Also noteworthy, Jordan violated league dress code by wearing the shoes, as his teammates wore all-black shoes. It wasn't the first time Jordan had run afoul of NBA footwear rules, having broken them with his very first signature shoe in 1985. He was fined $5,000 for not following the Bulls' colorway policy with the AJ XI. After the fine, Nike made him a pair of the shoes in a black/white/concord colorway for the series against Orlando; Jordan wore [[Penny Hardaway]]'s signature black Nike Air Flight shoes for Game 3 while said colorway was under production.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2020-05-11 |title=Why Michael Jordan Wore Penny Hardaway's Signature Nike During the Playoffs |url=https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/michael-jordan-penny-hardaway-signature-shoe/ |access-date=2025-01-27 |website=Highsnobiety |language=en}}</ref> A similar black/white/royal blue colorway was released to the public at the end of 2000. The colorway was changed for the public release because the concord purple had looked like royal blue on television.

Jordan wore the Air Jordan XI on the way to helping the Chicago Bulls claim the [[1996 NBA Finals|1995–96 NBA Championship]]. He also wore the XI white Columbia colorway in the [[1996 NBA All-Star Game]] and was selected [[NBA All-Star Game Kobe Bryant Most Valuable Player Award|MVP of the game]]. The shoes received more media exposure when Jordan wore the Air Jordan XI model in the 1996 animated movie ''[[Space Jam]]''. These shoes were eventually released in 2000 and re-released in 2009 and 2016<ref name="Jordans-in-film" /> with the nickname "Space Jams". The concord purple was changed to royal blue for the released versions of the shoe.

The Air Jordan XI is one of the most popular Air Jordans in the series and is Hatfield's favorite.<ref name="Hatfield-favorite">{{Cite web |last=Freeman |first=Thomas |date=2018-07-16 |title=Nike Designer Tinker Hatfield Reveals His Favorite and Least Favorite Air Jordans |url=https://www.maxim.com/style/nike-designer-best-worst-air-jordan-2018-7/ |access-date=2023-03-14 |website=[[Maxim (magazine)|Maxim]] |archive-date=March 15, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230315002716/https://www.maxim.com/style/nike-designer-best-worst-air-jordan-2018-7/ |url-status=live }}</ref> They were also Michael Jordan's favorite.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Gupta |first1=Shatakshi |title=Why is Air Jordan 11 Michael Jordan's Favorite? ;"Defining Moments"
Released in 2006 retailing at $295 containing the sneakers Michael Jordan wore during his first championship of his two three-peats. The Retro 11 Concord contains a gold Jumpman on the side, but originally was meant to also have gold eyelets spelling out Jordan. This was changed because of color bleeding. The retro 6 Black Infrared replaces its infrared for gold as well. Both shoes contained dog tags to reference the title won and a booklet showcasing a slam dunk highlight of the game and concept art of the shoe. Some of the original DMP Retro 11 Concords have surfaced and are considered some of the rarest Air Jordans.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://solecollector.com/news/2015/01/in-context-air-jordan-defining-moments-pack|title=In Context: Air Jordan Defining 'Moments Pack'|access-date=September 24, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160925021017/http://solecollector.com/news/2015/01/in-context-air-jordan-defining-moments-pack|archive-date=September 25, 2016|url-status=dead}}</ref>

;"Defining Moments II"
The "raging bull pack" retailed for $310 and drew inspiration from the [[Running of the Bulls|running of the bull]] that takes place every year in Spain. The pack contains two Air Jordan 5s; the Toro Bravo and the 3m. The Toro Bravo is a red suede sneaker, one of the first of its kind, and it takes inspiration from the red bandanas worn by the runners. The second pair, the 3m, is named after its reflective coating. Both shoes come in a wood gate exterior graphic box with double sided slide out, originally released in 2009.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://sneakernews.com/air-jordan/air-jordan-5/air-jordan-v-5-2009-dmp-retro-black-red-silver-red-black/ |title=Air Jordan V (5) 2009 DMP Retro - Toro Bravo - Raging Bull - SneakerNews.com |language=en-US|access-date=September 24, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161009211446/http://sneakernews.com/air-jordan/air-jordan-5/air-jordan-v-5-2009-dmp-retro-black-red-silver-red-black/|archive-date=October 9, 2016|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Jordan "6 Rings" shoe===
[[File:Air Jordan 6ix rings olympic dream and black red.jpg|thumb|Air Jordan "6 rings" in two colorways]]
The Jordan 6 Rings (aka Jordan Six Rings, Or Montells) is a combination of the seven Air Jordan shoes that Michael Jordan wore during his 6 championship seasons. That includes the Air Jordan 6, 7, 8, 11, 12, 13 and 14. The Jordan Brand company released the 6 Rings shoes starting in September 2008.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.sneakerfiles.com/air-jordans/air-jordan-6-rings-six-rings/|title=Air Jordan 6 Rings Six Rings Colorways|language=en-US|access-date=September 29, 2022|archive-date=September 29, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220929103008/https://www.sneakerfiles.com/air-jordans/air-jordan-6-rings-six-rings/|url-status=live}}</ref>

Jordan Brand released colorways representative of each team that the Chicago Bulls defeated in their six championship seasons during the 1990s: The [[Los Angeles Lakers]], [[Portland Trail Blazers]], [[Phoenix Suns]], [[Seattle SuperSonics]], and [[Utah Jazz]]. The shoes include laser-etched graphics detailing specific aspects about that particular championship series and the city of the competing team. Many other colorways exist.

There also exists a "winterized 6 rings" which are a modified 6 Rings shoe turned into a durable boot designed for the outdoors which changes some of the design and placement of the parts.

==Controversies==
The [[polyurethane]] (PU) material in the soles of many Air Jordan models can break down over time, and many collectors find that their vintage pairs are often unwearable.<ref name="Wired_decay">{{Cite magazine |last=Chun |first=Rene |title=The Sneakerheads Racing to Save Their Kicks From Decay |magazine=[[Wired (magazine)|Wired]] |url=https://www.wired.com/2015/05/sneakers/ |access-date=2023-04-10 |issn=1059-1028 |archive-date=March 5, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210305230911/https://www.wired.com/2015/05/sneakers/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Known as PU degradation, polyurethane soles are susceptible to [[hydrolysis]] and [[Oxidation state|oxidation]], and shoes with this material have been found to have a poor aging performance.<ref>{{Cite journal |last1=Gopalakrishnan |first1=S. |last2=Fernando |first2=T. Linda |date=2011-03-31 |title=Studies on ageing performance of some novel polyurethanes |url=https://www.jocpr.com/abstract/studies-on-ageing-performance-of-some-novel-polyurethanes-947.html |journal=Journal of Chemical and Pharmaceutical Research|volume=3 |issue=2 }}</ref> Nike does not provide information as to the type of PU they use in their Air Jordan lines, and have avoided answering questions in the past, as was the case in ''[[Wired (magazine)|Wired]]''{{'}}s widely cited article on the PU controversy within the sneaker community, "We asked Nike about PU degradation and what might be done about it, but the company declined to comment."<ref name="Wired_decay" />

===Manufacturing===
[[Nike, Inc.|Nike]] owns none of the factories where Air Jordans are produced and contracts the work to various factory owners. Company officials say that they only design and market the shoes. However, Nike dictates production terms and standards to the contractor, often without questioning labor or safety practices.{{Citation needed|date=June 2024}} In April 1997, 10,000 Indonesian workers went on strike over wage violations at an Air Jordan factory. The same month in Vietnam 1,300 workers went on strike demanding a 1-cent-per-hour raise, and a year later in 1998, 3,000 workers in China went on strike to protest hazardous working conditions and low wages.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.globalexchange.org/sweatfree/nike/faq |title=Nike FAQs - Global Exchange |access-date=November 8, 2016|url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161026223516/http://www.globalexchange.org/sweatfree/nike/faq |archive-date=October 26, 2016}}</ref>

== Social and cultural impact ==
Air Jordans became a status symbol in sneaker culture with global sales. The impact of Air Jordans expanded from basketball and sports into [[fashion]], [[pop culture]], and [[Hip hop music|hip-hop]]. Air Jordans becoming a  staple in casual wear and [[streetwear]] culture. The popularity of the sneaker has risen from its connection to the hip-hop scene since 1985.<ref name="Streetwear_Essential">{{Cite web |last=Dooley |first=David |date=2023-03-15 |title=Why The Nike Air Jordan 1 is a Streetwear Essential |url=https://www.fastfashionnews.co.uk/why-the-nike-air-jordan-1-is-a-streetwear-essential/ |access-date=2023-05-12 |website=Fast Fashion News |archive-date=May 6, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230506091928/https://www.fastfashionnews.co.uk/why-the-nike-air-jordan-1-is-a-streetwear-essential/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Rappers in the 1990s including [[2Pac]], [[The Notorious B.I.G.]], [[Ice Cube]], and [[Jay-Z]] mentioned Michael Jordan's sneakers and success in their music.<ref name="TempleNow" /> They were also seen on many hip-hop album covers including [[Eazy-E]] wearing Air Jordan III on the ''[[Eazy-Duz-It]]'' album cover and music videos including "[[Otis (song)|Otis]]" where Jay-Z and [[Kanye West]] are seen wearing the Air Jordan I and Air Jordans VI.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Williams |first=Aaron P. |date=2018-05-16 |title=How Jordan IIIs Became The Holy Grails Of Both Basketball And Hip-Hop |url=https://medium.com/@AaronSmarter/how-jordan-iiis-are-the-key-to-the-rap-hoops-sneaker-connection-56f47097cfcc |access-date=2023-05-12 |website=Medium |language=en |archive-date=May 5, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230505093825/https://medium.com/@AaronSmarter/how-jordan-iiis-are-the-key-to-the-rap-hoops-sneaker-connection-56f47097cfcc |url-status=live }}</ref> The shoes were also worn by many artists and celebrities on a casual and everyday basis. Collaborations between hip-hop artists and the Jordan Brand also drove its popularity, including [[Travis Scott]]'s "Cactus Jack" collaborations with the Jordan Brand and Nike.<ref name="TempleNow" /> With the Jordan Brand having a huge impact on hip-hop culture, it became a status symbol.<ref name="Streetwear_Essential" />

=== Sneaker collecting ===
{{Main|Sneaker collecting}}
The shoes had a large impact on the rise of "sneakerhead" culture. In the 1980s, collecting sneakers became more common, as well as trading and reselling them. As new models came out, more Air Jordans became in demand and a significant shoe to have in collections.<ref name="TempleNow" /> Reselling Air Jordans  became highly profitable and hundreds or thousands of dollars are spent on rare sneakers, including the original 1985 Air Jordan I on [[StockX]] selling up to $20,000.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Jelen |first=Greta |date=February 2023 |title=How Michael Jordan's Sneakers Shaped Basketball and Fashion History |url=https://www.lofficielusa.com/men/michael-jordan-air-jordan-sneaker-history-nike |website=lofficielusa |access-date=May 12, 2023 |archive-date=May 8, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230508201312/https://www.lofficielusa.com/men/michael-jordan-air-jordan-sneaker-history-nike |url-status=live }}</ref> Upon the height and rise of the sneaker community, the resale market is estimated to be worth $2 billion and is expected to gain $4 billion more by 2025.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2020-07-20 |title=Sneakers as an Alternative Asset Class, Part II |url=https://www.cowen.com/insights/sneakers-as-an-alternative-asset-class-part-ii/ |access-date=2023-08-15 |website=TD Cowen |language=en-US |archive-date=August 15, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230815164726/https://www.cowen.com/insights/sneakers-as-an-alternative-asset-class-part-ii/ |url-status=live }}</ref> The growth is due to collaborations, limited editions drops, and other factors. With the sneakers evolving over time and introducing new models, the sneakers have been rising in value for collectors and becoming a staple in their collections.

=== ''Looney Tunes'' and ''Space Jam'' ===
On January 26, 1992, Jordan Brand debuted a [[Super Bowl commercial|commercial]] during [[Super Bowl XXVI]] which showed [[Bugs Bunny]] enlisting the help of Michael Jordan to outsmart a bullying rival team using cartoon gags. A second ad premiered in 1993 featuring Bugs and Jordan facing off against [[Marvin the Martian]]. The ads inspired Jordan's agent, [[David Falk]], to pitch a film starring Jordan and the ''[[Looney Tunes]]'' characters. The pitch resulted in ''[[Space Jam]]'' (1996), a commercial success which grossed over $230 million at the box office and generated over $1 billion in merchandise sales.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Francisco |first1=Eric |title=The Nike Ad That Inspired 'Space Jam' Was Nostalgic Paydirt |url=https://www.inverse.com/article/23641-space-jam-nike-jordan-nostalgia-bugs-bunny |website=Inverse |date=November 14, 2016 |access-date=4 September 2020 |archive-date=November 16, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116115725/https://www.inverse.com/article/23641-space-jam-nike-jordan-nostalgia-bugs-bunny |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last1=Jones |first1=Nate |title=Why Did Michael Jordan Star in 'Space Jam'? |url=https://www.vulture.com/2020/05/why-michael-jordan-did-space-jam.html |website=Vulture |date=May 11, 2020|publisher=Vox Media|access-date=4 September 2020 |archive-date=November 16, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116115800/https://www.vulture.com/2020/05/why-michael-jordan-did-space-jam.html |url-status=live }}</ref> The success of the advertising campaign and the film contributed to the popularity of ''Looney Tunes'' and other cartoon characters as motifs in street fashion through the 1990s and 2000s.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Josephs |first1=Brian |title=Lord Forgive Me: The Life of Urban Looney Tunes |url=https://www.gq.com/story/life-of-urban-looney-tunes |website=GQ |date=July 18, 2018  |access-date=4 September 2020 |archive-date=November 16, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116115741/https://www.gq.com/story/life-of-urban-looney-tunes |url-status=live }}</ref> A sequel to the film, ''[[Space Jam: A New Legacy]]'', was released in 2021, with [[LeBron James]] in the lead role.

===''Air''===
{{main|Air (2023 American film)}}
''Air'' is an American biographical sports drama film directed by [[Ben Affleck]]. The film is based on true events about the origin of Air Jordan, when [[Sonny Vaccaro]], a Nike employee, seeks to strike a business deal with rookie player Jordan. It stars [[Matt Damon]] as Vaccaro.

=== ''Like Mike'' ===
{{main|Like Mike}}
''Like Mike'' is an American sports comedy film released in 2002. It stars a teenage orphan who gains basketball abilities when he wears sneakers inscribed by Michael Jordan. Upon gaining these powers he is offered a chance to play in the NBA.

==Sponsorships==
In 1997, Air Jordan selected the first three collegiate sponsorships for the Jordan Brand: [[Cincinnati Bearcats men's basketball|Cincinnati Bearcats]], [[St. John's Red Storm men's basketball|St. John's Red Storm]], and [[North Carolina A&T Aggies men's basketball|North Carolina A&T Aggies]].<ref>{{cite news |last=Samuels |first=Doug |title=Jordan reportedly lines up FCS school for apparel deal |url=https://footballscoop.com/news/jordan-reportedly-lines-up-fcs-school-for-apparel-deal#:~:text=When%20Nike%20launched%20the%20Jordan,%2C%20Larry%2C%20attended%20NC%20A%26T. |website=footballscoop |access-date=29 May 2023 |archive-date=May 29, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230529073123/https://footballscoop.com/news/jordan-reportedly-lines-up-fcs-school-for-apparel-deal#:~:text=When%20Nike%20launched%20the%20Jordan,%2C%20Larry%2C%20attended%20NC%20A%26T. |url-status=live }}</ref> [[North Carolina A&T]], a [[Historically Black College and University]] (HBCU), ended its sponsorship in 2003 when it signed with [[Russell Athletic (brand)|Russell Athletic]]. Since then, Jordan Brand partnered with [[Howard University]] Athletics, another HBCU, in 2022 for all their programs except the men's and women's golf teams, which are already sponsored by [[Stephen Curry]]'s Curry Brand.<ref>{{cite web |last=Dwyer |first=Ross |title=Jordan Brand Announce 20 Year Partnership with Howard University |url=https://www.sneakerfreaker.com/news/howard-university-jordan-brand-sponsorship |website=Sneaker Freaker |access-date=3 May 2023 |archive-date=May 3, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230503070249/https://www.sneakerfreaker.com/news/howard-university-jordan-brand-sponsorship |url-status=live }}</ref>

Starting in 2016, Air Jordan became the sole equipment provider for the [[Michigan Wolverines football]] team.<ref name=wolv>[https://www.mlive.com/wolverines/index.ssf/2015/08/michael_jordan_welcomes_michig.html Michael Jordan welcomes Michigan as first ever Jordan Brand football client] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181120055129/https://www.mlive.com/wolverines/index.ssf/2015/08/michael_jordan_welcomes_michig.html |date=November 20, 2018 }} on mlive.com, August 13, 2015</ref> This marked the brand's first venture into a sport besides basketball. As of 2023, Air Jordan is also the equipment provider for the [[North Carolina Tar Heels football|North Carolina Tar Heels]], [[Oklahoma Sooners football|Oklahoma Sooners]], [[Florida Gators football|Florida Gators]], and [[UCLA Bruins football]] programs.<ref>[https://www.cbssports.com/college-football/news/florida-makes-big-move-joining-jordan-brands-growing-college-football-footprint/ Florida makes big move joining Jordan brand's growing college football footprint] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181120023603/https://www.cbssports.com/college-football/news/florida-makes-big-move-joining-jordan-brands-growing-college-football-footprint/ |date=November 20, 2018 }} on CBSSports.com, December 6, 2017</ref>

In 2018, the Jordan Brand sponsored an [[association football]] (soccer) club for the first time in its history, when French club [[Paris Saint-Germain F.C.]] displayed the Jumpman logo on their third kits, worn in the [[2018–19 UEFA Champions League]].<ref>[https://www.mundodeportivo.com/futbol/internacional/20180914/451789732829/michael-jordan-se-cuela-en-la-camiseta-del-psg.html Michael Jordan se cuela en la camiseta del PSG] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200610232213/https://www.mundodeportivo.com/futbol/internacional/20180914/451789732829/michael-jordan-se-cuela-en-la-camiseta-del-psg.html |date=June 10, 2020 }}, Mundo Deportivo, September 14, 2018</ref>

In 2023, the Jordan Brand signed AL 2022 MVP [[Aaron Judge]] to an endorsement deal.

The Jordan Brand also sponsors [[23XI Racing]], which is co-owned by [[Michael Jordan]] in the No. 45 [[Toyota Camry]] driven by [[Tyler Reddick]] in the [[NASCAR Cup Series]].

==Philanthropy==
The Jordan Brand partners with the [[UNCF]] and others to fund the higher education of underprivileged youth.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Luck |first=Quierra |date=July 28, 2020 |title=Michael Jordan Trash Talking During Pickup Game, "You Might Want to Google Me" |url=https://www.si.com/college/unc/basketball/michael-jordan-jump-shot |access-date=2023-03-22 |website=[[FanNation]] |archive-date=March 23, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230323065519/https://www.si.com/college/unc/basketball/michael-jordan-jump-shot |url-status=live }}</ref>

The Jordan Brand also focuses on philanthropy with many large donations throughout the years to communities, athletes, and schools.<ref>{{Cite web |title=The Jordan Brand Community |url=https://www.nike.com/jordan/purpose |access-date=2023-05-10 |website=Nike.com |archive-date=May 11, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230511022302/https://www.nike.com/jordan/purpose |url-status=live }}</ref>{{Primary source inline|date=May 2023}} The brand pledges to help with pressing issues in black communities through grants to the communities focusing on economic justice, education, social justice, and Narrative changes to the youth.<ref>{{Cite web |title=The Jordan Brand Family |url=https://www.nike.com/jordan/community/family |access-date=2023-05-10 |website=Nike.com |archive-date=May 10, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230510203015/https://www.nike.com/jordan/community/family |url-status=dead }}</ref>{{Primary source inline|date=May 2023}}
