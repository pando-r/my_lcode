'''Jerome Richard Krause''' (April 6, 1939&nbsp;– March 21, 2017) was an American [[sports scout]] and executive who was the [[general manager (basketball)|general manager]] of the [[Chicago Bulls]] in the [[National Basketball Association]] (NBA) from 1985 to 2003.<ref>{{Citation|last=Evensen|first=Bruce J.|chapter=Krause, Jerry (6 Apr. 1939–21 Mar. 2017), basketball general manager and scout for basketball and baseball teams|date=2021-11-25|chapter-url=https://doi.org/10.1093/anb/9780198606697.013.370015|title=American National Biography Online|publisher=Oxford University Press|doi=10.1093/anb/9780198606697.013.370015| isbn=978-0-19-860669-7 |access-date=2022-01-25|chapter-url-access=subscription}}</ref>

His tenure with the Bulls included their six NBA championships between 1991 and 1998, led by superstar players [[Michael Jordan]] and [[Scottie Pippen]]. Krause received the [[NBA Executive of the Year Award]] in 1988 and 1996. He was posthumously inducted into the [[Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame|Basketball Hall of Fame]] in 2017. His career in sports included positions as a [[Scout (sport)|scout]] or general manager for the [[Baltimore Bullets (1963–73)|Baltimore Bullets]], [[Chicago White Sox]], and the Chicago Bulls.

==Early life==
Krause was born in 1939 to a [[Jewish]] family and grew up in [[Chicago]]. His parents operated a deli and then a shoe store.<ref name=johnson>{{cite news |last1=Johnson |first1=K.C. |title=Scout's honor |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/chicago-tribune-scouts-honor/188316444/ |access-date=January 6, 2026 |work=Chicago Tribune |date=March 6, 2016 |page=3 |via=[[Newspapers.com]]}}</ref> Krause played high school [[baseball]] as a [[catcher]] at [[William Howard Taft High School (Chicago, Illinois)|Taft High School]] in Chicago and attended [[Bradley University]].

Krause began evaluating basketball and baseball talent as a high school student.<ref name=sakamoto760327>{{cite news |last1=Sakamoto |first1=Bob |title=Krause signals new Bull era |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/chicago-tribune-krause-signals-new-bull/188300614/ |access-date=January 6, 2026 |work=Chicago Tribune |date=March 27, 1985 |page=43 |via=[[Newspapers.com]]}}</ref> He worked as a [[copy boy]] in the sports department of the ''[[Chicago American]]'' during his teenage years.<ref name=sakamoto760327/> Krause was obsessed with sports and was nicknamed "J. G. Taylor Krause" after [[J. G. Taylor Spink]], the editor of ''[[The Sporting News]]''.<ref name=sakamoto760327/> He had wanted to become a sportswriter when he was young but realised he was "just a hack" at writing and instead possessed a talent for scouting.<ref name=sakamoto850331/> Krause turned down a staff position at the ''Chicago American'' so he could become a scout.<ref name=johnson/>

==Early career==
After college, Krause worked as a go-fer for [[Philip K. Wrigley]], the owner of the [[Chicago Cubs]].<ref name=sakamoto850331/> He then worked as a scout and in public relations for the [[Baltimore Bullets (1963–73)|Baltimore Bullets]].<ref name=sakamoto850331/> Early on, Krause gained a reputation of being able to spot talent. He advocated for the Bullets to draft [[Jerry Sloan]] and [[Earl Monroe]].<ref name=markus>{{cite news |last1=Markus |first1=Robert |title=After a long chase, Krause finds dream in his backyard |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/chicago-tribune-krause-feature-june-3/52052361/ |access-date=January 6, 2026 |work=Chicago Tribune |date=June 3, 1976 |page=49 |via=[[Newspapers.com]]}}</ref>

While with the Bullets, Krause urged the team to pick [[North Dakota Fighting Sioux|North Dakota]] forward [[Phil Jackson]] in the [[1967 NBA draft]]. The Bullets did not draft him, but Krause continued to keep in touch during Jackson's playing career and into his first years as a coach. Their relationship flourished during the 1970s and 1980s. While Jackson was coaching the [[Albany Patroons]] in the [[Continental Basketball Association]], Krause once called him to ask for an analysis of the league's players, which Jackson provided in detail.<ref>{{cite book |last=Halberstam |first=David |author-link=David Halberstam |title=Playing for Keeps: Michael Jordan and the World He Made |year=1999 |publisher=Random House |location=New York City |isbn=0-679-41562-9 |page=[https://archive.org/details/playingforkeepsm00halb/page/198 198] |url-access=registration |url=https://archive.org/details/playingforkeepsm00halb/page/198 }}</ref>

Krause was a scout for the [[Chicago Bulls]] from their inaugural season in 1966 until 1971 when he had conflicts with head coach [[Dick Motta]].<ref name=logan/> He also scouted for the [[Phoenix Suns]], [[Philadelphia 76ers]] and [[Los Angeles Lakers]].<ref name=sakamoto850331>{{cite news |last1=Sakamoto |first1=Bob |title=Krause digs inside Bulls |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/chicago-tribune-krause-digs-inside-bulls/188302194/ |access-date=January 6, 2026 |work=Chicago Tribune |date=March 31, 1985 |page=43 |via=[[Newspapers.com]]}}</ref>

On June 1, 1976, Krause was hired as the director of player personnel for the Bulls.<ref name=logan>{{cite news |last1=Logan |first1=Bob |title=Krause tabbed player personnel director |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/chicago-tribune-bulls-hire-krause-june/52052318/ |access-date=January 6, 2026 |work=Chicago Tribune |date=June 2, 1976 |via=[[Newspapers.com]]}}</ref> One week into his role, he allegedly offered [[Ray Meyer]] the position of head coach but then denied it.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Logan |first1=Bob |title=Meyer snubs Bulls' bid, says Krause bungled |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/chicago-tribune-krause-meyer-bungled-s/52052722/ |access-date=January 6, 2026 |work=Chicago Tribune |date=June 10, 1976 |page=63 |via=[[Newspapers.com]]}}</ref> The job instead went to [[Ed Badger]] who was not Krause's choice to fill the vacancy.<ref name=stone>{{cite news |last1=Stone |first1=Ed |title=Krause quits; rift with Badger? |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/chicago-tribune-krause-resigns-or-maybe/52053017/ |access-date=January 6, 2026 |work=Chicago Tribune |date=September 1, 1976 |page=55 |via=[[Newspapers.com]]}}</ref> Krause wanted to select [[Robert Parish]] in the [[1976 NBA draft]] but Bulls owner [[Arthur Wirtz]] refused to negotiate with Parish's agent and instead drafted [[Scott May]].<ref name=sakamoto850331/> On August 31, Krause resigned after three months in the role over apparent policy disagreements with Badger.<ref name=stone/>

Krause also worked as a baseball scout. He worked in the 1970s for the [[Cleveland Indians]], [[Oakland Athletics]], [[Seattle Mariners]], and [[Chicago White Sox]]. He once worked as the general manager of a [[farm team]] for the Indians.<ref name=sakamoto760327/> While working for the Mariners, Krause continued to scout part-time for the Lakers.<ref name="kirk gibson">{{cite news |last1=Carpenter |first1=Les |title=Long before his Michael Jordan days, Jerry Krause believed he had found the next Mickey Mantle |url=https://sports.yahoo.com/news/long-before-his-michael-jordan-days-jerry-krause-believed-he-had-found-the-next-mickey-mantle-165705787.html |access-date=23 April 2020 |work=[[Yahoo Sports]] |date=March 22, 2017}}</ref> As a White Sox scout, he played a role in the signing of [[Ozzie Guillén]] and [[Kenny Williams (baseball)|Kenny Williams]], who would lead the White Sox to a [[World Series]] championship in {{wsy|2005}} as manager and general manager, respectively.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Hayes |first1=Dan |title=Jerry Krause's impact on the 2005 White Sox |url=https://www.nbcsports.com/chicago/chicago-white-sox/how-jerry-krause-impacted-2005-white-sox-world-series-title |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200424212746/https://www.nbcsports.com/chicago/chicago-white-sox/how-jerry-krause-impacted-2005-white-sox-world-series-title |url-status=dead |archive-date=April 24, 2020 |access-date=23 April 2020 |work=NBC Sports Chicago |date=21 March 2017}}</ref> He scouted for the White Sox until 1984.

==Chicago Bulls GM==
On March 26, 1985, Krause was hired by [[Jerry Reinsdorf]] as the vice president of basketball operations for the Bulls.<ref name=sakamoto760327/>

===Coaching staff===
Krause's first hire with the Bulls was longtime personal friend [[Tex Winter]]. Krause hired Winter as an assistant bench coach. Krause urged Winter to teach all of the Bulls players, especially [[Michael Jordan]], the [[Triangle Offense]]. Winter had learned the triangle offense as a college player at [[University of Southern California|USC]] under then head coach [[Sam Barry]], and later used it to successfully guide [[Kansas State Wildcats men's basketball|Kansas State]] to a number one ranking. At the end of the [[1988-1989 NBA season]], Krause fired then-head coach [[Doug Collins (basketball)|Doug Collins]] and replaced him with assistant coach [[Phil Jackson]]. Even though Collins had guided the Bulls to the Eastern Conference Finals, this was largely because Collins would not let Winter do as Krause had instructed.  Jackson managed to convince Jordan to give up the individual scoring plays that had become his trademark and try Winter’s system, where all five players on the floor were threats to score.<ref name="Aldridge, David">{{cite web|url=https://www.nba.com/article/2017/03/27/morning-tip-remembering-jerry-krause-legacy-chicago-bulls|author=Aldridge, David|work=nba.com|title=Chicago Bulls' golden era a reflection of Jerry Krause's words, deeds|date=March 27, 2017|access-date=April 19, 2020}}</ref>

Krause made strength coach Al Vermeil one of his first hires, and the Bulls became one of the first NBA teams to embrace weight training for players. Krause gave scouting and management opportunities to African-Americans when this was not the norm. [[Karen Stack Umlauf]], originally hired as a secretary for Krause, was soon writing scouting reports on potential draftees and working out players were signed for the Bulls, and eventually promoted to director of basketball operations, making her one of the few women to be deeply involved in basketball-centric duties.<ref name="Aldridge, David"/>

===Building the 1991–93 roster===
At the time Krause took over as general manager, he already had on the team Michael Jordan and [[John Paxson]] — two of the five players who would become key parts of the team that would win three consecutive championships from 1991 to 1993. Krause made a number of moves that began building the foundation for future success. He turned over the Bulls roster and built up a collection of draft picks, although the moves in 1985 and 1986 were not particularly noteworthy. Krause planned to select tall forward [[Brad Sellers]] in the [[1986 NBA draft]]. Sellers, Krause reasoned, handled the ball very well for a big man, and also had a solid outside jumper. Jordan, on the other hand, pushed management to take [[Johnny Dawkins]], a two-way guard from [[Duke Blue Devils men's basketball|Duke]].  It looked as though Krause would take Dawkins even as late as the morning of the draft; the coaches and players had made it clear that they wanted him and not Sellers, and Krause did not particularly have a problem with Dawkins' game. The Bulls' head coach at the time, Doug Collins, even told Duke coach [[Mike Krzyzewski]] the night before the draft that the team would pick Dawkins. However, Krause picked Sellers anyway, and spent the summer trying to sell him to Jordan. Jordan went after Sellers mercilessly in practice, and Sellers was traded after three seasons.<ref name="Sellers">{{Cite web|date=May 22, 2020|title=Michael Jordan called Olive Garden to let Brad Sellers know Bulls traded him|url=https://www.nbcsports.com/chicago/bulls/michael-jordan-called-olive-garden-let-brad-sellers-know-bulls-traded-him|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200531154127/https://www.nbcsports.com/chicago/bulls/michael-jordan-called-olive-garden-let-brad-sellers-know-bulls-traded-him|url-status=dead|archive-date=May 31, 2020|access-date=July 6, 2021|website=[[NBC Sports]]}}</ref>

It was in the [[1987 NBA draft]] that Krause drafted power forward [[Horace Grant]] and traded draft pick [[Olden Polynice]], a center, for Seattle's 1st Round draft pick — [[Scottie Pippen]], a small forward. The two were, along with Jordan, cornerstones of the Bulls' 1991–1993 championship teams. The 1988 draft failed to yield much, as first-round pick [[Will Perdue]] failed to develop as hoped.<ref name="Halberstam, p. 200">Halberstam, ''Playing for Keeps'', p. 200.</ref>

In 1988, Krause traded power forward [[Charles Oakley]] to the [[New York Knicks]] for center [[Bill Cartwright]]. Jordan initially despised the trade, as Oakley was Jordan's best friend on the team,<ref name="Halberstam, p. 246">Halberstam, ''Playing for Keeps'', p. 246.</ref> and as Jordan and Oakley learned of the trade while they were on their way to [[Las Vegas Valley|Las Vegas]] to see a [[Mike Tyson]] fight.<ref name="Halberstam, p. 200"/> Cartwright turned out to be everything the Bulls needed, however, providing a presence in the middle for all three Bulls championships from 1991 to 1993. Perhaps most importantly, Cartwright proved to be the league's best center at defending [[Patrick Ewing]], the key player on the [[New York Knicks]] who were the Bulls' most important early-1990s conference rival.<ref name="Halberstam, p. 246"/> Jordan later acknowledged that Krause had made the correct move in trading Oakley for Cartwright.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://sportsillustrated.cnn.com/multimedia/photo_gallery/0605/gallery.toxic/content.13.html |title=SI.com - Photo Gallery - Most Toxic Feuds of the Past 15 Years |access-date=May 21, 2006 |archive-date=February 29, 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080229034933/http://sportsillustrated.cnn.com/multimedia/photo_gallery/0605/gallery.toxic/content.13.html |url-status=dead }}</ref> Krause, one of the few members of the Bulls organization to say "no" to Jordan, also clashed with Jordan repeatedly (notably in 1986 over calling Jordan "property" in limiting playing minutes after return from an injury), such that Jordan allegedly referred to Krause as "Crumbs" in mocking Krause’s physical appearance and his seeming need to be one of the guys on the team bus. While Jordan was the public face of the NBA as its superstar, Krause was fiercely secretive and non-communicative with the local media.<ref name="Aldridge, David"/>

By 1988, the Bulls had assembled their starting five players (guards Jordan and Paxson, forwards Pippen and Grant, and center Cartwright) for the team that would reach five consecutive Eastern Conference finals from 1989 to 1993 and win three consecutive NBA championships from 1991 to 1993. Krause was named NBA Executive of the Year in 1988. In 1989 and 1990, Krause added depth to the roster. The Bulls picked [[Stacey King]] and [[B. J. Armstrong]] in the [[1989 NBA draft]] and later signed undrafted big man [[Scott Williams (basketball)|Scott Williams]]. Krause nonetheless was disliked by the entire Bulls locker room, while Jackson was enormously popular, such as Krause believed that Jackson took too much credit for the Bulls dynasty.<ref name="Aldridge, David"/>

===Building the 1996–98 roster===
The first retirement of Jordan, following the 1993 NBA season, brought massive change to the Chicago Bulls roster. The 1993–94 Bulls were composed entirely of Krause's acquisitions, including several minimum salary players.<ref name="ja-adande-wp-1996"/> Just before Jordan announced his retirement, Krause persuaded 1990 draft pick [[Toni Kukoč]] to buy himself out of his European contract and join the Bulls.<ref name="Halberstam, p. 289">Halberstam, ''Playing for Keeps'', p. 289.</ref> Despite losing Jordan, the Bulls won 55 games during the season, just two less than the season before. In the playoffs, the Bulls lost to the heavily favored New York Knicks in seven games in the second round.<ref name="ja-adande-wp-1996">{{cite news |author1=[[J.A. Adande]] |title=Krause is the brains behind these Bulls |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/sports/1996/06/12/krause-is-the-brains-behind-these-bulls/42258f6c-6850-473f-bf7e-5c353353ee5a/ |access-date=13 January 2024 |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |date=12 June 1996}}</ref>

During the 1993–94 NBA season, Stacey King was traded to the [[Minnesota Timberwolves]] in exchange for 7'2" center [[Luc Longley]].<ref name="longley">{{cite web|url=http://www.nba.com/bulls/history/alltime_transactions.html|title=Chicago Bulls All-Time Transactions|work=www.nba.com|access-date=August 28, 2009}}</ref> Longley's emergence for the Bulls during the 1994–95 season made Will Perdue expendable.<ref>{{cite news |author1=Sam Quinn |title=Dennis Rodman trade: How Michael Jordan's Bulls dealt their backup center for the NBA's best rebounder |url=https://www.cbssports.com/nba/news/dennis-rodman-trade-how-michael-jordans-bulls-dealt-their-backup-center-for-the-nbas-best-rebounder/ |access-date=2 May 2020 |work=[[CBS Sports]] |date=27 April 2020}}</ref> Just before the start of the 1995–96 season, Perdue was traded to the [[San Antonio Spurs]] for [[Dennis Rodman]].<ref>{{cite news |author1=Rick Gano |title=Bulls acquire Rodman from Spurs |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/sports/1995/10/03/bulls-acquire-rodman-from-spurs/05ef2039-04da-4483-a7bd-84219c40417d/ |access-date=2 May 2020 |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |date=3 October 1994}}</ref>

After Jordan returned to the NBA, the Bulls won a then-NBA record 72 games in the 1995–1996 season,<ref>{{Cite web|last=Armour|first=Terry|date=April 22, 1996|title=Chicago Bulls finish season with all-time best 72-10 record|url=https://www.chicagotribune.com/sports/bulls/ct-bulls-72-wins-record-1996-story.html|access-date=July 6, 2021|website=[[Chicago Tribune]]}}</ref> and Krause was named [[NBA Executive of the Year Award|Executive of the Year]] for the second time.<ref>{{Cite web|date=March 21, 2017|title=Former Bulls GM Jerry Krause dies at age 77|url=https://www.nbcsports.com/chicago/chicago-bulls/former-bulls-gm-jerry-krause-dies-age-77|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190201232311/https://www.nbcsports.com/chicago/chicago-bulls/former-bulls-gm-jerry-krause-dies-age-77|url-status=dead|archive-date=February 1, 2019|access-date=July 6, 2021|website=[[NBC Sports]]}}</ref> The next year, they tied the then second best-ever NBA record with 69 wins and repeated as champions.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Johnson|first=K.C.|date=June 16, 1997|title=Bulls got there because they'd been there|url=https://www.chicagotribune.com/news/ct-xpm-1997-06-16-9706160169-story.html|access-date=July 6, 2021|website=[[Chicago Tribune]]}}</ref>

===1997–98 season===
{{See also|The Last Dance (miniseries)}}
Krause and head coach [[Phil Jackson]] had been friends for years, but their relationship was, in Jackson's opinion, shattered early in the 1990s after ''[[Chicago Tribune]]'' reporter [[Sam Smith (sportswriter)|Sam Smith]] (whom Krause despised) published a book on the 1991 title team, ''[[The Jordan Rules (book)|The Jordan Rules]]''. The book detailed the tension that already existed between Krause and the players, and ultimately drove a wedge between Krause and Jackson.<ref>Halberstam, ''Playing for Keeps'', p. 311-15.</ref> Regardless of the success Jackson had as head coach of the Bulls, the tension between Jackson and Krause grew in the succeeding years, and by the 1997–98 season, was especially high, illustrated by several incidents. During the summer of 1997, Krause's stepdaughter married. All of the Bulls assistant coaches and their wives were invited to the wedding, as was [[Tim Floyd]], then the head coach at [[Iowa State Cyclones men's basketball|Iowa State]], whom Krause was openly courting as Jackson's successor (and who would eventually succeed Jackson). Jackson and his then wife, June, were not even told of the wedding, much less invited, only finding out about the event when the wife of Cartwright, who by that time had become a Bulls assistant, asked June what she would be wearing to the reception.<ref>Halberstam, ''Playing for Keeps'', p. 249.</ref>

After contentious negotiations between Jackson and the Bulls in that same period, Jackson was signed for the 1997–98 season only. Krause announced the signing in what Chicago media widely considered to be a mean-spirited manner, emphasizing that Jackson would not be rehired even if the Bulls won the 1997–98 title. That triggered an argument between Jackson and Krause in which Jackson essentially told Krause that he seemed to be rooting for the other side and not the Bulls. At that point, Krause told Jackson, "I don't care if it's 82-and-0 this year, you're fucking gone."<ref>Halberstam, ''Playing for Keeps'', p. 41. The "82" refers to the number of regular-season games each NBA team plays.</ref> Krause was widely quoted as saying, "Players and coaches don't win championships; organizations win championships." The statement particularly offended Michael Jordan. However, Krause said that his original phrasing was "Players and coaches ''alone'' don't win championships; organizations win championships."<ref name="Aldridge, David"/>

Although most of the narrative suggested that Jackson was forced out as head coach, Jackson had actually declined a long-term offer from owner [[Jerry Reinsdorf]] in 1998. Jackson himself held to a theory that "a coach loses the attention of his players after seven years", being taught by his father Charles, a traveling Pentecostal minister that, "You can only stay in one place five years and then your message starts falling on deaf ears." During Jackson's sixth season as head coach, he told Krause and Reinsdorf that he would only return after his contract expired in 1996 if the team cut loose every player from the first three-peat, including Pippen. Though Jordan did return to basketball after major-league baseball was halted by a strike, only he (having confided to Reinsdorf that he would only play for Jackson) and Pippen remained for the second string of championships.<ref name="blogabull.com"/> Jordan retired after the 1997-98 season because he was "burned out" shouldering an increasing burden for the team due to Pippen's absence and "babying Rodman".<ref>{{Cite web|date=2004-12-02|title=Smith: The decline and fall of the Bulls|url=https://www.espn.com/nba/columns/story?columnist=smith_sam&id=1936782|access-date=2020-09-25|website=ESPN.com|language=en}}</ref><ref name="blogabull.com"/> Reinsdorf, while offering Jackson the opportunity to return the following year as head coach, made it clear that the team would have to go into a rebuild because other than Jordan the rest of the players were at the end of the productive years and they would be too expensive to re-sign. Jackson refused by saying that he didn't want to coach a bad team and left after his contact expired,<ref>Jerry Reinsdorf offering Phil Jackson coaching contract for 1998-1999 season, [https://www.chicagotribune.com/news/ct-xpm-1998-06-23-9806230107-story.html], Accessed October 3, 2022.</ref> initially vowing never to coach again, but after he took a year off he decided to give it another chance with the [[Los Angeles Lakers]]. Longtime friend of Krause and Bulls assistant coach [[Tex Winter]], who was the architect of the triangle offense,<ref>{{cite web |url=http://blogs.hoopshype.com/blogs/lazenby/category/jerry-krause/ |title=Jerry Krause dies |publisher=Blogs.hoopshype.com |access-date=2017-03-22 |archive-date=January 2, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120102232649/http://blogs.hoopshype.com/blogs/lazenby/category/jerry-krause/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> stayed with the Bulls for another full season, leaving the organization in the summer of 1999, when Phil Jackson was hired to coach the Lakers. Krause said that he had not spoken to Jackson since. When Jordan was inducted into the Hall of Fame in 2009, Krause was not in attendance. Krause said he would not attend the ceremony over the Hall of Fame's refusal to induct Winter.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.espn.com/nba/news/story?id=4464526|title=Krause passes on MJ's induction on principle|date=September 12, 2009|access-date=March 22, 2017}}</ref> Krause and Jackson briefly put their differences aside when Winter was finally inducted two years later. The two shook hands before the ceremony and conducted themselves in a cordial manner during Winter's segment.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nbcsports.com/nba/news/for-tex-winter-can-kraus-and-jackson-put-aside-feud|title=For Tex Winter, Krause and Jackson put aside feud|first=Kurt|last=Helin|date=August 13, 2011|access-date=March 22, 2017}}</ref>

===Struggles with rebuilding===
After the 1998 title, deciding that the Bulls were aging and facing an uncertain future, Krause chose to unload the veterans and rebuild.<ref name="blogabull.com">{{Cite web|last=Silverstein|first=Jack M.|date=2017-03-24|title=The true story of Jerry Krause and the breakup of the Bulls|url=https://www.blogabull.com/2017/3/24/15044772/the-true-story-of-jerry-krause-breakup-of-the-bulls-michael-jordan-phil-jackson-scottie-pippen|access-date=2020-09-25|website=Blog a Bull|language=en|archive-date=April 4, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200404055053/https://www.blogabull.com/2017/3/24/15044772/the-true-story-of-jerry-krause-breakup-of-the-bulls-michael-jordan-phil-jackson-scottie-pippen|url-status=dead}}</ref>  On [[1997 NBA draft|1997 draft]] night he explored the possibility of trading Pippen for the rights to a young 18-year-old [[Tracy McGrady]]. But McGrady said "Jerry Krause had an obsession with me." McGrady flew out to Chicago to work out with the Bulls with the possibility of joining the team.  But the trade didn't happen, Bulls owner [[Jerry Reinsdorf]] vetoed the trade and many still believe [[Michael Jordan]] had a say in it.<ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gzMizNykDWI |title=Tracy McGrady: I was VERY close to being traded for Scottie Pippen on 1997 draft night {{!}} The Jump |date=2020-04-20 |last=ESPN |access-date=2026-05-27 |via=YouTube}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=By |last2=Feldman |first2=Dan |last3=By |last4=Feldman |first4=Dan |date=2020-04-21 |title=Tracy McGrady: Bulls nearly traded Scottie Pippen for me, but Michael Jordan interceded |url=https://www.nbcsports.com/nba/news/tracy-mcgrady-bulls-nearly-traded-scottie-pippen-for-me-but-michael-jordan-interceded |access-date=2026-05-27 |website=NBC Sports |language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=read |first=HoopsHype·1 min |date=2026-05-08 |title=Tracy McGrady: Jerry Krause tried to pair me with Michael Jordan |url=https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/tracy-mcgrady-jerry-krause-tried-142451146.html |access-date=2026-05-27 |website=Yahoo Sports |language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |title=“Jerry Krause was just obsessed with me” — Tracy McGrady recalls when the Chicago Bulls were close to trading Scottie Pippen for him |url=https://www.basketballnetwork.net/old-school/tracy-mcgrady-recalls-when-the-chicago-bulls-were-close-to-trading-scottie-pippen |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221208094925/https://www.basketballnetwork.net/old-school/tracy-mcgrady-recalls-when-the-chicago-bulls-were-close-to-trading-scottie-pippen |archive-date=2022-12-08 |access-date=2026-05-27 |work=Basketball Network - Your daily dose of basketball |language=en}}</ref> Krause was correct since neither Rodman nor Longley would play at their previous levels after leaving the Bulls, something Krause already feared due to what medical staff told him about these two players in the 1997-98 season. Pippen was also due for a significant raise as well, but also would not continue his previous stardom after leaving the Bulls. Krause "believed by pulling the plug on the dynasty instead of allowing it to expire of natural causes, he could avoid the sort of malaise that has endured with the Knicks for two decades", however this meant that Krause would be better remembered as the one who dismantled the Bulls championship teams rather than its architect.<ref>{{cite web | title=Presented as 'Last Dance' villain, Jerry Krause brought more to Bulls' championships than (Almost) anyone &#124; Sporting News Canada | date=May 18, 2020 | url=https://www.sportingnews.com/ca/nba/news/presented-as-the-last-dance-villain-jerry-krause-brought-more-to-bulls-championships-than-almost-anyone/1tdnqb8ku1wao1hhhaawukcqpp }}</ref>

The draft brought prolific collegiate players such as [[Elton Brand]], [[Ron Artest]], [[Marcus Fizer]], [[Jamal Crawford]], and [[Jay Williams (basketball)|Jay Williams]] to the Bulls. After the Bulls finished 15–67 during the 2000–2001 season, Krause decided to trade away his best player (Brand) for high schooler [[Tyson Chandler]], who was hyped as "the next Kevin Garnett". He also drafted another high schooler, [[Eddy Curry]], who was hyped as a slightly smaller version of [[Shaquille O'Neal]], with the fourth overall pick in the draft. Krause believed that Chandler and Curry would develop into elite players and provide the foundation for another dynasty. A mid-season trade the following year brought scorer [[Jalen Rose]] to the Bulls in exchange for [[Brad Miller (basketball player)|Brad Miller]] and Artest, clearing playing time for Chandler and Curry. After drafting Jay Williams during the offseason, the Bulls had a roster with Rose, Crawford, Curry, Chandler, Williams, and Fizer that fulfilled Krause's dream of a talented young athletic team. From the end of Jordan's retirement until Krause's final year, the Bulls posted a '''96–282''' (a .254 winning percentage) record.<ref>{{Cite web |title= |url=https://www.basketball-reference.com/teams/CHI/}}</ref>

==Later years and death==
In 2003, Krause resigned from his post as general manager. He explained, "The rigors and stress of the job have caused me some minor physical problems in the past few years."<ref>{{Cite news| url =https://www.nytimes.com/2003/04/08/sports/basketball-bulls-builder-krause-resigns-after-18-years.html |title=Bulls Builder Krause Resigns After 18 Years|author=Liz Robbins|work=[[The New York Times]] |date = April 8, 2003}}</ref> The Bulls fell to 23–59 in the next season, and Krause's dream of a talented young athletic team imploded; all of his acquisitions were traded or out of the league within three years.{{citation needed|date=December 2025}}

Krause went back to his roots and worked briefly for the [[New York Yankees]] as a scout before joining the [[New York Mets]] in 2005.<ref>Fred Mitchell. ''Chicago Tribune''. May 4, 2005. Retrieved on September 10, 2009.</ref> In 2010, he rejoined the [[Chicago White Sox]] as a scout, a position he had held in the 1970s and 80s.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.espn.com/chicago/mlb/news/story?id=5056444 |title=The Chicago White Sox hire former Chicago Bulls GM Jerry Krause to oversee scouting system in Dominican Republic |publisher=[[ESPN]] |date=2010-04-05 |access-date=2017-03-22}}</ref> He was appointed by the [[Arizona Diamondbacks]] as a special assistant in its scouting department on April 1, 2011.<ref>Gilbert, Steve [http://arizona.diamondbacks.mlb.com/news/article.jsp?ymd=20110401&content_id=17260502&notebook_id=17274552&vkey=notebook_ari&c_id=ari "D-backs hire Krause as special assistant"]{{dead link|date=October 2024|bot=medic}}{{cbignore|bot=medic}}, MLB.com, Friday, April 1, 2011</ref> Krause retired from scouting in 2016.<ref name=johnson/>

On March 21, 2017, Krause died at the age of 77.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Presented as 'Last Dance' villain, Jerry Krause brought more to Bulls' championships than (almost) anyone|url=https://www.sportingnews.com/us/nba/news/presented-as-the-last-dance-villain-jerry-krause-brought-more-to-bulls-championships-than-almost-anyone/p9qx4kd84c6y1sv7ixlx224al|access-date=2022-01-25|website=www.sportingnews.com|date=May 18, 2020 |language=en}}</ref> He had been struggling with health issues such as [[osteomyelitis]].<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/sports/jerry-krause-general-manager-of-chicago-bulls-1990s-juggernauts-dies-at-77/2017/03/22/37afa584-0f1a-11e7-ab07-07d9f521f6b5_story.html|title=Jerry Krause, general manager of Chicago Bulls' 1990s juggernauts, dies at 77|newspaper=Washington Post|date=March 22, 2017|access-date=April 20, 2020}}</ref> He was inducted into the 2017 Basketball Hall of Fame class posthumously.
