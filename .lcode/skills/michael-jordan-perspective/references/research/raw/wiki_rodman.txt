'''Dennis Keith Rodman''' (born May 13, 1961) is an American former professional [[basketball]] player, [[Professional wrestling|professional wrestler]], and actor. Renowned for his defensive and [[rebound (basketball)|rebounding]] abilities, his biography on the official NBA website states that he is "arguably the best rebounding forward in NBA history".<ref>{{cite web | url=https://clutchpoints.com/25-greatest-defenders-nba-history-ranked | title=25 Greatest Defenders in NBA History, Ranked | website=ClutchPoints | date=August 7, 2023 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=20 greatest defensive power forwards ever: The HoopsHype list |url=https://www.hoopshype.com/story/sports/nba/rankings/2025/03/06/20-greatest-defensive-power-forwards-ever-the-hoopshype-list/79228621007/ |website=HoopsHype |date=2025-03-06 |access-date=2025-06-25}}</ref> Nicknamed "'''the Worm'''",<ref>{{cite web |last1=Levy |first1=Dan |title=The Greatest Nicknames in NBA History |url=https://bleacherreport.com/articles/1667613-the-greatest-nicknames-in-nba-history |website=[[Bleacher Report]] |access-date=October 13, 2020 |date=June 10, 2013 |archive-date=October 14, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201014091012/https://bleacherreport.com/articles/1667613-the-greatest-nicknames-in-nba-history |url-status=live }}</ref> he played for the [[Detroit Pistons]], [[San Antonio Spurs]], [[Chicago Bulls]], [[Los Angeles Lakers]], and [[Dallas Mavericks]] of the [[National Basketball Association]] (NBA). Rodman played at the [[small forward]] position in his early years before becoming a [[power forward]].

He earned [[NBA All-Defensive First Team]] honors seven times and won the [[NBA Defensive Player of the Year Award]] twice. He also led the NBA in rebounds per game for a record seven consecutive years and won five NBA championships. On April 1, 2011, the Pistons retired Rodman's No. 10 jersey,<ref name="autogenerated34">{{cite web |last1=Lidz |first1=Franz |title=Dennis Rodman - As the Worm Turns |url=https://www.si.com/vault/2015/07/09/106342554/dennis-rodman |website=Vault |access-date=January 7, 2020 |date=July 8, 2013 |archive-date=December 26, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191226033541/https://www.si.com/vault/2015/07/09/106342554/dennis-rodman |url-status=live }}</ref> and he was inducted into the [[Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame]] later that year.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.foxsports.com/nba/story/basketball-hall-of-fame-enshrines-chris-mullin-dennis-rodman-arvydas-sabonis-tex-winter-081211|title=Rodman, Mullin enshrined in Hall of Fame|access-date=August 12, 2011|work=Fox Sports|date=August 12, 2011|archive-date=January 12, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150112001159/http://www.foxsports.com/nba/story/basketball-hall-of-fame-enshrines-chris-mullin-dennis-rodman-arvydas-sabonis-tex-winter-081211|url-status=live}}</ref> In October 2021, Rodman was honored as one of the&nbsp;league's greatest players of all-time by being named to the&nbsp;[[NBA 75th Anniversary Team]].<ref>nba.com/75</ref>

Rodman experienced an unhappy childhood and was often described as shy and [[introverted]] in his early years. After contemplating suicide in 1993, he reinvented himself as a "bad boy" and became notorious for numerous controversial antics. He repeatedly dyed his hair in artificial colors, had many [[Body piercing|piercing]]s and [[tattoo]]s, and regularly disrupted games by clashing with opposing players and officials. He famously wore a [[wedding dress]] to promote his 1996 autobiography ''[[Bad As I Wanna Be: The Dennis Rodman Story|Bad as I Wanna Be]]''. Rodman also attracted international attention for his visits to [[North Korea]] and his subsequent befriending of the North Korean supreme leader [[Kim Jong Un]] in 2013.

In addition to being a former professional basketball player, Rodman has appeared in [[professional wrestling]]. He was a member of the [[New World Order (professional wrestling)|nWo]] and fought alongside [[Hulk Hogan]] in the [[main event]] of two [[Bash at the Beach]] pay-per-views. In professional wrestling, Rodman was the first-ever winner of the [[Hulk Hogan's Celebrity Championship Wrestling|Celebrity Championship Wrestling]] tournament. He had his own TV show, ''[[The Rodman World Tour]]'', and had starring roles in the action films ''[[Double Team (film)|Double Team]]'' (1997) and ''[[Simon Sez]]'' (1999). He appeared in several reality TV series and was the winner of the $222,000 main prize of the 2004 edition of ''[[The Mole (American season 4)|Celebrity Mole]]''.

==Early life==
Rodman was born in [[Trenton, New Jersey]], the son of Shirley and Philander Rodman, Jr., an [[United States Air Force|Air Force]] enlisted member, who later fought in the [[Vietnam War]]. When he was young, his father left his family, eventually settling in the [[Philippines]].<ref name="philander">{{cite news|date=September 23, 1996|title=Dennis Rodman's dad has 27 kids and runs a bar in the Philippines|work=Jet |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=YzkDAAAAMBAJ&q=dennis+rodman+jet&pg=PA50|access-date=May 5, 2020|archive-date=December 31, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211231112229/https://books.google.com/books?id=YzkDAAAAMBAJ&q=dennis+rodman+jet&pg=PA50|url-status=live}}</ref> Rodman has many brothers and sisters: according to his father, he has either 26 or 28 siblings on his father's side. However, Rodman has stated that he is the oldest of a total of 47 children.<ref name="philander" /><ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NQQu-JWzBug |title=Dennis Rodman Emotional Hall of Fame Speech |access-date=March 28, 2012 |publisher=YouTube |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120214081057/http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NQQu-JWzBug |archive-date=February 14, 2012}} at 5:30 mins.</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.nbcsports.com/nba/news/just-for-the-record-rodman-only-has-28-siblings|title=Just for the record, Rodman only has 28 siblings|access-date=March 28, 2012|publisher=NBC Sports|date=August 15, 2011|archive-date=September 17, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110917044835/http://probasketballtalk.nbcsports.com/2011/08/15/just-for-the-record-rodman-only-has-28-siblings/|url-status=live}}</ref>

After his father left, Shirley took many odd jobs to support the family, up to four at the same time.<ref name="ESPN 2006"/> In his 1996 biography ''Bad As I Wanna Be'', he expresses his feelings for his father: "I haven't seen my father in more than 30 years, so what's there to miss ... I just look at it like this: Some man brought me into this world. That doesn't mean I have a father".<ref name="philander" /> He would not meet his father again until 2012.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cbsnews.com/news/nba-hall-of-famer-dennis-rodman-finally-meets-his-father-after-42-years/ |title=NBA Hall of Famer Dennis Rodman finally meets his father after 42 years |website=[[CBS News]] |date=July 19, 2012 |access-date=February 24, 2017 |archive-date=February 24, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170224133056/http://www.cbsnews.com/news/nba-hall-of-famer-dennis-rodman-finally-meets-his-father-after-42-years/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

Rodman and his two sisters, Debra and Kim,<ref name="aggies">{{cite web|url=http://www.aggieathletics.com/sports/w-baskbl/spec-rel/031507aaa.html|title=Big Hopes In Big Dance For Big 12 Champion and No. 4 Seeded Aggies|date=March 15, 2007|publisher=Texas A&M Athletic Department|access-date=August 31, 2008 |archive-date=July 7, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110707093607/http://www.aggieathletics.com/sports/w-baskbl/spec-rel/031507aaa.html}}</ref> grew up in the [[Oak Cliff]] section of [[Dallas]],<ref>{{cite web|author=Rodman, Dennis|access-date=December 13, 2021|url=https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/?date=19960609&slug=2333674|title=After A Difficult Childhood In Projects Near Dallas, Dennis Rodman Reached A Turning Point As A Basketball Player At Southeastern Oklahoma. While attending [[South Oak Cliff High School]], Rodman was a gym class student of future [[Texas A&M University|Texas A&M]] basketball coach [[Gary Blair]].<ref name="gym">{{cite web|url=http://www.aggieathletics.com/ViewArticle.dbml?SPSID=632734&SPID=93247&DB_LANG=C&DB_OEM_ID=27300&ATCLID=205232700|title=Big Hopes In Big Dance For Big 12 Champion and No. 4 Seeded Aggies|website=Aggieathletics.com|access-date=January 24, 2013|archive-date=June 24, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130624222344/http://www.aggieathletics.com/ViewArticle.dbml?SPSID=632734&SPID=93247&DB_LANG=C&DB_OEM_ID=27300&ATCLID=205232700|url-status=live}}</ref> Blair coached Rodman's sisters Debra and Kim, winning three state championships.<ref name="hoops">{{cite web |last1=Miller |first1=Jeff |title=Gary Blair engineers women's hoops revival at Texas A&M |url=http://usatoday30.usatoday.com/sports/college/womensbasketball/2011-03-30-gary-blair-texas-am_N.htm |website=USA Today |access-date=January 24, 2013 |date=March 30, 2011 |archive-date=January 7, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140107184615/http://usatoday30.usatoday.com/sports/college/womensbasketball/2011-03-30-gary-blair-texas-am_N.htm |url-status=live }}</ref> However, Rodman was not considered an athletic standout. According to Rodman, he was "unable to hit a [[layup]]" and was listed in the high school basketball teams but was either benched or cut from the squads. Measuring only {{height|ft=5|in=6}} as a freshman in high school,<ref name="ESPN 2006">{{cite web|url=http://sports.espn.go.com/espn/classic/bio/news/story?page=Rodman_Dennis |title=Rodman, King or Queen of Rebounds? |date=February 21, 2006 |work=ESPN |access-date=August 6, 2010 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110629025453/http://sports.espn.go.com/espn/classic/bio/news/story?page=Rodman_Dennis |archive-date=June 29, 2011}}</ref> he also failed to make the football teams and was "totally devastated".<ref name="bwg">{{cite magazine|url=http://vault.sportsillustrated.cnn.com/vault/article/magazine/MAG1067269/index.htm |title=Black, White – and Gray: Piston Dennis Rodman's life was complicated by racial matters long before his inflammatory words about Larry Bird |last=Bruce |first=Newman |date=May 2, 1988 |magazine=Sports Illustrated |access-date=August 31, 2008 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080617184025/http://vault.sportsillustrated.cnn.com/vault/article/magazine/MAG1067269/index.htm |archive-date=June 17, 2008}}</ref>

==College career==
After finishing high school, Rodman worked as an overnight janitor at [[Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport|Dallas Fort Worth International Airport]]. He then experienced a sudden growth spurt from {{height|ft=5|in=11}} to {{height|ft=6|in=7}} and decided to try basketball again,<ref name="nbacom">{{cite web|url=http://www.nba.com/playerfile/dennis_rodman/bio.html |title=Dennis Rodman bio |publisher=NBA |access-date=August 31, 2008 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100420020818/http://www.nba.com/playerfile/dennis_rodman/bio.html |archive-date=April 20, 2010}}</ref> despite becoming even more withdrawn because he felt odd in his own body.<ref name="bwg" />

A family friend tipped off the head coach of Cooke County College (now [[North Central Texas College]]) in [[Gainesville, Texas]]. In his single semester there, he averaged 17.6 points and 13.3 rebounds, before flunking out due to poor academic performance.<ref name="ESPN 2006"/> After his short stint in Gainesville, he transferred to [[Southeastern Oklahoma State University]], an [[National Association of Intercollegiate Athletics|NAIA]] school. There, Rodman was a three-time NAIA [[All-America]]n and led the NAIA in rebounding twice (1985, 1986). In three seasons there (1983–1986), he averaged 25.7 points and 15.7 rebounds,<ref name="stats">{{cite web|url=https://www.basketball-reference.com/players/r/rodmade01.html|title=Dennis Rodman Statistics|publisher=Basketball-Reference.com|access-date=August 31, 2008|archive-date=July 4, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110704085153/http://www.basketball-reference.com/players/r/rodmade01.html|url-status=live}}</ref> and registered a .637 field goal percentage.<ref name="nbacom" /> In 1986 he led his team to the NAIA semifinals where he scored 46 points in a single game, while grabbing a tournament-tying record 32 rebounds, as they finished the season with the highest ranking in school history, at No. 3 in the nation. This helped get him invited to the [[Portsmouth Invitational Tournament]], a pre-draft camp for NBA hopefuls, where he won Most Valuable Player honors and caught the attention of the [[Detroit Pistons]].<ref name="ESPN 2006"/>

During college, Rodman worked at a summer youth basketball camp, where he befriended camper Byrne Rich who was 13 years old at the time, who was shy and withdrawn due to a hunting accident in which he mistakenly shot and killed his best friend. The two became almost inseparable. Rich invited Rodman to his rural Oklahoma home; at first, Rodman was not well-received by the Riches due to the fact he was 22 years old and black, but the Riches were so grateful to him for bringing their son out of his shell that they were able to set aside their prejudices.<ref name="bwg2">{{cite magazine |last1=Newman |first1=Bruce |title=Black, White–and Gray (Part 2) |url=http://vault.sportsillustrated.cnn.com/vault/article/magazine/MAG1067269/2/index.htm |magazine=Sports Illustrated |access-date=August 31, 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080617184007/http://vault.sportsillustrated.cnn.com/vault/article/magazine/MAG1067269/2/index.htm |archive-date=June 17, 2008 |date=May 2, 1988}}</ref> Although Rodman had severe family and personal issues himself, he "adopted" the Riches as his own in 1982 and went from the city life to "driving a tractor and messing with cows".<ref name="bwg2" /> Though Rodman credited the Riches as his "surrogate family" that helped him through college, as of 2013 he had stopped communicating with the Rich family after Byrne's mother allegedly referred to Rodman as a "[[nigger]]".<ref>{{cite web |author= Rohan Nadkarni |url= https://www.si.com/.amp/nba/2019/09/10/dennis-rodman-espn-30-30-bulls-pistons-spurs-donald-trump |title= Dennis Rodman Needs A Hug |publisher= Sports Illustrated |date= September 10, 2019 |access-date= September 18, 2022 |archive-date= September 20, 2022 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20220920170330/https://www.si.com/.amp/nba/2019/09/10/dennis-rodman-espn-30-30-bulls-pistons-spurs-donald-trump |url-status= live }}</ref>

==Professional basketball career==

=== Detroit Pistons (1986–1993) ===

====Early years and first championship (1986–1989)====
[[File:Dennis Rodman Autographed Wilson Basketball.jpg|thumb|Dennis Rodman autographed Wilson basketball.]]
Rodman made himself eligible for the [[1986 NBA draft]]. He was drafted by the [[Detroit Pistons]] as the third pick in the second round (27th overall), joining the rugged team of coach [[Chuck Daly]] that was called "Bad Boys" for their hard-nosed approach to basketball. The squad featured [[Isiah Thomas]] and [[Joe Dumars]] at the [[Basketball position#Backcourt (guards)|guard]] positions, [[Adrian Dantley]] and [[Sidney Green (basketball)|Sidney Green]] at forward, and [[center (basketball)|center]] [[Bill Laimbeer]]. Bench players who played more than 15 minutes per game were [[sixth man]] [[Vinnie Johnson]] and the backup forwards [[Rick Mahorn]] and [[John Salley]].<ref name="pistons8687">{{cite web|url=https://www.basketball-reference.com/teams/DET/1987.html|title=1986–87 Detroit Pistons|publisher=Basketball-Reference.com|access-date=August 31, 2008|archive-date=September 14, 2008|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080914202108/http://www.basketball-reference.com/teams/DET/1987.html|url-status=live}}</ref> Rodman fit well into this ensemble, providing 6.5 points, 4.7 rebounds and some tough defense in 15.0 minutes of playing time per game.<ref name="stats" />

Winning 52 games, the Pistons comfortably entered the [[1987 NBA playoffs|1987 playoffs]]. They swept the [[Washington Bullets]] and soundly beat the [[Atlanta Hawks]] in five games but bowed out in seven matches against the archrival [[Boston Celtics]] in what was called one of the physically and mentally toughest series ever. Rodman feuded with Celtics guard [[Dennis Johnson]] and taunted Johnson in the closing seconds when he waved his right hand over his own head. As the Celtics closed out Game Seven, Johnson went back to Rodman in the last moments of the game and mimicked his taunting gesture.<ref name="simmons">{{cite web |url=https://www.espn.com/espn/page2/story?page=simmons/070222 |title=Page 2 – DJ should have made Springfield while still alive |access-date=August 17, 2007 |work=ESPN |date=February 23, 2007 |last=Simmons |first=Bill |archive-date=October 18, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121018204850/http://sports.espn.go.com/espn/page2/story?page=simmons/070222 |url-status=live }}</ref>

After the loss, Rodman made headlines by saying Celtics star [[Larry Bird]] was overrated because he was white. "Larry Bird is overrated in a lot of areas....Why does he get so much publicity? Because he's white. You never hear about a black player being the greatest". Although teammate Thomas supported him, he endured harsh criticism, but avoided being called a racist because, according to him, his own girlfriend Anicka "Annie" Bakes was white.<ref name="ESPN 2006"/><ref name="bwg" />

In the following [[1987–88 NBA season|1987–88 season]], Rodman steadily improved his stats, averaging 11.6 points and 8.7 rebounds and starting in 32 of 82 regular season games.<ref name="stats" /> The Pistons fought their way into the [[1988 NBA Finals]], and took a 3–2 lead, but lost in seven games against the [[Los Angeles Lakers]]. In Game Six, the Pistons were down by one point with eight seconds to go; Dumars missed a shot, and Rodman just fell short of an offensive rebound and a putback that could have won the title. In Game Seven, L.A. led by 15 points in the fourth quarter, but Rodman's defense helped cut down the lead to six with 3:52 minutes to go and to two with one minute to go. But then, he fouled [[Magic Johnson]], who hit a free throw, missed an ill-advised shot with 39 seconds to go, and the Pistons never recovered.<ref name="1988finals">{{cite web |title=Lakers Capture the Elusive Repeat |url=http://www.nba.com/history/finals/19871988.html |website=NBA.com |access-date=January 7, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120222173733/http://www.nba.com/history/finals/19871988.html |archive-date=February 22, 2012}}</ref> In that year, he and his girlfriend Annie had a daughter they named Alexis.<ref name="ESPN 2006"/>

Rodman remained a bench player during the [[1988–89 NBA season|1988–89 season]], averaging 9.0 points and 9.4 rebounds in 27 minutes, yet providing such effective defense that he was voted into the All-Defensive Team, the first of eight times in his career.<ref name="stats" /> He also began seeing more playing time after Adrian Dantley was traded at midseason to Dallas for [[Mark Aguirre]]. In that season, the Pistons finally vanquished their playoffs bane by sweeping the Boston Celtics, then winning in six games versus the [[Chicago Bulls]]—including scoring champion [[Michael Jordan]]—and easily defeating the Lakers 4–0 in the [[1989 NBA Finals]]. Rodman was hampered by back spasms but dominated the boards, grabbing 19 rebounds in Game 3 and providing tough interior defense.<ref name="1989finals">{{cite web |title=Waiting Game Ends for Impatient Pistons |url=http://www.nba.com/history/finals/19881989.html |website=NBA.com |access-date=January 7, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120222151438/http://www.nba.com/history/finals/19881989.html |archive-date=February 22, 2012}}</ref>

====Second championship and rebounding titles (1989–1993)====
[[File:Dennis Rodman-Jackson Citizen Patriot-3 February 1989.jpg|thumb|Rodman in 1989]]
In the [[1989–90 NBA season|1989–90 season]], Detroit lost perennial defensive forward Rick Mahorn, who was taken by the [[Minnesota Timberwolves]] in that year's [[expansion draft]] and ended up on the [[Philadelphia 76ers]] when the Pistons could not reacquire him. It was feared that the loss of Mahorn – average in talent, but high on hustle and widely considered a vital cog of the "Bad Boys" teams – would diminish the Pistons' spirit, but Rodman seamlessly took over his role.<ref name="1990nbafinals">{{cite web |title=Bad Boys Still the Best |url=http://www.nba.com/history/finals/19891990.html |website=NBA.com |access-date=January 7, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120222183055/http://www.nba.com/history/finals/19891990.html |archive-date=February 22, 2012}}</ref> Averaging 8.8 points and 9.7 rebounds while starting in the last 43 regular-season games, he established himself as the best defensive player in the game, lauded by the NBA "for his defense and rebounding skills, which were unparalleled in the league".<ref name="nbacom" /> Rodman's feats drew him his first big individual accolade: the [[NBA Defensive Player of the Year Award]]. He also had a .595 field goal percentage, best in the league.<ref name="stats" /> In the [[1990 NBA playoffs|1990 playoffs]], the Pistons beat the Bulls again, and in the [[1990 NBA Finals]], Detroit met the [[Portland Trail Blazers]]. Rodman suffered from an injured ankle and was often replaced by Mark Aguirre, but even without his defensive hustle, Detroit beat Portland in five games and claimed their second title.<ref name="1990nbafinals" />

During the [[1990–91 NBA season|1990–91 season]], Rodman finally established himself as the starting [[small forward]] of the Pistons. He played such strong defense that the NBA said he "could shut down any opposing player, from point guard to center".<ref name="nbacom" /> After coming off the bench for most of his earlier years, he started in 77 of the 82 regular-season games, averaged 8.2 points and 12.5 rebounds, and won his second Defensive Player of the Year Award.<ref name="stats" /> In the [[1991 NBA playoffs|1991 playoffs]], the Pistons were swept by the Chicago Bulls in the Eastern Conference Finals. The Bulls went on to win the championship.

In the [[1991–92 NBA season|1991–92 season]], Rodman made a remarkable leap in his rebounding. He collected an astounding 18.7 per game, winning his first of seven consecutive rebounding crowns, along with scoring 9.8 points per game, and making his first All-NBA Team.<ref name="stats" /> His 1,530 season rebounds (the most since [[Wilt Chamberlain]]'s 1,572 in the [[1971–72 NBA season|1971–1972 season]]) have not since been surpassed. ([[Kevin Willis]], who grabbed 1,258 boards that same season, came closest.<ref name="rebleader">{{cite web |title=NBA & ABA Single Season Leaders and Records for Total Rebounds |url=https://www.basketball-reference.com/leaders/trb_season.html |website=Basketball-Reference.com |access-date=August 31, 2008 |archive-date=September 22, 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080922121630/http://www.basketball-reference.com/leaders/trb_season.html |url-status=live }}</ref> Willis lamented that Rodman had an advantage in winning the rebounding title with his lack of offensive responsibilities.<ref>{{cite news|last=Barnard|first=Bill|title=Rebounding rage|date=March 22, 1992|newspaper=Chicago Sun-Times|url=http://www.highbeam.com/doc/1P2-4101905.html|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150329142053/http://www.highbeam.com/doc/1P2-4101905.html|url-status=usurped|archive-date=March 29, 2015|via=[[HighBeam Research]]|url-access=subscription|access-date=February 22, 2013}}</ref>) In a March 1992 game, Rodman grabbed a career-high 34 rebounds.<ref>{{cite web |title=Indiana Pacers at Detroit Pistons Box Score, March 4, 1992 |url=https://www.basketball-reference.com/boxscores/199203040DET.html |website=Basketball-Reference.com |access-date=January 7, 2020 |archive-date=June 17, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200617023347/https://www.basketball-reference.com/boxscores/199203040DET.html |url-status=live }}</ref> However, the aging Pistons were eliminated by the up-and-coming [[New York Knicks]] in the first round of the [[1992 NBA playoffs|1992 playoffs]].

Rodman experienced a tough loss when coach Chuck Daly, whom he had admired as a surrogate father, resigned in May; Rodman skipped the preseason camp and was fined $68,000.<ref name="ESPN 2006"/> The following [[1992–93 NBA season|1992–93 season]] was even more tumultuous. Rodman and Annie Bakes, the mother of his daughter Alexis, were divorcing<ref name=annie>{{cite news|title=Rumor: Rodman to Kings for Tisdale|url=https://news.google.com/newspapers?id=QJUzAAAAIBAJ&pg=5631,5111053&dq=dennis-rodman+annie-bakes+divorce&hl=en|newspaper=Lodi News-Sentinel|date=June 11, 1993|access-date=June 2, 2020|archive-date=June 22, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200622195732/https://news.google.com/newspapers?id=QJUzAAAAIBAJ&pg=5631%2C5111053&dq=dennis-rodman+annie-bakes+divorce&hl=en|url-status=live}}</ref> after a short marriage, an experience which left him traumatized.<ref name="ESPN 2006"/> The Pistons won only 40 games and missed the [[1993 NBA playoffs|1993 playoffs]] entirely.

Four years later in his biography ''Bad As I Wanna Be'', Rodman revealed just how far he had fallen. He had driven to The Palace of Auburn Hills in February 1993 late one night carrying a loaded rifle in his truck, debating whether or not he wanted to continue living. Eventually he fell asleep in the truck, where he was found by police who had been called to perform a welfare check on Rodman by a friend. It was in that moment that he had an [[Epiphany (feeling)|epiphany]]: "I decided that instead [of killing myself] I was gonna kill the impostor that was leading Dennis Rodman to a place he didn't want to go ... So I just said, 'I'm going to live my life the way I want to live it and be happy doing it.' At that moment I tamed [sic] my whole life around. I killed the person I didn't want to be."<ref name="rifle">{{cite web |last1=Marvel |first1=Mark |title=Ramrodman - interview with basketball player Dennis Rodman |url=http://findarticles.com/p/articles/mi_m1285/is_n2_v27/ai_19192189 |access-date=September 1, 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080428120325/http://findarticles.com/p/articles/mi_m1285/is_n2_v27/ai_19192189 |archive-date=April 28, 2008 |url-status=usurped |work=Interview |issue=2 |volume=27 |date=February 1997}}</ref> The book was later adapted for a TV movie ''Bad As I Wanna Be: The Dennis Rodman Story''. Although he had three years and $11.8 million remaining on his contract, Rodman demanded a trade. On October 1, 1993, the Pistons dealt him to the [[San Antonio Spurs]].<ref name="ESPN 2006"/>

=== San Antonio Spurs (1993–1995) ===

==== Playoff upset (1993–1994) ====
In the [[1993–94 NBA season|1993–94 season]], Rodman joined a Spurs team that was built around perennial All-Star center [[David Robinson (basketball)|David Robinson]], with a supporting cast of forwards [[Dale Ellis]], [[Willie Anderson (basketball)|Willie Anderson]] and guard [[Vinny Del Negro]].<ref name="sas9394">{{cite web |title=1993-94 San Antonio Spurs Roster and Stats |url=https://www.basketball-reference.com/teams/SAS/1994.html |website=Basketball-Reference.com |access-date=August 31, 2008 |archive-date=September 14, 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080914081247/http://www.basketball-reference.com/teams/SAS/1994.html |url-status=live }}</ref> On the hardwood, Rodman now was played as a [[power forward (basketball)|power forward]] and won his third straight rebounding title, averaging 17.3 boards per game, along with another All-Defensive Team call-up.<ref name="stats" /> Living up to his promise of killing the "shy imposter" and "being himself" instead, Rodman began to show first signs of unconventional behavior: before the first game, he shaved his hair and dyed it blonde, which was followed up by stints with red, purple, blue hair and a look inspired from the film ''[[Demolition Man (film)|Demolition Man]]''.<ref name="nbacom" /> During the season, he headbutted [[Stacey King]] and [[John Stockton]], refused to leave the hardwood once after being ejected, and had a highly publicized affair with [[Madonna]].<ref name="ESPN 2006"/><ref name="top10">{{cite news|last=Hannigan|first=Dave|url=http://www.timesonline.co.uk/tol/sport/article786188.ece|title=The top 10 Dennis Rodman moments|newspaper=Sunday Times|date=January 8, 2006|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110629124702/http://www.timesonline.co.uk/tol/sport/article786188.ece|archive-date=June 29, 2011}}</ref> The only player to whom Rodman related was reserve center [[Jack Haley (basketball)|Jack Haley]], who earned his trust by not being shocked after a visit to a [[gay bar]].<ref name="haley">{{cite news |last1=Friend |first1=Tom |title=PRO BASKETBALL; A Nonconformist in a League of His Own |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1995/04/20/sports/pro-basketball-a-nonconformist-in-a-league-of-his-own.html |website=The New York Times |access-date=August 31, 2008 |date=April 20, 1995 |archive-date=April 3, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090403223550/http://www.nytimes.com/1995/04/20/sports/pro-basketball-a-nonconformist-in-a-league-of-his-own.html |url-status=live }}</ref> However, despite a 55-win season, Rodman and the Spurs did not survive the first round of the [[1994 NBA playoffs|1994 playoffs]] and bowed out against the [[Utah Jazz]] in four games.

==== Team conflict and shoulder injury (1994–1995) ====
In the following [[1994–95 NBA season|1994–95 season]], Rodman clashed with the Spurs front office. He was suspended for the first three games, took a leave of absence on November 11, and was suspended again on December 7. He finally returned on December 10 after missing 19 games.<ref name="nbacom" /> After joining the team, he suffered a shoulder separation in a motorcycle accident, limiting his season to 49 games. Normally, he would not have qualified for any season records for missing so many games, but by grabbing 823 rebounds, he just surpassed the 800-rebound limit for listing players and won his fourth straight rebounding title by averaging 16.8 boards per game and made the All-NBA Team.<ref name="nbacom" /> The Spurs won 62 games.

However, things fell apart in the [[1995 NBA playoffs|playoffs]]. During the second round series against the Los Angeles Lakers, Rodman was suspended for insubordination for sitting on the floor with his shoes off during a timeout. Despite this, the Spurs advanced to the Western Conference Finals against their division rivals, the Houston Rockets. The Rockets had only recorded 47 wins and had to come back from a 3-1 deficit to win their previous series against the Phoenix Suns, but the team was the defending NBA champion, and it was thought that Rockets center [[Hakeem Olajuwon]] would have a hard time asserting himself versus Robinson and Rodman, who had both been voted into the NBA All-Defensive Teams.<ref name="nbacom" />

The Spurs, however, were never able to stop Olajuwon as he averaged 35.3 points per game in the series. Rodman was particularly critical of Spurs coach Bob Hill's play calling, saying that he coached the playoffs "like we were playing Minnesota in the middle of December" and that his idea that Rodman should assist Robinson, who he accused of freezing up in big moments, in defending Olajuwon was counterproductive because it would force him off his man and leave the Spurs prone to giving up scoring opportunities. He even went off on the organization, including general manager [[Gregg Popovich]], in the locker room following a game, saying that everything they did "sucked" and that Hill was "a loser". The Spurs would lose in six games, in a series Rodman said they "could have and SHOULD have" won.

Rodman admitted his frequent transgressions, but asserted that he lived his own life and thus a more honest life than most other people:

<blockquote>I just took the chance to be my own man ... I just said: "If you don't like it, kiss my ass." ... Most people around the country, or around the world, are basically working people who want to be free, who want to be themselves. They look at me and see someone trying to do that ... I'm the guy who's showing people, hey, it's all right to be different. And I think they feel: "Let's go and see this guy entertain us."<ref name="rifle" /></blockquote>

=== Chicago Bulls (1995–1998) ===

==== Third championship (1995–1996) ====
[[File:United Center.jpg|thumb|280px| The [[United Center]], home of the [[Chicago Bulls]]. Rodman wrote history in the [[1996 NBA Finals]] when he twice secured 11 offensive rebounds in this building, tying an all-time NBA record.]]
Before the [[1995–96 NBA season|1995–96 season]], Rodman was traded to the [[Chicago Bulls]] for center [[Will Perdue]] to fill a void at power forward left by [[Horace Grant]], who had left the team before the 1994–95 season.<ref name="tradebulls">{{cite news |last1=Gano |first1=Rick |title=Bulls acquire Rodman from Spurs |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/sports/1995/10/03/bulls-acquire-rodman-from-spurs/05ef2039-04da-4483-a7bd-84219c40417d/ |newspaper=Washington Post |access-date=January 7, 2020 |date=October 3, 1995 |archive-date=May 4, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200504082455/https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/sports/1995/10/03/bulls-acquire-rodman-from-spurs/05ef2039-04da-4483-a7bd-84219c40417d/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Bulls Take a Chance on Rodman : Pro basketball: Controversial forward is traded from San Antonio for Will Perdue. |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1995-10-03-sp-52721-story.html |website=Los Angeles Times |access-date=January 7, 2020 |date=October 3, 1995 |archive-date=May 6, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200506125349/https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1995-10-03-sp-52721-story.html |url-status=live }}</ref> Rodman could not wear No. 10 jersey because the Bulls had retired it for [[Bob Love]], and the NBA denied him the reversion 01, Rodman instead picked the number 91, whose digits add up to 10.<ref>Armour, Terry. [https://www.chicagotribune.com/1995/10/06/joining-bulls-almost-like-a-storybook-for-former-collins-prep-brown/ Joining Bulls `Almost Like A Storybook' For Former Collins Prep Brown] . October 6, 1995</ref> Although the trade for the already 34-year-old and volatile Rodman was considered a gamble at that time,<ref name="nbacom" /> the power forward quickly adapted to his new environment, helped by the fact that his best friend [[Jack Haley (basketball)|Jack Haley]] was also traded to the Bulls.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Villanueva |first1=Virgil |title="We spend a lot of time out there together" — Jack Haley on "babysitting" Dennis Rodman |url=https://www.basketballnetwork.net/old-school/jack-haley-on-babysitting-dennis-rodman |website=Basketball Network |date=August 21, 2022 |language=en |access-date=January 7, 2023 |archive-date=January 7, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230107161202/https://www.basketballnetwork.net/old-school/jack-haley-on-babysitting-dennis-rodman |url-status=live }}</ref> Under coach [[Phil Jackson]], he averaged 5.5 points and 14.9 rebounds per game, winning yet another rebounding title, and was part of the great Bulls team that won 72 of 82 regular season games, an NBA record at the time.<ref name="bestever">{{cite web |last1=Hareas |first1=John |title=Best Ever? Ten Reasons Why |url=http://www.nba.com/features/bullsbestever_top10.html |website=NBA.com |access-date=January 7, 2020 |date=March 28, 2007 |archive-date=November 21, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121121235352/http://www.nba.com/features/bullsbestever_top10.html |url-status=live }}</ref> About playing next to the iconic Jordan and co-star [[Scottie Pippen]], Rodman said:

<blockquote>On the court, me and Michael are pretty calm and we can handle conversation. But as far as our lives go, I think he is moving in one direction and I'm going in the other. I mean, he's goin' north, I'm goin' south. And then you've got Scottie Pippen right in the middle. He's sort of the equator.<ref name="rifle" /></blockquote>

Although struggling with calf problems early in the season, Rodman grabbed 20 or more rebounds 11 times and had his first [[triple-double]] against the [[Philadelphia 76ers]] on January 16, 1996, scoring 10 points and adding 21 rebounds and 10 assists; by playing his trademark tough defense, he joined Jordan and Pippen in the All-NBA Defense First Team. On March 16, 1996, Rodman [[Headbutt|head butted]] referee Ted Bernhardt during a game in [[New Jersey]]; he was suspended for six games and fined $20,000, a punishment that was criticized as too lenient by the local press.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://goinside.com/97/6/rodman.html |title=Dennis Rodman and the $50,000 Mormon Fine |access-date=August 9, 2006 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/19980111112402/http://goinside.com/97/6/rodman.html |archive-date=January 11, 1998}}. Retrieved August 31, 2008</ref>

In the [[1996 NBA playoffs|1996 playoffs]], Rodman scored 7.5 points and grabbed 13.7 rebounds per game and had a large part in the six-game victory against the [[Seattle SuperSonics]] in the [[1996 NBA Finals]]: in Game Two at home in the Bulls' [[United Center]], he grabbed 20 rebounds, among them a record-tying 11 offensive boards, and in Game Six, again at the United Center, the power forward secured 19 rebounds and again 11 offensive boards, scored five points in a decisive 12–2 Bulls run, unnerved opposing power forward [[Shawn Kemp]] and caused Seattle coach [[George Karl]] to say: "As you evaluate the series, Dennis Rodman won two basketball games. We controlled Dennis Rodman for four games. But Game 2 and tonight, he was the reason they were successful."<ref name="1996finals">{{cite web |title=Bulls' Record-Setting Season Ends in Victory |url=http://www.nba.com/history/finals/19951996.html |website=NBA.com |access-date=January 7, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120222154955/http://www.nba.com/history/finals/19951996.html |archive-date=February 22, 2012}}</ref> His two games with 11 offensive rebounds each tied the NBA Finals record of [[Elvin Hayes]].<ref name="nbacom"/>

Rodman garnered much publicity for his public antics.<ref name="top10" /> In 1996, Rodman wore a [[wedding dress]] to promote his autobiography ''Bad As I Wanna Be'', claiming that he was [[Bisexuality|bisexual]] and that he was marrying himself.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Barboza |first=Craigh |date=May 12, 2020 |title=Remember when Dennis Rodman put on a wedding dress and claimed to marry himself? |url=https://www.cnn.com/style/article/remember-when-dennis-rodman/index.html |access-date=January 11, 2024 |website=CNN |language=en |archive-date=September 9, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230909103300/https://www.cnn.com/style/article/remember-when-dennis-rodman/index.html |url-status=live }}</ref>

==== Suspensions and fourth championship (1996–1997) ====
In the [[1996–97 NBA season|1996–97 season]], Rodman won his sixth rebounding title in a row with 16.7 boards per game, along with 5.7 points per game, but failed to rank another All-Defensive Team call-up.<ref name="stats" /> However, he made more headlines for his notorious behavior. On January 15, 1997, he was involved in an incident during a game against the [[Minnesota Timberwolves]]. After tripping over cameraman Eugene Amos, Rodman kicked Amos in the groin. Though he was not assessed a [[technical foul]] at the time, he ultimately paid Amos a $200,000 settlement, and the league suspended Rodman for 11 games without pay. Thus, he effectively lost $1 million.<ref>{{cite news |title=Rodman to Pay Cameraman |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1997/01/21/sports/rodman-to-pay-cameraman.html |website=The New York Times |access-date=January 7, 2020 |date=January 21, 1997 |archive-date=January 3, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200103164036/https://www.nytimes.com/1997/01/21/sports/rodman-to-pay-cameraman.html |url-status=live }}</ref> Missing another three games to suspensions, often getting technical fouls early in games<ref name="nbacom" /> and missing an additional 13 matches due to knee problems, Rodman was not as effective in the [[1997 NBA playoffs|1997 playoffs]], in which the Bulls reached the [[1997 NBA Finals]] against the [[Utah Jazz]]. He struggled to slow down Jazz power forward [[Karl Malone]] but did his share to complete the six-game Bulls victory.<ref name="1997finals">{{cite web |title=MJ Adds More Finals Heroics to His Legacy |url=http://www.nba.com/history/finals/19961997.html |website=NBA.com |access-date=January 7, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120222150423/http://www.nba.com/history/finals/19961997.html |archive-date=February 22, 2012}}</ref>

==== Fifth ring and Finals controversy (1997–1998) ====
The regular season of the [[1997–98 NBA season|1997–98 season]] ended with Rodman winning his seventh consecutive rebounding title with 15.0 boards per game, along with 4.7 points per game.<ref name="stats" /> He grabbed 20 or more rebounds 11 times, among them a 29-board outburst against the [[Atlanta Hawks]] and 15 offensive boards (along with ten defensive) versus the [[Los Angeles Clippers]].<ref name="nbacom" /> Led by the aging Jordan and Rodman (respectively 35 and 37 years old), the Bulls reached the [[1998 NBA Finals]], again versus the Jazz. After playing strong defense on Malone in the first three games,<ref name="1998finals">{{cite web |title=Jordan's Jumper Secures Chicago's Sixth Title |url=http://www.nba.com/history/finals/19971998.html |website=NBA.com |access-date=January 7, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120222153354/http://www.nba.com/history/finals/19971998.html |archive-date=February 22, 2012}}</ref> he caused major consternation when he left his team prior to Game Four to go wrestling with [[Hulk Hogan]]. He was fined $20,000, but it was not even ten percent of what he earned with this stint.<ref name="top10" /> However, Rodman's on-court performance remained top-notch, again shutting down Malone in Game Four until the latter scored 39 points in a Jazz Game Five win, bringing the series to 3–2 from the Bulls perspective. In Game Six, Jordan hit the decisive basket after a memorable drive on Jazz forward [[Bryon Russell]], the Bulls won their third title in a row and Rodman his fifth ring.<ref name="1998finals" />

After the [[1997–98 NBA season|1997–98 season]], the Bulls started a massive rebuilding phase, largely at the behest of general manager [[Jerry Krause]]. Head coach [[Phil Jackson]] and several members of the team left via [[free agency (sports)|free agency]] or retirement, including [[Michael Jordan]], [[Scottie Pippen]], [[Steve Kerr]], and [[Jud Buechler]].<ref name="krause">{{cite web |title=Krause cites health concerns for resignation |url=https://static.espn.go.com/nba/news/2003/0407/1535067.html |website=ESPN |access-date=August 31, 2008 |date=April 7, 2003 |archive-date=December 25, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091225202844/http://static.espn.go.com/nba/news/2003/0407/1535067.html |url-status=live }}</ref> Rodman was released by the Bulls on January 21, 1999, before the start of the lockout-shortened [[1998–99 NBA season|1998–99 season]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=May 16, 2020 |title='The Last Dance': Timeline of Dennis Rodman's eventful, title-winning stint with Michael Jordan, Bulls |url=https://www.cbssports.com/nba/news/the-last-dance-timeline-of-dennis-rodmans-eventful-title-winning-stint-with-michael-jordan-bulls/ |access-date=February 19, 2024 |website=CBSSports.com |language=en}}</ref>

=== Los Angeles Lakers (1999) ===
With his sister acting as his agent at the time, Rodman joined the [[Los Angeles Lakers]], for a pro-rated salary for the remainder of the 1998–1999 season. With the Lakers he played in only 23 games, in which he started in 11 of them and averaged 2 points and 11 rebounds per game and was released in the offseason.<ref name="stats" />

=== Dallas Mavericks (2000) ===
In the [[1999–2000 NBA season|1999–2000 season]], the then-38-year-old power forward Rodman was signed by the [[Dallas Mavericks]], returning Rodman to his hometown. Prior to signing Rodman, the Mavericks, who needed rebounding as well as media attention, set a player vote to decide whether Rodman should join; "yes" narrowly won out. Rodman wanted the jersey number 69, and one jersey was made, which he held up in his introductory press conference. The league forbid him from wearing that jersey due to [[69 (sex position)|the number's sexual connotations]]; Rodman wound up wearing number 70 instead. Rodman lived in team owner [[Mark Cuban]]'s guest house until the league prevented it because it violated [[salary cap]] rules.<ref name=":7">{{Cite news |last1=Cato |first1=Tim |last2=Yousuf |first2=Saad |date=2020-05-18 |title=Dennis Rodman's last dance: The oral history of The Worm's month with Mavericks |url=https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/1819520/2020/05/18/dennis-rodmans-last-dance-the-oral-history-of-the-worms-month-with-mavericks/ |access-date=2025-08-30 |work=The New York Times |language=en-US |issn=0362-4331}}</ref> Dallas had won 10 of 13 before his arrival but went just 4–9 until he was waived by the Mavericks. He played 12 games, received six technical fouls, was ejected twice, and served a one-game suspension.<ref name="tamaso_04042011" /><ref>{{cite news|last=Kroichick|first=Ron|title=Showdown of the Clueless and Lowdown|date=March 11, 2000|website=SFGate.com|url=https://www.sfgate.com/sports/kroichick/article/Showdown-of-the-Clueless-and-Lowdown-3328229.php|access-date=April 28, 2020|quote=Well, six technical fouls and two ejections in 12 games seems like a fine place to start.|archive-date=October 1, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201001053923/https://www.sfgate.com/sports/kroichick/article/Showdown-of-the-Clueless-and-Lowdown-3328229.php|url-status=live}}</ref> Rodman frequently refused to speak to teammates or staff, and would entirely decline to participate in defensive schemes or play offense in any capacity. In one instance, Rodman, after not receiving a foul call, sat down near the free-throw line mid-game and refused to stand up again, while players continued playing around him.<ref name=":7" /> While averaging 14.3 rebounds per game, above his career average of 13.1, Rodman alienated the franchise with his erratic behavior and did not provide leadership to a team trying to qualify for their first playoffs in 10 years.<ref name="tamaso_04042011">{{cite news|last=Tomaso|first=Bruce|title=Hall of Famer Dennis Rodman wasn't a fan of Cuban's style while with Mavericks|date=April 4, 2011|newspaper=Dallas News|url=https://sportsday.dallasnews.com/dallas-mavericks/mavericksheadlines/2011/04/04/hall-of-famer-dennis-rodman-wasn_t-a-fan-of-cuban_s-style-while-with-mavericks-|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171015094235/https://sportsday.dallasnews.com/dallas-mavericks/mavericksheadlines/2011/04/04/hall-of-famer-dennis-rodman-wasn_t-a-fan-of-cuban_s-style-while-with-mavericks-|archive-date=October 15, 2017|url-status=dead|access-date=October 15, 2017}}</ref> Dallas guard [[Steve Nash]] commented that Rodman "never wanted to be [a Maverick]" and therefore was unmotivated.<ref name="lubbock">{{cite web |title=Rodman critical of Mavericks' decision to release him |url=http://www.lubbockonline.com/stories/031000/pro_031000037.shtml |website=lubockonline.com |access-date=August 31, 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121016014849/http://www.lubbockonline.com/stories/031000/pro_031000037.shtml |archive-date=October 16, 2012 |date=March 10, 2000}}</ref>

==Legacy in basketball==
From the beginning of his career, Rodman was known for his defensive hustle, which was later accompanied by his rebounding prowess. In Detroit, he was mainly played as a [[small forward]], and his usual assignment was to neutralize the opponent's best player; Rodman was so versatile that he could guard centers, forwards, or guards equally well<ref name="nbacom" /> and won two [[NBA Defensive Player of the Year Award]]s. From 1991 on, he established himself as one of the best rebounders of all time, averaging at least 15 rebounds per game in six of the next seven years.<ref name="stats" /> Playing [[Power forward (basketball)|power forward]] as member of the Spurs and the Bulls, he had a historical outburst in the [[1996 NBA Finals]]: he twice snared 11 offensive rebounds, equalling an all-time NBA record. In addition, he had a career-high 34-rebound game on March 4, 1992.<ref name="singlegamerebounds">{{cite web |title=NBA Single Game Leaders and Records for Total Rebounds |url=https://www.basketball-reference.com/leaders/trb_game.html |website=Basketball-Reference.com |access-date=September 11, 2008 |archive-date=July 4, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110704063837/http://www.basketball-reference.com/leaders/trb_game.html |url-status=live }}</ref> Rodman's rebounding prowess with Detroit and San Antonio was also aided by his decreased attention to defensive positioning and helping teammates on defense.<ref>{{cite news|last=Schmitz|first=Brian|title=Hill and Spurs have hardly felt Rodman's loss|date=March 31, 1996|newspaper=Orlando Sentinel|page=5D|url=https://news.google.com/newspapers?id=_aZhAAAAIBAJ&dq=rodman%20rotate%20spurs%20defense&pg=6550%2C6191428|access-date=February 22, 2013|archive-date=February 25, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210225043405/https://news.google.com/newspapers?id=_aZhAAAAIBAJ&dq=rodman%20rotate%20spurs%20defense&pg=6550%2C6191428|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="halberstam">{{cite book|last=Halberstam|first=Dave|title=Playing for Keeps: Michael Jordan and the World He Made|year=2012|publisher=Open Road Media|isbn=9781453286142|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=QnAG5M8lpm4C&q=rodman+rebound+defense+help&pg=PT357|access-date=February 22, 2013|quote=He'll cheat a little bit on his positioning on name="halberstam" /> Rodman's defensive intensity returned while with Chicago.<ref name="simmons_book" />

On offense, Rodman's output was mediocre. He averaged 11.6 points per game in his second season, but his average steadily dropped: in the three championship seasons with the Bulls, he averaged five points per game and connected on less than half of his field goal attempts.<ref name="stats" /> His free throw shooting (lifetime average: .584) was considered a big liability: on December 29, 1997, [[Bubba Wells]] of the [[Dallas Mavericks]] committed six intentional fouls against him in only three minutes, setting the record for the fastest foul out in NBA history. The intention was to force him to attempt free throws, which in theory would mean frequent misses and easy ball possession without giving up too many points. However, this plan backfired, as Rodman hit 9 of the 12 attempts.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Fischer |first1=David |title=Take My Record, Please |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2005/05/15/sports/take-my-record-please.html |website=The New York Times |access-date=September 11, 2008 |date=May 15, 2005 |archive-date=May 29, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150529184032/http://www.nytimes.com/2005/05/15/sports/take-my-record-please.html |url-status=live }}</ref> This was Dallas coach [[Don Nelson]]'s early version of what would later develop into the famous "[[Hack-a-Shaq]]" method that would be implemented against [[Shaquille O'Neal]], [[Dwight Howard]], and other poor free throw shooters.

In 14 NBA seasons, Rodman played in 911 games, scored 6,683 points, and grabbed 11,954 rebounds, translating to 7.3 points and 13.1 rebounds per game in only 31.7 minutes played per game.<ref name="stats" /><ref name="rebleader" /> NBA.com lauds Rodman as "arguably the best rebounding forward in NBA history and one of the most recognized athletes in the world" but adds "enigmatic and individualistic, Rodman has caught the public eye for his ever-changing hair color, tattoos and, unorthodox lifestyle".<ref name="nbacom" /> On the hardwood, he was recognized as one of the most successful defensive players ever, winning the NBA championship five times in six NBA Finals appearances (1989, 1990, 1996–1998; only loss 1988), being crowned [[NBA Defensive Player of the Year Award|NBA Defensive Player of the Year]] twice (1990–1991) and making seven NBA All-Defensive First Teams (1989–1993, 1995–1996) and NBA All-Defensive Second Teams (1994). He additionally made two All-NBA Third Teams (1992, 1995), two NBA All-Star Teams (1990, 1992) and won seven straight rebounding crowns (1992–1998) and finally led the league once in field goal percentage (1989).<ref name="stats" />

Rodman was recognized as the prototype bizarre player, stunning basketball fans with his artificial hair colors, numerous tattoos and body piercings, multiple verbal and physical assaults on officials, frequent ejections, and his tumultuous private life.<ref name="nbacom" /> He was ranked No. 48 on the 2009 revision of ''[[SLAM Magazine]]''{{'}}s Top 50 Players of All-Time.<ref>"We Got 50!", ''Slam Magazine'', August 2009</ref> In 2021, to commemorate the NBA's 75th Anniversary ''[[The Athletic]]'' ranked their top 75 players of all time, and named Rodman as the 62nd greatest player in NBA history.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/2958330/2021/11/18/nba-75-at-no-62-dennis-rodman-broke-the-mold-of-the-conventional-basketball-star/|title=NBA 75: At No. 62, Dennis Rodman broke the mold of the conventional basketball star|first=Jovan|last=Buha|website=The Athletic|access-date=March 11, 2023|archive-date=March 5, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230305232909/https://theathletic.com/2958330/2021/11/18/nba-75-at-no-62-dennis-rodman-broke-the-mold-of-the-conventional-basketball-star/|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Metta World Peace]] played one year with the 91 jersey number in homage to Rodman, who he described as a player who he liked "on the court as a hustler, not when he kicked the cameraman."<ref>{{cite web |title=NBA: 24–second clock |url=https://www.deseret.com/2004/11/1/19858701/nba-24-151-second-clock |website=Deseret News |access-date=January 7, 2020 |date=November 1, 2004 |archive-date=March 8, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210308172548/https://www.deseret.com/2004/11/1/19858701/nba-24-151-second-clock |url-status=live }}</ref>

===World Championship Wrestling (1997–1999)===
{{Main|New World Order (professional wrestling)|l1=New World Order (nWo)}}
Rodman took up his hobby of professional wrestling seriously and appeared on the edition of March 10 of ''[[WCW Monday Nitro|Monday Nitro]]'' with [[Hulk Hogan|Hollywood Hulk Hogan]] in [[World Championship Wrestling]] (WCW). At the March 1997 [[Uncensored 1997|Uncensored]] event, he appeared as a member of the [[New World Order (professional wrestling)|nWo]]. His first match was at the July 1997 [[Bash at the Beach (1997)|Bash at the Beach]] event, where he teamed with Hogan in a loss to [[Lex Luger]] and [[Big Show|The Giant]].<ref name="interbasket" /> At the August 1997 [[Road Wild 1997|Road Wild]] event, Rodman appeared as the Impostor [[Sting (wrestler)|Sting]] hitting Luger with a baseball bat to help Hogan win the [[WCW World Heavyweight Championship]], and then afterwards made an appearance without the Sting attire where he joined other NWO members backstage and spray painted the "NWO" initials on the WCW Heavyweight Championship belt.

After the [[1997–98 NBA season|1997–98 season]], where Rodman and the [[Chicago Bulls]] defeated [[Karl Malone]] and the [[Utah Jazz]] in the [[1998 NBA Finals]], Rodman and Malone squared off again, this time in a tag team match at the July 1998 [[Bash at the Beach (1998)|Bash at the Beach]] event. He fought alongside [[Hulk Hogan]], and Malone tagged along with [[Diamond Dallas Page]]. In a poorly received match, the two power forwards exchanged "rudimentary headlocks, slams and clotheslines" for 23 minutes. Rodman bested Malone again as he and Hogan picked up the win.<ref name="bash1998">{{cite web |last1=Wong |first1=Alex |title=Remembering When the Rodman-Malone NBA Finals Feud in 1998 Led to a WCW Match |url=https://bleacherreport.com/articles/2771950-remembering-when-the-rodman-malone-nba-finals-feud-in-1998-led-to-a-wcw-match |website=Bleacher Report |access-date=January 7, 2020 |date=June 1, 2018 |archive-date=April 21, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200421213742/https://bleacherreport.com/articles/2771950-remembering-when-the-rodman-malone-nba-finals-feud-in-1998-led-to-a-wcw-match |url-status=live }}</ref>

Rodman returned to [[WCW]] in 1999 and feuded with [[Randy Savage]]. This culminated in a match at [[Road Wild (1999)|Road Wild]] which Rodman lost.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.prowrestlinghistory.com/supercards/usa/wcw/roadwild.html#99|title=Road Wild 1999 results|access-date=September 24, 2016|work=Wrestling Supercards and Tournaments|archive-date=April 20, 2008|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080420063202/http://prowrestlinghistory.com/supercards/usa/wcw/roadwild.html#99|url-status=live}}</ref>

He would be inducted into the WWE Hall of Fame's [[WWE Hall of Fame (2026)|class of 2026]] by former NWO members [[Kevin Nash]] and [[Sean Waltman]] (Syxx).<ref>{{cite news|url=https://411mania.com/wrestling/411s-2026-wwe-hall-of-fame-ceremony-report/|title=411’s 2026 WWE Hall of Fame Ceremony Report|first=Robert|last=Leighty|publisher=411 Mania|date=April 18, 2026|access-date=April 18, 2026}}</ref>

Despite the timeline of the two events, the U.S. State Department, The White House, and Rodman all flatly denied any diplomatic connection or coordination between Rodman's visit and the U.S. government. The U.S. State Department said the release of Warmbier was negotiated and secured by high level U.S. diplomats including [[Joseph Y. Yun|Joseph Yun]], the State Department's special envoy on North Korea.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.reuters.com/article/us-usa-northkorea-detainee-idUSKBN1941UX|title=High-level U.S. visit leads North Korea to free student in coma|date=June 14, 2017|work=Reuters|access-date=June 16, 2017|archive-date=June 15, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170615212447/http://www.reuters.com/article/us-usa-northkorea-detainee-idUSKBN1941UX|url-status=live}}</ref> Warmbier, who was in a nonresponsive coma throughout much of his imprisonment in North Korea, died days after being returned to his family in the U.S.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/grade-point/wp/2017/06/19/otto-warmbier-dies-days-after-release-from-north-korean-detainment/|title=Otto Warmbier dies days after release from North Korean detention|newspaper=Washington Post|access-date=June 24, 2017|archive-date=June 19, 2017|archive-url=https://archive.today/20170619220836/https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/grade-point/wp/2017/06/19/otto-warmbier-dies-days-after-release-from-north-korean-detainment/|url-status=live}}</ref>

In an emotional interview with [[Michael Strahan]] of ''[[Good Morning America]]'', Rodman expressed sorrow for the death of Warmbier and said, "I was just so happy to see the kid released. Later that day, that's when we found out he was ill. No one knew that." He added that he wished to give "all the prayer and love" to the Warmbier family and had contacted them and hoped to meet with them personally.<ref name=":2">{{cite web|url=https://abcnews.go.com/International/dennis-rodman-people-good-side-north-korea/story?id=48224976|title=Dennis Rodman speaks out about his visit to North Korea|date=June 23, 2017|website=ABC News|access-date=June 24, 2017|archive-date=June 24, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170624061900/https://abcnews.go.com/International/dennis-rodman-people-good-side-north-korea/story?id=48224976|url-status=live}}</ref>

Rodman's agent, Chris Volo, told ABC News that before they left for the 2017 trip, he had asked North Korean officials to release Warmbier as a symbol of good faith for any future sports-relations visits. "I asked on behalf of Dennis for his release three times," Volo said.<ref name=":2" />

In December 2017, [[Columbia University]] professor of [[neurobiology]] [[Joseph Terwilliger]], who has accompanied Rodman to North Korea, argued that "While I don't suspect that very many Americans would have chosen him to be an emissary or international goodwill ambassador, Dennis has had a long friendship with Mr. Trump and has also developed a very cordial friendship with Mr. Kim. In this tense climate, as we stand at a perilous crossing, Mr. Rodman's unique position as a friend to the leaders of both U.S. and North Korea could provide a much-needed bridge to help resolve the current nuclear standoff."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Terwilliger  |first=Joseph  |date=December 6, 2017 |title=Blocking nuclear war would be a win for both the U.S., North Korea |url=https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2017/dec/6/blocking-nuclear-war-would-be-a-win-for-both-the-u/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171212142356/https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2017/dec/6/blocking-nuclear-war-would-be-a-win-for-both-the-u/ |archive-date=December 12, 2017 |access-date=December 12, 2017 |website=[[The Washington Times]]}}</ref>
