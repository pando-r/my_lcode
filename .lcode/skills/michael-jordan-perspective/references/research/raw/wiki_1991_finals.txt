The '''1991 NBA Finals''' was the [[NBA Finals|championship series]] of the [[National Basketball Association]]'s (NBA) [[1990–91 NBA season|1990–91 season]], and the conclusion of the [[1991 NBA playoffs|season's playoffs]]. It was also the first NBA Finals broadcast by [[NBA on NBC|NBC]] after 17 years with [[NBA on CBS|CBS]].

The [[Eastern Conference (NBA)|Eastern Conference]] champion [[1990–91 Chicago Bulls season|Chicago Bulls]] took on the [[Western Conference (NBA)|Western Conference]] champion [[1990–91 Los Angeles Lakers season|Los Angeles Lakers]] for the title, with Chicago having [[home court advantage]]. It was [[Michael Jordan]]'s first NBA Finals appearance, [[Magic Johnson]]'s last, and the last [[NBA Finals]] for the [[Los Angeles Lakers|Lakers]] until [[2000 NBA Finals|2000]]. The Bulls would win the series, 4–1. Jordan averaged 31.2 points on 56% shooting, 11.4 assists, 6.6 rebounds, 2.8 steals and 1.4 blocks en route to his first [[NBA Finals MVP|NBA Finals MVP Award]].<ref>[https://www.basketball-reference.com/players/j/jordami01/gamelog/1991 Michael Jordan 1990–91 NBA Finals], basketball-reference.com, accessed April 26, 2009.</ref>

The series was not the first time that the Bulls and Lakers faced off in the playoffs. Prior to 1991, they met for four postseason series ([[1968 NBA playoffs|1968]], [[1971 NBA playoffs|1971]], [[1972 NBA playoffs|1972]] and [[1973 NBA playoffs|1973]]), all Lakers victories. Chicago was a member of the [[Western Conference (NBA)|Western Conference]] at the time and moved into the East in 1980. The 1991 Finals marked the first time the Bulls defeated the Lakers in a playoff series.

This series would mark the end of the Lakers [[Showtime (basketball)|Showtime]] era and the beginning of the Bulls' dynasty. After winning five championships in eight finals appearances in the 1980s, the Lakers would enter a period of mediocrity for most of the 1990s before winning five championships between the 2000 and 2010 seasons.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Poulard |first=J. M. |date=2011-11-25 |title=Magic's Forgotten Finals |url=https://www.forumblueandgold.com/2011/11/25/magics-forgotten-finals/ |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=Forum Blue And Gold}}</ref>

The 1991 Lakers were led by Johnson, who was 31 and playing in what would be his last full season, as well as fellow [[NBA All Star|All-Star]] teammate [[James Worthy]]; [[Kareem Abdul-Jabbar]] had retired two seasons earlier. The Bulls, led by Jordan and small forward [[Scottie Pippen]], would win five more championships after 1991 in a seven-year span, cementing their status as a [[Dynasty (sports)|dynasty]]. Jordan became only the fourth player in NBA history (after [[Joe Fulks]], [[George Mikan]], and Abdul-Jabbar) to capture the [[List of National Basketball Association annual scoring leaders|scoring title]] and a championship in the same season.<ref>{{Cite web |last=@NBAcrazystats |first=Crazy Stats |date=February 17, 2025 |title=Michael Jordan is the only player in league history to win MVP, Scoring Title, 1st Team All-Defensive selection, NBA Championship, and Finals MVP in the same year. He did it four times (1990-91, 1991-92, 1995-96, and 1997-98). |url=https://x.com/NBAcrazystats/status/1891456321179828453?lang=en |website=[[X, formerly known as Twitter]]}}</ref>

==Background==
This was the first meeting between teams from Chicago and Los Angeles for a major professional sports championship since the [[1959 World Series]].
===Chicago Bulls===
{{main|1990–91 Chicago Bulls season}}
The 1990–91 season marked the Bulls' 25th in franchise history. The team was coming off a grueling seven-game loss to the [[1989–90 Detroit Pistons season|Detroit Pistons]] in the [[1990 NBA Playoffs|1990 Eastern Conference finals]], and in the six years since [[Michael Jordan]] joined the Bulls, they were showing signs of improvement.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Kirschenbaum |first=Alex |date=2026-03-01 |title=Sam Smith Unpacks Origins of 'The Jordan Rules' (Exclusive) |url=https://www.bleachernation.com/bulls/2026/03/01/sam-smith-jordan-rules/ |access-date=2026-06-22}}</ref>
[[File:Chicago Bulls and New Jersey Nets, January 23, 1991.jpg|thumb|Jordan, with the ball, against the [[1990–91 New Jersey Nets season|New Jersey Nets]] during the [[1990–91 Chicago Bulls season|1990–91 season]].]]
They managed to put it all together that season, winning a then-franchise record 61 games. Jordan won the scoring title for a fifth consecutive season, but the team was no longer a one-man show of years past. Instead, Jordan distributed the ball with regularity, thanks in large part to the [[triangle offense]] instituted by head coach [[Phil Jackson]] and assistant [[Tex Winter]]. This gave the Bulls additional offensive weapons to choose from, ranging from wingman [[Scottie Pippen]] and post players [[Horace Grant]] and [[Bill Cartwright]] to shooters such as [[John Paxson]] and [[B. J. Armstrong]]. Jordan's improved all-around play earned him his second [[NBA Most Valuable Player Award|MVP award]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=1990-91 NBA Awards Voting |url=https://www.basketball-reference.com/awards/awards_1991.html |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=Basketball-Reference.com}}</ref>

In the playoffs, the Bulls lost only once in the first three rounds. They swept the [[1990–91 New York Knicks season|New York Knicks]] in the first round, then eliminated the [[1990–91 Philadelphia 76ers season|Philadelphia 76ers]] in the second round. Their much-awaited rematch with the [[1990–91 Detroit Pistons season|Detroit Pistons]] in the Conference finals showcased the maturity and poise that the Bulls displayed all season, as they swept the Pistons. In a last show of defiance, most of the Pistons walked off the court with :08 left on the clock in a blowout loss at home so as not to congratulate the new Eastern Conference champions, though [[Joe Dumars]], [[Vinnie Johnson]], and [[John Salley]] did remain to shake the Bulls' hands.<ref>{{cite news |url=http://detnews.com/article/20100701/SPORTS0102/7010448/1361/Ex-Piston-Isiah-Thomas-regrets-snub-of-Bulls-in--91 |title=Ex-Piston Isiah Thomas regrets snub of Bulls in '91 |author=Vincent Goodwill |newspaper=[[The Detroit News]] |date=July 1, 2010 |access-date=July 1, 2010}}</ref>

===Los Angeles Lakers===
{{main|1990–91 Los Angeles Lakers season}}
The Lakers were coming off a second-round loss to the [[1989–90 Phoenix Suns season|Phoenix Suns]] in [[1990 NBA Playoffs|prior season's playoffs]].<ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3eR8uIQ0M1k |title=Phoenix Suns @ L.A. Lakers (Game 5 1990 NBA WCSF) |date=2024-07-15 |last=Retro Junk (Including Classic NBA) |access-date=2026-06-22 |via=YouTube}}</ref> Even though the Lakers won 63 games that season, and [[Magic Johnson]] was second in league MVP voting while surpassing [[Oscar Robertson]] for the all-time career assist record, it was clear that the team was growing weary of head coach [[Pat Riley]]'s intense approach.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Pearlman |first=Jeff |title=Exclusive Book Excerpt: Bitter, Dark Days of Pat Riley and the 'Showtime' Lakers |url=https://bleacherreport.com/articles/1967588-exclusive-book-excerpt-bitter-dark-days-of-pat-riley-and-the-showtime-lakers |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=bleacherreport.com}}</ref>

The Lakers replaced Riley with [[Mike Dunleavy, Sr.]], formerly an assistant coach with the [[1989–90 Milwaukee Bucks season|Milwaukee Bucks]]. Dunleavy then abandoned the trademark [[Showtime (basketball)|Showtime offense]] in favor of a more deliberate style of play, but despite the change in playbook, the Lakers still enjoyed an impressive season, winning 58 games.<ref>{{Cite web |last=CHERWA |first=JOHN |date=1992-05-12 |title=Bucks Reportedly Hire Dunleavy : Pro basketball: Laker coach for two seasons said to be leaving to become Milwaukee coach and general manager. |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1992-05-12-sp-1855-story.html |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=Los Angeles Times}}</ref>

In the playoffs, the Lakers swept the [[1990–91 Houston Rockets season|Houston Rockets]] in the first round, then eliminated [[Run TMC]] and the [[1990–91 Golden State Warriors season|Golden State Warriors]] in the second round. Next up for the Lakers were the [[1990–91 Portland Trail Blazers season|Portland Trail Blazers]], who were coming off a trip to the [[1990 NBA Finals|NBA finals the previous year]]. The Lakers stunned the Blazers in Portland to open the series, and went on to win the conference finals in six games.<ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QgYHsYjoS1o |title=Blazers @ Lakers 1991 WCF Game 6 (NBA On NBC) |date=2025-01-31 |last=MDBBall |access-date=2026-06-22 |via=YouTube}}</ref>

[[Michael Jordan]] started dominating with 15 points, 3 rebounds and 5 assists in the first quarter alone. In the second quarter, the Lakers continued to stay competitive despite Magic Johnson not attempting one field goal in the second quarter. Despite this, Johnson would hit back-to-back 3 pointers in the third quarter to give the Lakers their largest lead, and also  Johnson's 29th career playoff triple-double. Jordan made a comeback in the fourth quarter with 13 points, but it was [[Scottie Pippen]]'s two free throws that would give the Bulls a 91–89 lead. Each team ran the shot clock down but neither could hit a shot until [[Sam Perkins]] hit a 3-pointer (the potential game winner) with 14 seconds left to give the Lakers a 92–91 lead.  Jordan's 17-foot jumper then rattled out and was rebounded by [[Byron Scott]], who was fouled with 2.7 seconds remaining. Scott missed the first of his two free throws and made the second for a 93–91 Lakers lead. The Bulls, out of timeouts, had a final chance, but a midcourt heave from Pippen at the buzzer caromed off the back of the rim.<ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VNmzbX79dic |title=✮ 1991 NBA Finals {{!}} Game 1 {{!}} Chicago Bulls vs Los Angeles Lakers {{!}} Full Game Replay |date=2024-07-06 |last=NBA Archive |access-date=2026-06-22 |via=YouTube}}</ref>

This was also the last time any game of the NBA Finals aired in the afternoon ([[Eastern Time Zone]]), with every game of the NBA Finals since this game in 1991 airing in prime time, including all weekend games. To date, this is the most recent time any finals game in any of the four [[Major professional sports leagues in the United States and Canada|major American sports leagues]] started before 6:00&nbsp;p.m. Eastern.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Nidetz |first=Steve |date=1991-07-19 |title=NBA FINALS STICK WITH PRIME TIME |url=https://www.chicagotribune.com/1991/07/19/nba-finals-stick-with-prime-time/ |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=Chicago Tribune |language=en-US}}</ref>

The biggest decision of the game was putting Scottie Pippen on Magic Johnson, while Michael Jordan guarded Vlade Divac and also switched to the point guard position for the rest of the series.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Scottie Pippen's Greatest Moments: World champions for the first time in 1991 {{!}} Chicago Bulls |url=https://www.nba.com/bulls/history/pippen10_1991.html |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=www.nba.com}}</ref> For the Bulls, the hero was Horace Grant who led the Bulls with 14 first half points, while Jordan only had 2 points for the first 20 minutes; however, for Jordan, this shooting drought would only prove to be temporary and Jordan would hit his next 13 shots in compensation, capped by the signature moment of the series came when Jordan accelerated towards the basket, raised the ball in his right hand for a dunk, then, to avoid a potential Sam Perkins block, switched the ball to his left and banked in a layup that caused a booming standing ovation at Chicago Stadium. This would go on to be one of the most memorable plays in NBA Finals history.<ref>{{cite web |title=This Date in NBA History: Michael Jordan Switches Hands in Mid-Air vs. LAL in Game 2 of the 1991 NBA Finals {{!}} NBA.com |url=https://www.nba.com/watch/video/this-date-in-nba-history-michael-jordan-switches-hands-in-mid-air-vs-lal-in-game-2-of-the-1991-nba-finals |website=NBA |access-date=8 June 2025 |language=en}}</ref> The Lakers were in the game even when Chicago was leading 58–51 until Byron Scott fouled Pippen, and the Bulls would make 17 of their next 20 field goals in the third quarter despite Jordan spending time on the bench with foul trouble. By the time Jordan returned, their lead was 16 and then Jordan led the Bulls to an 11–0 run to a Bulls victory.<ref>{{Cite web |title=1991 NBA Finals Game 2: Lakers vs Bulls, June 5, 1991 |url=https://www.basketball-reference.com/boxscores/199106050CHI.html |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=Basketball-Reference.com}}</ref>

The Bulls were having trouble in the frontcourt (in Game 1, the Lakers frontcourt outscored Chicago's 60–31). In Game 3, the Lakers would be the ones having trouble, setting a rebounding low in the Finals. Despite this problem, the Lakers went for an 18–2 run that brought them from 3 down (49–52) to 13 up (67–54). The Bulls would answer with a 20–7 run that would tie the game in the 4th quarter at 74. Horace Grant's layup gave the Bulls a 90–87 lead with 1:07 to play.  Perkins then scored to cut the Bulls' lead to 90–89 with 49 seconds left.  After a Bulls' miss, Divac scored on a layup and was fouled.  He made the free throw to give the Lakers a 92–90 lead with 10.9 seconds remaining. Jordan made a 14-foot jump shot just inches away from Divac's fingertips with 3.4 seconds left to tie the game at 92 and force overtime. In overtime, Jordan would score half of the Bulls' 12 points to win the game.<ref>{{Cite web |title=1991 NBA Finals Game 3: Bulls vs Lakers, June 7, 1991 |url=https://www.basketball-reference.com/boxscores/199106070LAL.html |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=Basketball-Reference.com |language=en}}</ref>

[[File:1991 NBA Finals - Game 4 - Chicago Bulls at Los Angeles Lakers 1991-06-09 (ticket).JPG|thumb|right|280px|A ticket for Game 4 of the 1991 NBA Finals at the Great Western Forum.]]
The Lakers had a 28–27 lead in the first quarter; only the second time in the 1991 playoffs that a team led the Bulls at the end of the first quarter. This did not matter as Chicago went on a 19–9 run to start the second period and gain a 46–37 lead. While Michael Jordan scored 11 points in the second quarter, the Lakers only made 12 of their 41 shots in the second and third quarters. The Lakers faced a huge blow as Worthy and Scott left the game (and eventually the series) with ankle and shoulder injuries, respectively (Worthy's ankle injury from the Conference finals against Portland had flared up, and Scott injured his shoulder after slipping on a wet spot on the floor). With the lack of Worthy and Scott, and bad shooting (especially from Sam Perkins), the Bulls were able to take a 16-point lead in the third quarter. The Lakers refused to go down and shrank the deficit to 7 points in the fourth quarter, but it was the closest they would get, as Pippen and Jordan led the Bulls on a 19–8 run to put them one game away from their first NBA Championship.<ref>{{Cite web |title=1991 NBA Finals Game 4: Bulls vs Lakers, June 9, 1991 |url=https://www.basketball-reference.com/boxscores/199106090LAL.html |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=Basketball-Reference.com}}</ref>

The Lakers were facing elimination, and the absences of James Worthy and Byron Scott increased the difficulty for the Lakers. This would not stop Magic Johnson who added 20 assists and 11 rebounds. Jordan guarded Magic Johnson in Game 5 which led Magic to struggle by scoring 16 points on 33% (4/12) shooting with 6 turnovers.<ref>{{cite web|author=Tremendous Upside|url=https://youtube.com/watch?v=S-drsM6ULVk|title=Lakers x Bulls 1991 Finals Game 5|publisher=[[YouTube]]|date=January 18, 2019|accessdate=November 25, 2021}}</ref> Elden Campbell outscored Michael Jordan with 13 points in the first half. The Lakers still fought and even led 93–90 in the fourth quarter, but the Bulls went on a 9–0 run, and Paxson's 10 points in the final half of the fourth quarter helped secure the Chicago Bulls', and Jordan's, first NBA title. It was the only one of the Bulls' six Finals appearances to end in four games to one. This was also the last [[NBA Finals]] game to be played in [[The Forum (Inglewood, California)|The Forum]] (by the time the Lakers returned to the [[2000 NBA Finals|NBA Finals]], they had moved to the new [[Staples Center]]).

Scottie Pippen led the Bulls with 32 points and collected 13 rebounds. Jordan, who was named the Finals MVP, added 30 points and 10 assists.<ref>{{Cite web |title=1991 NBA Finals Game 5: Bulls vs Lakers, June 12, 1991 |url=https://www.basketball-reference.com/boxscores/199106120LAL.html |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=Basketball-Reference.com}}</ref>

==Media coverage==
This was the first Finals to be telecast in the United States by [[NBA on NBC|NBC]]. [[NBC Sports]] used [[Marv Albert]] on play-by-play and [[Mike Fratello]] as color analyst. [[Ahmad Rashad]] (for the [[Chicago Bulls|Bulls]]) and [[Steve Jones (basketball)|Steve "Snapper" Jones]] (for the [[Los Angeles Lakers|Lakers]]) served as sideline reporters.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Silverstein |first=Jack M. |date=2021-06-11 |title=The 1991 NBA Finals Were David Stern's Godsend |url=https://readjack.substack.com/p/the-1991-nba-finals-were-david-sterns |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=A Shot On Ehlo}}</ref>

This was the only NBA Finals series in which recently departed Lakers coach [[Pat Riley]] worked as a broadcaster; he was paired with [[Bob Costas]] in the pre-game, half-time and post-game presentations.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Stewart |first=Larry |date=1991-06-07 |title=NBC Will Improve if Riley Gets Costas Out of His Hair |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1991-06-07-sp-83-story.html |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=Los Angeles Times}}</ref> Riley was then hired as head coach by the [[New York Knicks]] in the 1991 offseason and would eventually win another title with the [[Miami Heat]] in [[2006 NBA Finals|2006]].

In Chicago, this was [[Jim Durham]]'s only Finals appearance as the Bulls' radio announcer (he also announced Bulls games on television via simulcast during the regular season and playoffs). He would later announce several NBA Finals games on [[NBA on ESPN Radio|ESPN Radio]]. [[Neil Funk]] succeeded Durham [[1991-92 Chicago Bulls season|the following season]] and was the radio voice on five NBA Finals involving the Bulls ([[1992 NBA Finals|1992]], [[1993 NBA Finals|1993]], [[1996 NBA Finals|1996]], [[1997 NBA Finals|1997]] and [[1998 NBA Finals|1998]]). The simulcasts would also end after the season, with [[Wayne Larrivee]] (on [[WGN-TV]]) and [[Tom Dore]] (on cable) becoming the Bulls' television announcers the next season; by the [[2008–09 NBA season]] Funk was calling television play-by-play on both networks while [[Chuck Swirsky]] called radio play-by-play. This was also the only NBA Finals to feature [[Johnny Kerr]] on color commentary, as he would become exclusive to television after the Finals.

==Aftermath==
[[File:President George H. W. Bush welcomes members of the NBA Champion Chicago Bulls, including Craig Hodges, to the White House.jpg|thumb|290x290px|President [[George H. W. Bush]] welcomes members of the 1991 NBA Champion Chicago Bulls, including [[Craig Hodges]], to the [[White House]].]]
During the Bulls [[White House]] visit in 1991, guard [[Craig Hodges]] wore a [[dashiki]] and delivered a letter to [[George H. W. Bush|President George H.W. Bush]] criticizing his administration's treatment of minorities and the poor. Hodges believed this act of activism, along with his outspokenness, led to him being blackballed from the NBA shortly after in 1992.<ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HevEcY5pgtU |title=Why Craig Hodges Was Blackballed From The NBA |date=2025-05-02 |last=ALL THE SMOKE |access-date=2026-02-18 |via=YouTube}}</ref>

The Bulls’ victory in the 1991 Finals started a dynasty that lasted until [[1998 NBA Finals|1998]]. They continued their dominance over the NBA [[1992 NBA Finals|the next year]], as they knocked off the [[Portland Trail Blazers]] in six games to repeat as champions.<ref>{{Cite web |title=1991-92 Chicago Bulls Roster and Stats |url=https://www.basketball-reference.com/teams/CHI/1992.html |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=Basketball-Reference.com}}</ref>

The Lakers' [[Showtime (basketball)|Showtime]] era would end on November 7, 1991, as [[Magic Johnson]] announced his retirement due contracting the [[HIV virus]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Nestler |first=Stefan |date=November 8, 2024 |title=Magic Johnson changed how HIV and AIDS are perceived |url=https://www.dw.com/en/magic-johnsons-1991-press-conference-changed-how-hiv-and-aids-are-perceived/a-70906185 |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=dw.com |language=en}}</ref> He did, however, play in the [[1992 NBA All-Star Game]] and eventually with the [[1992 United States men's Olympic basketball team|Dream Team]] in the [[1992 Summer Olympics]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Paying homage to greatest basketball ever assembled: The Dream Team |url=https://www.nba.com/news/morning-tip-remembering-dream-team-michael-jordan-magic-johnson-larry-bird-1992 |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=NBA.com |language=en}}</ref> The Lakers would decline in the years following Magic's retirement, being eliminated in the first round of the playoffs two straight years ([[1992 NBA Playoffs|1992]] – [[1993 NBA Playoffs|1993]]) before missing the playoffs entirely in [[1993-94 NBA season|1994]]. The Lakers would eventually return to the Finals in [[2000 NBA Finals|2000]], knocking off the [[Reggie Miller]]-led [[Indiana Pacers]] in six games, which marked the start of a new dynasty for the franchise led by stars [[Shaquille O'Neal]] and [[Kobe Bryant]].

Many of the Showtime era would stick around in the organizations after their playing careers were done. Magic Johnson coached the Lakers briefly in 1994 and ran the team as president of basketball operations from 2017 to 2019; [[Byron Scott]] coached many teams in the NBA, but coached the Lakers from 2014 to 2016; [[Larry Drew]] coached the team as an assistant from 1992 to 1999; [[James Worthy]] has been a studio analysts for the Lakers since 1998 over the various networks the team has been on over the years; and [[Mychal Thompson]] has been the radio [[color commentator]] for the team since 2003. [[Norm Nixon]] and [[Kurt Rambis]], players not on the 1991 Finals team but recognizable from their Showtime era days, also took jobs in and around the organization. Nixon was studio color analyst for all Lakers home games for [[Fox Sports West]] for a number of years. Rambis had a short-lived stint as head coach in [[1998–99 Los Angeles Lakers season|1999]], was an assistant coach throughout multiple stints over the next 15 years, and senior basketball adviser in the front office since 2018.

[[Phil Jackson]] would later coach to Lakers from 1999 to 2004 and 2005 to 2011 after the Bulls dynasty ran its course in [[1998 NBA Finals|1998]], leading the Lakers to five championships over the course of those two stints.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Most games coached: Los Angeles Lakers |url=https://www.nba.com/news/most-games-coached-los-angeles-lakers |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=NBA.com}}</ref>
