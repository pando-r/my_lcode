The '''1997 NBA Finals''' was the [[NBA Finals|championship series]] of the [[National Basketball Association]]'s (NBA) [[1996–97 NBA season|1996–97 season]] and conclusion of the [[1997 NBA playoffs|season's playoffs]]. The [[Western Conference (NBA)|Western Conference]] champion [[1996–97 Utah Jazz season|Utah Jazz]] took on the [[1996 NBA Finals|defending NBA champion]] and [[Eastern Conference (NBA)|Eastern Conference]] champion [[1996–97 Chicago Bulls season|Chicago Bulls]] for the title, with the Bulls holding [[home team|home court advantage]]. The series was played under a [[Playoff format#Best-of-seven playoff|best-of-seven format]], with the first 2 games in [[Chicago]], the next 3 games in [[Salt Lake City]], and the last 2 games in Chicago.

The Bulls won the series 4 games to 2. For the fifth time in as many Finals appearances, [[Michael Jordan]] was named [[Bill Russell NBA Finals Most Valuable Player Award|NBA Finals MVP]].

The Bulls and Jazz won a combined 133 regular season games, the second most in Finals history behind [[1996 NBA Finals|the previous year's]] 136. Until [[2016 NBA Finals|2016]], the 1997 NBA Finals was the last to feature teams that won a total of at least 130 regular season games.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Hauser |first=Jeff |date=2026-05-18 |title=10,170 days: Spurs vs. Thunder Western Conference Finals is a generational NBA Playoff series |url=https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/10-170-days-spurs-vs-141107997.html |access-date=2026-05-19 |website=YahooSports.com}}</ref>

==Background==

===Chicago Bulls===
{{main|1996–97 Chicago Bulls season}}
For the Chicago Bulls, the campaign was almost identical to their record-breaking 1995–96 season. They began the season 12–0, and by the [[1997 NBA All-Star Game|All-Star break]], were 42–6, putting them on pace to win 70 games for a second year in a row. But some late-season injuries and poor play, including [[Scottie Pippen]]'s failed attempt at a game-winning three-pointer in the Bulls' final regular season game against the [[New York Knicks]], and the Bulls settled for a league-best 69–13 record.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2026-06-17 |title=Chicago Bulls 1997 Scores, Stats, Schedule, Standings |url=https://www.statmuse.com/nba/team/chicago-bulls-25/1997 |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=StatMuse |language=en}}</ref> This tied the [[1971–72 Los Angeles Lakers season|1971–72 Los Angeles Lakers]] for the second-best regular season, only behind the [[1995–96 Chicago Bulls season|1995–96 Bulls]] (72–10) at the time. 19 years later, the [[2015–16 Golden State Warriors season|2015–16 Golden State Warriors]] (73–9) would break the wins record.<ref>{{Cite web |last=@warriors |first=Golden State Warriors |title=73 wins. Iconic. 10 years ago today, the Warriors made history ⚡️ |url=https://www.instagram.com/p/DXE3rC6laUi/ |website=[[Instagram]]}}</ref>

In the playoffs, the Bulls swept the [[1996–97 Washington Bullets season|Washington Bullets]] in the first round, dispatched the [[1996–97 Atlanta Hawks season|Atlanta Hawks]] in five games in the second round, then defeated the [[1996–97 Miami Heat season|Miami Heat]] in five games in the [[NBA Conference finals#Conference|Eastern Conference finals]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Bulls vs Heat, 4-1 - 1997 Eastern Conference Finals |url=https://www.landofbasketball.com/playoffs_series/1997_bulls_vs_heat.htm |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240802022731/https://www.landofbasketball.com/playoffs_series/1997_bulls_vs_heat.htm |archive-date=2024-08-02 |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=www.landofbasketball.com}}</ref>

===Utah Jazz===
{{main|1996–97 Utah Jazz season}}
The Jazz emerged as a force in the Western Conference as far back as the mid-1980s, powered by its All-Star duo of [[point guard]] [[John Stockton]] and [[power forward (basketball)|power forward]] [[Karl Malone]]. They advanced to the Western Conference finals three times between [[1992 NBA playoffs|1992]] and [[1996 NBA playoffs|1996]] (1992, 1994 and 1996), but lost on each occasion.

However, a different story was written for the 1996–97 season. Powered by [[NBA Most Valuable Player Award|league MVP]] Karl Malone, along with the talented John Stockton, the Jazz finally asserted themselves atop the Western Conference, finishing with a franchise-record 64 wins.<ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8rlpm5g-0S8 |title=1997-98 Utah Jazz Season Summary |date=2024-03-01 |last=Utah Jazz History |access-date=2026-06-22 |via=YouTube}}</ref> Also notable on the season was Utah's complete rebrand, as they replaced their classic "J-Note" logo and Mardi Gras colors with a bold, gradient "Purple Mountain" motif.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Miller |first=Ryan |date=June 12, 2024 |title='No one knew this would be popular': Original designer reflects on legacy of Jazz rebrand |url=https://www.ksl.com/article/sports/utah-jazz/no-one-knew-this-would-be-popular-original-designer-reflects-on-legacy-of-jazz-rebrand/51041140 |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=www.ksl.com |language=en}}</ref>

In the playoffs, Utah swept the [[1996–97 Los Angeles Clippers season|Los Angeles Clippers]] in the opening round and eliminated the [[1996–97 Los Angeles Lakers season|Los Angeles Lakers]] in five games in the second round. Utah then eliminated the [[1996–97 Houston Rockets season|Houston Rockets]] to advance to their first NBA Finals in franchise history, thanks to John Stockton's buzzer-beating, three-point shot in Game 6 of the Western Conference finals.<ref>{{Cite web |title=On this date: John Stockton's buzzer-beating 3 sends Jazz to 1997 Finals |url=https://www.nba.com/watch/video/2020/05/28/20200528-1997-western-conference-finals-game-6 |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=NBA.com}}</ref>

Another storyline heading into the Finals was Jazz head coach [[Jerry Sloan]] coaching against his former team as a player. Sloan made the All-Star game twice with the Bulls and was a key player on [[Dick Motta]]’s defensive-minded teams throughout the 1960s and 1970s.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Time has come for Dick Motta to make basketball's hall of fame {{!}} Dallas Mavericks |url=https://mavs.com/mavs/news/time-has-come-for-dick-motta-to-make-basketballs-hall-of-fame |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=mavs.com}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Jerry Sloan {{!}} Guard {{!}} Chicago Bulls |url=https://www.nba.com/player/78173/jerry-sloan |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=NBA.com}}</ref>

The Finals were played using a 2–3–2 site format, where the first two and last two games are held at the team with home court advantage's (Chicago's) home court ([[United Center]]).

Had the Western Conference finals between the Jazz and the [[Houston Rockets]] reached a game 7, the Finals would have started on Wednesday, June 4 and followed the similar Wednesday-Friday-Sunday rotation.

Despite injuring his foot in the Eastern Conference finals against Miami, [[Scottie Pippen]] helped the Bulls to an 84–82 victory over Utah on Sunday. He scored 27 points while Jordan scored 31. The Bulls trailed by one in the 4th, yet were able to grab an 81–79 lead after Pippen blocked Antoine Carr, then made his third 3-pointer with 1:11 remaining. However, John Stockton answered with a 3 of his own with 51.7 seconds left to give Utah an 82–81 lead. Michael Jordan made 1 of 2 free throws with 35.8 seconds left to tie it at 82. Then, Karl Malone was fouled by Rodman with 9.2 seconds left and had a chance to give Utah the lead. Scottie famously psyched him out, saying, "Just remember, the mailman doesn't deliver on Sundays, Karl", before he stepped up to the line. He missed them both. Jordan got the rebound and quickly called a [[Time-out (sport)|time-out]] with 7.5 seconds left. With the game on the line, the Bulls put the ball in Jordan's hands. He dribbled out most of the waning seconds, then launched a 20-footer (game-winning shot) that went in at the buzzer to give Chicago a 1–0 series lead, after which he pumped his fist in triumph. The fist-pumping often draws comparisons to another famous Jordan reaction to a [[Buzzer beater|buzzer-beater]], when he leaped into the air after hitting [[The Shot (Bulls–Cavaliers)|The Shot]] that eliminated the [[Cleveland Cavaliers]] in Game 5 of the 1989 Eastern Conference quarterfinals.

Jordan joined [[Cliff Hagan]] in Game 6 in [[1957 NBA Finals|1957]], [[Jerry West]] in Game 3 in [[1962 NBA Finals|1962]], [[Larry Wright (basketball)|Larry Wright]] in Game 1 in [[1979 NBA Finals|1979]], and [[Dennis Johnson]] in Game 4 in [[1985 NBA Finals|1985]] as the only NBA Finals buzzer-beaters. To date, there has not been another buzzer-beating shot to win a Finals game.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Merron |first=Jeff |title=Best NBA playoff buzzer-beaters |url=http://www.espn.com/espn/page2/story?page=list/nba_buzzerbeaters |access-date=2026-06-12 |website=espn.com}}</ref>

The Bulls simply dominated Game 2. After a hard-fought first quarter, the Bulls took control of the game with a 12–0 second-quarter run. Utah's 31 points in the first half was only one point above an NBA low. Karl Malone, who missed those two key free throws in Game 1, had another bad night, making only 6 of 20 field goals. Dennis Rodman nailed a late 3-pointer to put the Bulls up 97–85.

Utah's fans welcomed their proud Western Conference champs with force. During the introductions of the Jazz's starting lineups, the Bulls players plugged their ears, due to the loud cheers and fireworks within the Delta Center. The Chicago Bulls started off the first three quarters with mediocre play, despite Scottie Pippen tying a then-Finals record with seven 3-pointers. Utah was led by Karl Malone, who scored 37 points and had 10 rebounds. Chicago attempted a 4th-quarter comeback, cutting a 24-point deficit down to 7, but Utah ultimately won its first game of the series. With the Bulls trailing by 18 points in the second quarter, Michael Jordan threw down an [[Alley-oop (basketball)|alley-oop]] slam which drew loud boos from the crowd. Dennis Rodman defended his poor performance in Game 3 with a vulgar remark denigrating [[Mormons]] and was fined a then record $50,000 for this remark and [[CKE Restaurants]] dropped his television commercials permanently. He apologized, claiming he was not aware that Mormon referred to a religion and intended his remark at obnoxious Jazz fans.<ref name=pfo>{{cite news|issn=0021-5996|volume=92|issue=6|magazine=Jet|publisher=Johnson Publishing Co.|title=Rodman fined $50,000 for remarks about Mormons|author=Jet Magazine staff|date=June 30, 1997|access-date=October 2, 2019|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=JsMDAAAAMBAJ&q=%22Rodman%20fined%20%2450%2C000%20for%20remarks%20about%20Mormons%22&pg=PA61|page=61|via=Google.com}}</ref><ref name=nytrodman>{{cite news|last=Wise|first=Mike|title=N.B.A. Fines Rodman $50,000 for Remarks on Mormons|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1997/06/13/sports/nba-fines-rodman-50000-for-remarks-on-mormons.html|newspaper=[[The New York Times]]|date=June 13, 1997|access-date=December 16, 2009}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|last=Howard-Cooper|first=Scott|title=Rodman Gets Himself in Another Fine Mess|date=June 13, 1997|newspaper=Los Angeles Times|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1997-06-13-sp-2874-story.html|access-date=October 2, 2019}}</ref>

Due to the extremely loud Jazz fans in Game 3, Bulls coach Phil Jackson wore a pair of ear plugs. A tight game with many lead changes throughout, the Jazz led by five after the first quarter, but trailed by five at halftime. The score was tied going into the fourth quarter. Late in the game, Michael Jordan made a fast break dunk to give the Bulls a 71–66 lead, but John Stockton buried a momentum-shifting three-pointer at the top of the key to cut the deficit to 71–69. Jordan made a jumper to give the Bulls a 73–69 lead with 2:01 remaining, but the Bulls would not score again. The Jazz pulled to within one when Stockton stole the ball from Jordan and made two free throws at the other end. On the next possession, Stockton grabbed a rebound from a Jordan miss and threw a full-court pass to Karl Malone for a layup with 44.5 seconds left that put Utah in front for good, 74–73. After Stockton made the assist, he jumped up into the air several times pumping his fist. After some Bulls misses, Karl Malone made two free throws with 17 seconds left to put the Jazz up by three. On the next possession, Michael Jordan's potential game-tying three-pointer with less than 10 seconds left rattled out; Stockton grabbed the rebound and threw another full-court pass to Bryon Russell, who escaped the intentional foul and dunked with five seconds left in the game to seal it, drawing a huge roar from the crowd. The Jazz's 12–2 run tied the series at two games apiece. The Delta Center grew so loud that during the final moments of the game when Bryon Russell made the dunk that iced the game, [[Marv Albert]] was unable to be clearly heard. The 78–73 score was one of the lowest scores in NBA Finals history. Jackson later wrote that the team's equipment manager "had mistakenly served our players Gaterlode, a high-carbohydrate drink, instead of [[Gatorade]]," making the team "sluggish... Each of the players, it was estimated, had ingested the equivalent of about twenty baked potatoes."<ref>{{cite book |last1=Jackson |first1=Phil |last2=Delehanty |first2=Hugh |title=Eleven rings : the soul of success |date=2014 |publisher=Penguin Press |isbn=9780143125341 |page=Chapter 12}}</ref>

Game 5, known as "'''The Flu Game'''", was one of Michael Jordan's most memorable games. At 2:30&nbsp;a.m. on Tuesday morning, Jordan woke up and began to experience [[vomiting]] and [[diarrhea]], and called his personal trainer to his hotel room, where he was found lying curled up in the [[fetal position]], shaking, and sweating profusely. He hardly had the strength to sit up in bed and requested a doctor. He was diagnosed with a stomach virus or [[Foodborne illness|food poisoning]], likely caused by a pizza ordered the night before. Jordan eventually claimed it was food poisoning in the 2020 docuseries [[The Last Dance (TV series)|''The Last Dance'']].<ref>{{cite news|last=Grover|first=Tim|title=Trainer: MJ had food poisoning|url=https://www.espn.com/chicago/nba/story/_/id/9183990/michael-jordan-flu-game-was-really-food-poisoning-trainer-says|publisher=ESPN|access-date=April 23, 2013}}</ref> The Bulls' athletic trainers told Jordan that there was no way he could play the next day, but Jordan insisted that he play. The Jazz were a perfect 10–0 at home in the postseason up until that point, and a third consecutive win would give them the series lead. The Bulls needed Jordan to assist them, as it was a critical game, and despite his sickness, Jordan got out of bed at 5:50&nbsp;p.m. on Wednesday, just in time for the 7 o'clock tip-off at the [[Delta Center]].<ref name="ESPN flu">{{cite news|last=Weinberg |first=Rick |title=79: Jordan battles flu, makes Jazz sick |url=https://www.espn.com/espn/espn25/story?page=moments/79 |publisher=ESPN |year=2004 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070921024606/http://sports.espn.go.com/espn/espn25/story?page=moments%2F79 |archive-date=September 21, 2007 }}</ref>

Jordan was weak as he stepped on the court for Game 5, and missed team warmups. At first, he displayed little energy, and [[John Stockton]], along with reigning MVP [[Karl Malone]], led the Jazz to a 16-point lead (36–20) in the second quarter. But Jordan slowly began to make shots despite lacking his usual speed. He scored 17 points in the quarter as the Bulls ended the half with a large run cutting the Jazz lead to four (53–49). While Jordan was fatigued in the third and sitting on the bench, Utah was able to reclaim the lead and stretched it to 8 points (77–69). Jordan shot well again in the fourth quarter, scoring 15 points. With 46.4 seconds left and Chicago down 85–84, he was fouled and went to the free throw line. He made the first to tie the game, but missed the second. Toni Kukoč got the offensive rebound to Jordan, who dribbled back to allow the offense to set up. He passed the ball to Pippen, who was quickly double-teamed. Pippen then passed the ball back to a now-unguarded Jordan, who made a 3-point shot to give the Bulls an 88–85 lead with 25 seconds remaining in the game. A Greg Ostertag dunk brought the Jazz back within one point, but Luc Longley answered with a dunk of his own, and Chicago held on for a victory when John Stockton missed the first of two free throws in front of the stunned crowd. With only a few seconds remaining and the game's result safely in Chicago's favor, Jordan collapsed into Scottie Pippen's arms, creating an iconic image that has come to symbolize The Flu Game.<ref name="ESPN flu"/>

Malone was the high-scorer for the Jazz with 19 points but shot poorly during the game, air-balling an off-balance shot on the possession prior to Jordan's 3-pointer. Malone finished the second half 1-for-6 from the field. Jordan played 44 minutes, finishing the game with 38 points, 7 rebounds, 5 assists, 3 steals and 1 block.

Michael Jordan hadn't fully recovered from his illness, but he was feeling much better and led the Bulls with 39 points. Chicago struggled in the first half, scoring just 37 points and making only 9 of 27 field goals. With the Bulls struggling in the third quarter, Jordan dunked after a steal, bringing the crowd to its feet and Jud Buechler buried a 3 to help give the Bulls the momentum. The Bulls trailed by 9 early in the fourth quarter, but went on a 10–0 run to take their first lead since the opening minutes when Steve Kerr hit a 3-pointer, but the Jazz regained the lead and the game remained one possession until the final score. In the final minutes, Jordan's fadeaway jumper extended the Bulls lead to 3, before Bryon Russell hit a 3-pointer with 1:44 left to tie the game at 86. The two teams failed to score on their next possessions. Shandon Anderson then missed a reverse layup. The Jazz argued that this was due to Pippen grabbing the rim before the shot, causing the basketball standard to shake. However, the officials ruled that the ball had no chance of going in. Dennis Rodman grabbed the rebound and called time-out with 28 seconds left in a tie game. The Jazz expected Jordan to take the final shot. Instead, Jordan drew a double-team, then passed to a wide-open Steve Kerr, who hit a 17-footer with 5 seconds left to send the United Center into a frenzy.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Steve Kerr's clutch jumper clinches Bulls' 1997 title |url=https://www.nba.com/watch/video/this-date-in-history-steve-kerr-seals-bulls-championship |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=NBA.com}}</ref> The Jazz looked for a final shot to stay alive, but Scottie Pippen made a significant defensive play as he knocked away Bryon Russell's inbound pass intended for Shandon Anderson and rolled the ball over to Toni Kukoč, who dunked the final 2 points of the game before the roaring crowd to bring the Finals to an end, despite there being 0.6 seconds left in the game. Jordan finished with 39 points to go along with 11 rebounds and four assists, and he was named Finals MVP for the 5th time.<ref>{{Cite web |last=@ESPNInsights |first=ESPN Insights |date=June 13, 2020 |title=On this date in 1997, the Chicago Bulls won their 5th NBA title in 7 seasons with a 90-86 win over the Utah Jazz in Game 6 of the '97 NBA Finals. Michael Jordan had 39 pts & 11 rebs in the win and earned his 5th NBA Finals MVP award. |url=https://x.com/ESPNInsights/status/1271804517462532097?lang=en |website=[[X, formerly known as Twitter]]}}</ref>

This would also be the last United Center championship celebration until eighteen years later when the [[NHL]]'s [[Chicago Blackhawks]] beat the [[Tampa Bay Lightning]] to win the [[2015 Stanley Cup Finals|2015 Stanley Cup]].

==Broadcasting==
[[Marv Albert]] was the play-by-play announcer for his seventh straight NBA Finals for the ''[[NBA on NBC]]''. It was his third straight NBA Finals working with color analysts [[Matt Guokas]] and [[Bill Walton]]. This would be the last NBA Finals that Albert would announce in the 1990s because of a [[Marv Albert#Sexual assault|sex scandal]] that would force [[NBC Sports]] to fire him later in 1997.

During these NBA Finals, [[NBC Sports]]' [[Hannah Storm]] became the first woman to serve as pre-game host of an NBA Finals. She would continue in this role for the next three [[NBA Finals]].

[[NBA on NBC|NBC Sports]] also used [[Ahmad Rashad]] ([[Chicago Bulls|Bulls]] sideline) and [[Jim Gray (sportscaster)|Jim Gray]] ([[Utah Jazz|Jazz]] sideline) as the sideline reporters.

This would be the only NBA Finals on NBC during the 1990s that would not include [[Bob Costas]] in any capacity. He had served as the pre-game host from 1991 to 1996. The next year, during the [[1998 NBA Finals]], Costas served as the play-by-play announcer, a role in which he would continue until after the [[2000 NBA Finals]].

The local NBC affiliates of the participating teams included [[WMAQ-TV]] (Chicago) and [[KSL-TV]] (Salt Lake City).

==Aftermath==

{{Further|1998 NBA Finals}}
Both teams met again in the Finals in [[1998 NBA Finals|1998]], the first time the same two teams met in the NBA Finals since [[1989 NBA Finals|1989]], when the Lakers and Pistons went up against each other. Only this time, the Jazz had home-court advantage. The Bulls won that series in 6 games, highlighted by [[Michael Jordan]]'s last shot as a Bull in [[Game 6 of the 1998 NBA Finals|Game 6]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=September 14, 2021 |title=Top NBA Finals moments: Michael Jordan's jumper seals 1998 title |url=https://www.nba.com/news/history-finals-moments-michael-jordan-last-shot-1998 |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=NBA.com |language=en}}</ref>{{Quote box|width=25em|bgcolor=|align=right|quote="I just kind of whispered in his ear that the Mailman doesn't deliver on Sunday"<ref>{{cite web |last1=Fleming |first1=Dave |title=Before 'The Last Dance,' Scottie Pippen delivered six words of trash talk that changed NBA history
 |url=https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/29166548/before-last-dance-scottie-pippen-delivered-six-words-trash-talk-changed-nba-history |website=ESPN |access-date=26 March 2025 |language=en}}</ref>|source= — [[Scottie Pippen]] telling reporters after the game what he said to [[Karl Malone]] (nicknamed the Mailman) before crucial free throws in Game 1.}}

Nationally, [[Karl Malone]], the [[NBA Most Valuable Player|MVP]] of the league in the regular season, was criticized heavily for a subpar NBA Finals.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Roberts |first=Selena |title=He Is the M.V.P., but Malone Feels Low |url=http://www.nytimes.com/1997/06/15/sports/he-is-the-mvp-but-malone-feels-low.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171228193041/http://www.nytimes.com/1997/06/15/sports/he-is-the-mvp-but-malone-feels-low.html |archive-date=2017-12-28 |access-date=2025-12-20 |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=1997-06-06 |title=Old losing habits return to haunt Jazz MVP Malone takes heat as NBA Finals resemble Utah's past playoff failures |url=https://www.baltimoresun.com/1997/06/06/old-losing-habits-return-to-haunt-jazz-mvp-malone-takes-heat-as-nba-finals-resemble-utahs-past-playoff-failures/ |access-date=2025-12-20 |website=Baltimore Sun |language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0mJrrabVs_A |title=Peter Vecsey Tries to Get Punched |date=2011-03-04 |last=yushername |access-date=2025-12-20 |via=YouTube}}</ref> [[Dennis Rodman]] and [[Bison Dele|Brian Williams]] forced Malone into taking more jump shots than he had in any playoff series in his career to that point.<ref>{{cite web |title=Jazz @ Bulls January 1998 (NBA On NBC) |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aLvoeid88MY&t=651s |website=Youtube.com |access-date=30 November 2025 |date=3 November 2025}}</ref> In the 1997 Finals, Malone shot 44% compared to 53% in the regular season and 60% at the free throw line compared to 78%. In Game 1, Malone missed 2 free throws with 9 seconds left, which left Michael Jordan to hit the game winner. In Game 5, he went 7-for-17 from the floor and 5-for-9 at the free throw line for 19 points in a Jazz two-point loss. In Game 6, he went 7-for-15 from both the field and free throw line in what turned out to be a close four-point loss.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Virgil |first1=Villanueva |title="They had to tighten the rim after the Miami series" - Kerr credited the Bulls mascot for Malone's free-throw misses in the 1997 NBA Finals |url=https://sports.yahoo.com/article/had-tighten-rim-miami-series-084400764.html |website=Yahoo Sports |access-date=6 June 2025 |date=4 May 2025}}</ref> Directly after the Bulls' Game 6 clincher, Malone was interviewed by [[NBC Sports|NBC]] analyst [[Peter Vecsey (sports columnist)|Peter Vecsey]], who aggressively pressed Malone about his poor series and asked if he felt like he let his team down. To Malone's credit, he took full blame and stated he did not feel like he let his team down.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Rigdon |first=Jay |date=2020-05-03 |title=Peter Vecsey isn't happy that 'The Last Dance' never called him for an interview |url=https://awfulannouncing.com/nba/peter-vecsey-isnt-happy-that-the-last-dance-never-called-him-for-an-interview.html |access-date=2026-11-15 |website=Awful Announcing}}</ref>

This was the only Bulls' championship Brian Williams was a part of. In 1998, Williams changed his name to Bison Dele to honor his [[Native Americans in the United States|Native American]] (Cherokee) and [[Ethnic groups of Africa|African]] ancestry.<ref name="SI">{{cite news |last=Ballard|first=Chris|title=Lost Soul|url=http://si.com/longform/bison-dele/|access-date=October 25, 2013|newspaper=Sports Illustrated|date=October 21, 2013}}</ref> At the age of 30, he retired after the [[1998–99 NBA season|1998–99 season]], walking away from the remaining five years and US$36.45 million on his contract with the [[Detroit Pistons]] rather than be traded.<ref>{{cite news |agency=The Associated Press|title=Pistons' Dele Retires|url=https://www.cbsnews.com/news/pistons-dele-retires/|work=CBS News|date=October 22, 1999|access-date=September 29, 2007}}</ref> In 2002, Dele, his girlfriend Serena Karlan, and boat captain Bertrand Saldo were killed by his Dele's brother, Miles Dabord, after the two got into an argument aboard Dele's boat near [[Tahiti]] across the [[Pacific Ocean]].<ref>{{Cite news |title=Bison Dele killed by brother: report {{!}} CBC Sports |url=https://www.cbc.ca/sports/basketball/bison-dele-killed-by-brother-report-1.323223 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260112102425/https://www.cbc.ca/sports/basketball/bison-dele-killed-by-brother-report-1.323223 |archive-date=2026-01-12 |access-date=2026-06-05 |work=CBC |language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=October 4, 2002 |title=Reports: Fight ended with three thrown overboard |url=http://www.espn.com/nba/news/2002/1003/1440688.html |access-date=2026-06-05 |website=espn.com}}</ref>
