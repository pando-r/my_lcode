The '''Naismith Basketball Hall of Fame'''  is an American [[history museum]] and [[hall of fame]], located at 1000 Hall of Fame Avenue in [[Springfield, Massachusetts]]. It serves as [[basketball]]'s most complete library, part of its mission of promoting and preserving the [[history of basketball]]. Dedicated to [[Canadian-American]] physician [[James Naismith]], who invented the sport in Springfield, the Hall of Fame inducted its first class in 1959 and opened its first facility on February 17, 1968.<ref name="markovits">{{cite book |last1=Markovits |first1=Andrei |last2=Rensmann |first2=Lars |title=Gaming the World: How Sports Are Reshaping Global Politics and Culture |date=December 2013 |publisher=Princeton University Press |isbn=9780691162034 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=umqYDwAAQBAJ&dq=naismith%20hall%20of%20fame%20%22february%2017%201968%22&pg=PA22 |access-date=January 5, 2022}}</ref>

{{As of|2024}}, the Hall has inducted 436 players, coaches, referees, and other basketball professionals.<ref name=":0">{{cite web|title=The Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame :: Hall Of Famers|url=https://www.hoophall.com/hall-of-famers/browse/?players=true&coaches=true&teams=true&contributors=true&referees=true&yr=2019&country=&loc=&team=&college=&position=&height=|access-date=2020-08-05|website=www.hoophall.com|language=en|archive-date=July 28, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210728194237/https://www.hoophall.com/hall-of-famers/browse/?players=true&coaches=true&teams=true&contributors=true&referees=true&yr=2019&country=&loc=&team=&college=&position=&height=|url-status=dead}}</ref> The [[Boston Celtics]] have the most inductees, with 41.<ref>{{cite news |title=Which NBA team has the most Basketball Hall of Fame members? |url=https://www.nbcsportsbayarea.com/nba/golden-state-warriors/which-nba-team-has-the-most-basketball-hall-of-fame-members/1430426/ |date=September 8, 2022 |access-date=April 13, 2026 |publisher=NBC Sports |archive-date=January 24, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250124060904/https://www.nbcsportsbayarea.com/nba/golden-state-warriors/which-nba-team-has-the-most-basketball-hall-of-fame-members/1430426/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

==History of the Springfield building==
The Naismith Basketball Hall of Fame was established in 1959, without a physical location, by [[Lee Williams (basketball)|Lee Williams]], a former athletic director at [[Colby College]]. In the 1960s, the Hall of Fame struggled to raise enough money to build its first facility and finally opened it on February 17, 1968,<ref name="markovits"/> less than a month after the [[National Basketball Association]] played its [[1968 NBA All-Star Game|18th All-Star Game]]. The Basketball Hall of Fame's board named four inductees in its first year. 

The Hall of Fame also sought to make contributions of its own. In 1979, it sponsored the [[Hall of Fame Tip Off|Tip-Off Classic]], a pre-season college basketball exhibition. The Tip-Off Classic has been the start to the college basketball season ever since, generally returning to Springfield every few years.<ref name=":1">{{cite web|title=The Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame :: History of The Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame|url=http://www.hoophall.com/about/about-hall/history/|access-date=2020-08-05|website=www.hoophall.com|language=en|archive-date=June 20, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180620115046/http://www.hoophall.com/about/about-hall/history|url-status=live}}</ref>

In the 17 years that the Basketball Hall of Fame operated at [[Springfield College]], it drew over 630,000 visitors. Its popularity eventually required a new building, and on June 30, 1985, an $11.5 million facility was opened beside the [[Connecticut River]] in Springfield.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Goldaper |first1=Sam |title=Home of Basketball Memories |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1985/06/30/sports/home-of-basketball-memories.html |access-date=January 5, 2022 |work=The New York Times |date=June 30, 1985 |archive-date=January 5, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220105165414/https://www.nytimes.com/1985/06/30/sports/home-of-basketball-memories.html |url-status=live }}</ref> As the new hall opened, it also recognized women for the first time, with inductees such as [[Senda Berenson Abbott]], who introduced women's basketball at [[Smith College]]. The new facility drew far more visitors than anticipated, due in large part to the increasing popularity of the game but also to the scenic location beside the river and the second Hall's interesting modern architecture.<ref name=":1" />

In 2002, the Hall of Fame moved again<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.thebasketballfans.com/where-is-the-national-basketball-hall-of-fame-located/|title=Where is the national basketball Hall of Fame located? – The Basketball Fans|date=2018-10-16|work=The Basketball Fans|access-date=2018-11-28|language=en-US|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181128170230/https://www.thebasketballfans.com/where-is-the-national-basketball-hall-of-fame-located/|archive-date=2018-11-28|url-status=dead}}</ref>—albeit merely 100 yards south along Springfield's riverfront—into a $47 million facility designed by [[Gwathmey Siegel & Associates Architects|Gwathmey Siegel & Associates]]. The building has a metallic silver, basketball-shaped sphere flanked by two symmetrical [[rhombus]]es. The dome is illuminated at night and features 80,000 square foot (7,400 m<sup>2</sup>), including a Subway sandwich shop and a gift shop. The second Basketball Hall of Fame was converted into an [[LA Fitness]] health club. The Basketball Hall of Fame includes Center Court, a full-sized basketball court on which visitors can play, a game gallery, interactive exhibits, several theaters, and an honor ring of inductees. A large theater for ceremonies seats up to 300. Honorees inducted in 2002 included the [[Harlem Globetrotters]] and [[Magic Johnson]], a five-time NBA champion, three-time NBA finals MVP and Olympic gold medalist.<ref name=":1" />

As of 2011, the Basketball Hall of Fame has greatly exceeded attendance expectations, with basketball fans traveling there from all over the world.<ref>{{cite news | url=https://www.wamc.org/post/riverfront-park-stearns-square-reopen-after-major-renovations |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200322011415/https://www.wamc.org/post/riverfront-park-stearns-square-reopen-after-major-renovations |archive-date=2020-03-22 |url-status=dead | title= Riverfront Park and Stearns Square Reopen After Major Renovations | publisher= Northeast Public Radio}}</ref>

==Criteria for induction==
In contrast to the [[National Soccer Hall of Fame|Soccer]] and the [[National Baseball Hall of Fame and Museum|National Baseball]] Halls of Fame, Springfield honors international and American professionals, as well as American and international amateurs, making it arguably the most comprehensive Hall of Fame among major American sports. From 2011 to 2015, seven committees were, and as of 2016, six committees are employed to both screen and elect candidates. Four of the committees screen prospective candidates:<ref name="2011 class">{{cite press release |url=http://www.hoophall.com/news/2011/2/18/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-announces-12-final.html |title=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame Announces 12 Finalists for 2011 Election |publisher=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame |date=February 18, 2011 |access-date=February 18, 2011 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110222011516/http://www.hoophall.com/news/2011/2/18/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-announces-12-final.html |archive-date=February 22, 2011 }}</ref>
* North American Screening Committee (9 members)
* Women's Screening Committee (7 members)
* International Screening Committee (7 members)
* Veterans Screening Committee (7 members), with "Veterans" defined as individuals whose careers ended at least 35 years before they are considered for election.<ref name=Enshrinement>{{cite web |url=http://www.hoophall.com/enshrinement-process/ |title=Guidelines For Nomination and Election Into the Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame |publisher=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame |access-date=February 18, 2011 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090904105906/http://www.hoophall.com/enshrinement-process/ |archive-date=September 4, 2009 }}</ref>

Since 2011, the Veterans and International Committees also vote to directly induct one candidate for each induction class.<ref name="2013 class">{{cite press release |url=http://www.hoophall.com/news/2013/2/15/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-announces-12-final.html |title=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame Announces 12 Finalists for 2013 Election |publisher=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame |date=February 15, 2013 |access-date=February 25, 2013 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130218082808/http://www.hoophall.com/news/2013/2/15/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-announces-12-final.html |archive-date=February 18, 2013 }}</ref>

Three committees were formed in 2011 to directly elect one candidate for each induction class:<ref name="2013 class"/>
* [[American Basketball Association]] Committee – This committee was permanently disbanded in 2015, because it had fulfilled its purpose over the previous five years.<ref name="Modifications">{{cite web | url=http://www.hoophall.com/news/2015/12/14/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-announces-modifica.html | title=Hall of Fame Announces Modifications to its Enshrinement Process | publisher=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame | date=December 14, 2015 | access-date=December 24, 2015 | url-status=dead | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151225153600/http://www.hoophall.com/news/2015/12/14/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-announces-modifica.html | archive-date=December 25, 2015 }}</ref>
* Contributor Direct Election Committee
** Other committees may choose to elect contributors. For example, the 2014 class included two contributors. 
* Early [[African American|African-American]] Pioneers of the Game Committee

Individuals who receive at least seven votes from the North American Screening Committee or five votes from one of the other screening committees in a given year are eligible to advance to an Honors Committee, composed of 12 members plus rotating groups of 12 specialists (one group for female candidates, one group for international candidates, and one group for American and veterans candidates) who vote on each candidate. Each screening committee has a limited number of candidates it may submit to the Honors Committee—10 from the North American Committee, and two from each of the other committees. Any individual receiving at least 18 affirmative votes (75% of all votes cast) from the Honors Committee is approved for induction into the Hall of Fame. As long as the number of candidates receiving sufficient votes from a screening committee is not greater than the number of finalists that the committee is permitted to submit, advancement to the Honors Committee is generally pro forma, although the Hall's Board of Trustees may remove from consideration any candidate who "has damaged the integrity of the game of basketball".{{dead link|date=June 2017}}<ref name=Enshrinement/>

To be considered for induction by a screening committee, a player, retired coach, or referee must be fully retired from that role for at least three full seasons.<ref name="2018 changes">{{cite press release |url=http://www.hoophall.com/news/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-announces-modifications-to-its-enshrinement-process-beginning-with-the-class-of-2018/ |title=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame Announces Modifications to its Enshrinement Process Beginning with the Class of 2018 |publisher=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame |date=December 19, 2017 |access-date=February 17, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180212075506/http://www.hoophall.com/news/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-announces-modifications-to-its-enshrinement-process-beginning-with-the-class-of-2018/ |archive-date=February 12, 2018 |url-status=dead }}</ref> The waiting period had originally been five years, but was changed to four years in December 2015,<ref name="Nomination">{{cite web | url=http://www.hoophall.com/enshrinement-process/ | title=Guidelines For Nomination and Election | publisher=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame | access-date=December 24, 2015 | url-status=dead | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090904105906/http://www.hoophall.com/enshrinement-process/ | archive-date=September 4, 2009 }}</ref> and to three seasons in December 2017.<ref name="2018 changes"/> Prior to the induction class of 2018, referees had been eligible for induction after 25 years of full-time service, even if still active.<ref name="Nomination"/> Changes to the criteria for consideration of active coaches were also announced as part of the 2017 changes. Coaches become eligible upon 25 years of full-time service at the high school level or above, or three seasons after retirement.<ref name=Nomination/> Effective with the class of 2020, active coaches must meet the years of service requirement and be at least 60 years old.<ref name="2018 changes"/> No years of service criterion is required for those who have made a "significant contribution to the game of basketball". Sportswriters and commentators can be elected as full-fledged members, in contrast to the Baseball Hall of Fame that places them in a separate category from players and other inductees.<ref name=Enshrinement/>

===Criticism===
Aspects of the Hall's voting procedures, including voter anonymity, are controversial. While sportswriter voters of other major sports' Halls of Fame openly debate their choices, the Naismith Hall process is not transparent.<ref>{{cite web|last=Aschburner|first=Steve|title=Hall of Fame selection process leaves much to be desired|website=[[NBA.com]]|url=http://www.nba.com/2010/news/features/steve_aschburner/08/12/hall.process/index.html|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140408215012/http://www.nba.com/2010/news/features/steve_aschburner/08/12/hall.process/index.html |archive-date=2014-04-08|url-status=dead}}</ref> The Hall has also been criticized for a tendency to enshrine active collegiate coaches and relatively obscure players while omitting some accomplished players and coaches.<ref>{{cite web|last=Ziller|first=Tom|title=Fans to Vote for Basketball Hall of Fame Inductees|url=http://nba.fanhouse.com/2010/03/30/fans-to-vote-for-basketball-hall-of-fame-inductees/|publisher=AOL News|access-date=May 11, 2013|date=March 30, 2010|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110212083544/http://nba.fanhouse.com/2010/03/30/fans-to-vote-for-basketball-hall-of-fame-inductees/|archive-date=February 12, 2011}}</ref>

The Hall's selection process has been criticized for excluding certain players, coaches, and innovators. For example, the 24-second shot-clock had two developers for the [[National Basketball Association]] in [[Leo Ferris]] and [[Danny Biasone]], but only the latter has been inducted into the Hall of Fame. Ferris was listed as an eligible nominee from 2017 to 2023.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.nba.com/news/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-announces-eligible-candidates-for-class-of-2022 |title=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame announces eligible candidates for Class of 2022 |work=NBA |accessdate=2022-02-28 |archive-date=February 20, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220220123030/https://www.nba.com/news/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-announces-eligible-candidates-for-class-of-2022 |url-status=live }}</ref> [[Tim Hardaway]] was not inducted into the Hall until 2022, possibly due to his past negative comments on the [[LGBT community]] despite support from peers involving his playing credentials.<ref>{{cite web|last=Aston |first=Ryan |url=https://heavy.com/sports/miami-heat/tim-hardaway-hof-finalist/ |title=Heat Rumors: Hardaway Gets Another HOF Shot |publisher=Heavy.com |date=2022-02-19 |accessdate=2022-02-28}}</ref> Nine of the ten [[List of college men's basketball coaches with 600 wins|NCAA Division I head coaches who won 800 games]] are currently in the Hall of Fame, with [[Cliff Ellis]] (909 wins) being the only exception. [[Leta Andrews]], the all-time winningest coach in the United States (1,416 wins), has been a finalist five times but never inducted.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.star-telegram.com/sports/spt-columns-blogs/mac-engel/article259868355.html |title=Basketball Hall of Fame's ongoing snub of winningest high school coach reeks of sexism |work=Fort Worth Star-Telegram |last=Engel |first=Mac |date=March 28, 2022 |access-date=July 22, 2022}}</ref> Most notably, [[Eddie Sutton]] waited twelve years after his retirement to be inducted.<ref>{{cite web|last=Kolok|first=Jeremy|title=Column: Eddie Sutton's exclusion from Hall of Fame a disgrace to basketball|url=https://www.ocolly.com/sports/column-eddie-suttons-exclusion-from-hall-of-fame-a-disgrace-to-basketball/article_c671170c-4eab-11ea-8642-fbaffe2b3f97.html|access-date=2020-07-22|website=ocolly.com|date=February 13, 2020|language=en|archive-date=July 22, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200722164952/https://www.ocolly.com/sports/column-eddie-suttons-exclusion-from-hall-of-fame-a-disgrace-to-basketball/article_c671170c-4eab-11ea-8642-fbaffe2b3f97.html|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Ken Anderson (basketball)|Ken Anderson]], holder of the third-highest winning percentage for all college basketball coaches in history, has not been inducted.

The Basketball Hall of Fame stands in contrast to its baseball and football counterparts, specifically for its induction and voting process. There are multiple different voting committees for the Basketball Hall: The International Committee, North American Committee, Women's Committee, Contributor Direct Election Committee, Early African-American Pioneers Committee, and Veterans Committee. Each committee makes their own individual selections for their inductees into the Hall. The members of these committees serve three-year terms. How the members get selected, and who they are, is virtually unknown to the public and is kept completely anonymous. Inductees do not know who inducted them, and those who inducted them supposedly never let them know.<ref name=":2">{{cite news |last=Washburn |first=Gary |title=Naismith Basketball Hall of Fame Voting a Secret Society |work=Boston Globe |edition=Online |date=September 5, 2019}}</ref>

According to Mark Purdy, a sportswriter and selection committee member for the Baseball Hall of Fame, the Basketball Hall of Fame's selection process is quite non-exclusive. He argues that the Baseball Hall of Fame first started inducting individuals in 1936, and Basketball did not start inducting players until 1959, but the Basketball Hall as of 2014 had more members than the Baseball Hall.<ref>{{cite news |last=Purdy |first=Mark |title=Purdy: The Non-Exclusive Hall of Fame |work=Oakland Tribune |date=April 8, 2014}}</ref> Naismith Basketball Hall of Fame President and CEO, [[John Doleva]], defends the exclusivity argument: "I'm used to explaining it and it's different than football and baseball," Doleva said. "Because football and baseball cover the men's game professionally, end of story. Naismith invented this game for everyone: men, women, high school, college, pro, coaches, players. We really represent the entire game, so we have a broader class".<ref name=":2" />

* The [[Kareem Abdul-Jabbar Award]], presented since 2015 to the top [[Center (basketball)|center]].<ref>{{cite press release |url=http://www.hoophall.com/news/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-narrows-watch-list-for-2018-kareem-abdul-jabbar-award/ |title=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame Narrows Watch List for 2018 Kareem Abdul-Jabbar Award |publisher=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame |date=February 6, 2018 |access-date=February 18, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191221204111/http://www.hoophall.com/news/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-narrows-watch-list-for-2018-kareem-abdul-jabbar-award/ |archive-date=December 21, 2019 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

Each of the award winners is chosen by a Hall of Fame selection committee, plus the award's namesake.

The Hall, in cooperation with the [[Women's Basketball Coaches Association]], presents analogous awards for the top Division I women's players at each position. One has been awarded since 2000; the others were first presented in 2018.
* The [[Nancy Lieberman Award]] for the top point guard was the Hall's only women's positional award that was presented before 2018, having first been awarded in 2000.<ref>{{cite press release |url=http://www.hoophall.com/news/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-womens-basketball-coaches-association-narrow-watch-list-for-2018-nancy-lieberman-award/ |title=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame, Women's Basketball Coaches Association Narrow Watch List for 2018 Nancy Lieberman Award |publisher=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame |date=January 29, 2018 |access-date=February 18, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181118122714/http://www.hoophall.com/news/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-womens-basketball-coaches-association-narrow-watch-list-for-2018-nancy-lieberman-award/ |archive-date=November 18, 2018 |url-status=dead }}</ref>
* The [[Ann Meyers Drysdale Award]], first presented in 2018 to the top shooting guard.<ref>{{cite press release |url=http://www.hoophall.com/news/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-womens-basketball-coaches-association-narrow-watch-list-for-2018-ann-meyers-drysdale-a/ |title=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame, Women's Basketball Coaches Association Narrow Watch List for 2018 Ann Meyers Drysdale Award |publisher=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame |date=January 30, 2018 |access-date=February 18, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191220140343/http://www.hoophall.com/news/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-womens-basketball-coaches-association-narrow-watch-list-for-2018-ann-meyers-drysdale-a/ |archive-date=December 20, 2019 |url-status=dead }}</ref>
* The [[Cheryl Miller Award]], first presented in 2018 to the top small forward.<ref>{{cite press release |url=http://www.hoophall.com/news/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-womens-basketball-coaches-association-narrow-watch-list-for-2018-cheryl-miller-award/ |title=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame, Women's Basketball Coaches Association Narrow Watch List for 2018 Cheryl Miller Award |publisher=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame |date=January 31, 2018 |access-date=February 18, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191130150905/http://www.hoophall.com/news/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-womens-basketball-coaches-association-narrow-watch-list-for-2018-cheryl-miller-award/ |archive-date=November 30, 2019 |url-status=dead }}</ref>
* The [[Katrina McClain Award]], first presented in 2018 to the top power forward.<ref>{{cite press release |url=http://www.hoophall.com/news/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-womens-basketball-coaches-association-narrow-watch-list-for-2018-katrina-mcclain-award/ |title=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame, Women's Basketball Coaches Association Narrow Watch List for 2018 Katrina McClain Award |publisher=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame |date=February 1, 2018 |access-date=February 18, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180221233957/http://www.hoophall.com/news/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-womens-basketball-coaches-association-narrow-watch-list-for-2018-katrina-mcclain-award/ |archive-date=February 21, 2018 |url-status=dead }}</ref>
* The [[Lisa Leslie Award]], first presented in 2018 to the top center.<ref>{{cite press release |url=http://www.hoophall.com/news/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-womens-basketball-coaches-association-narrow-watch-list-for-2018-lisa-leslie-award/ |title=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame, Women's Basketball Coaches Association Narrow Watch List for 2018 Lisa Leslie Award |publisher=Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame |date=February 2, 2018 |access-date=February 18, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191221185259/http://www.hoophall.com/news/naismith-memorial-basketball-hall-of-fame-womens-basketball-coaches-association-narrow-watch-list-for-2018-lisa-leslie-award/ |archive-date=December 21, 2019 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

As with the men's awards, the selection committee for the women's awards includes each award's namesake.

The Hall also formerly presented the [[Frances Pomeroy Naismith Award]] to two college seniors—one male player no taller than {{convert|72|in|m|sigfig=3}}, and one female player no taller than {{convert|68|in|m|sigfig=3}}—determined to have been the nation's best student-athletes. The men's award, given since 1969, was voted on by the National Association of Basketball Coaches (NABC), and the women's, given since 1984, by members of the [[Women's Basketball Coaches Association]]. Both awards were discontinued after the 2012–13 season.
