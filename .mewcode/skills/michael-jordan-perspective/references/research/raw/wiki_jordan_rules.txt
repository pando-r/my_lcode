The '''Jordan Rules''' was a basketball tactic employed by the [[Detroit Pistons]] of the [[National Basketball Association]] to limit scoring by the [[Chicago Bulls|Chicago Bulls']] [[Michael Jordan]]. 

== History ==
After the Chicago Bulls’ Michael Jordan scored 59 points against the Detroit Pistons in a game on April 3, 1988 at the [[Pontiac Silverdome]], Pistons coach [[Chuck Daly]] and his assistants, [[Ron Rothstein]] and [[Dick Versace]], devised a strategy "to play him tough, to physically challenge him and to vary its defenses so as to try to throw him off balance."<ref>30 for 30: Bad Boys. Dir. Zak Levitt. Perf. Isiah Thomas and Dennis Rodman. ESPN Films, 2014. DVD.</ref> Key players were [[Dennis Rodman]] and [[Bill Laimbeer]].

The Jordan Rules became part of the [[Bulls–Pistons rivalry|rivalry]] between the "Bad Boys" Pistons and Jordan's Bulls in the late 1980s and early 1990s.  

Daly described the Jordan Rules in an interview with ''[[Sports Illustrated]]'':<ref>{{cite web|url=http://sportsillustrated.cnn.com/2007/writers/jack_mccallum/05/28/daly.lebron/1.html |title='Jordan Rules' revisited (cont.) |first=Jack |last=McCallum |date=May 29, 2007 |access-date=December 11, 2013 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121020061711/http://sportsillustrated.cnn.com/2007/writers/jack_mccallum/05/28/daly.lebron/1.html |archive-date=October 20, 2012 }}</ref>

{{cquote|If Michael was at the point, we forced him left and doubled him. If he was on the left wing, we went immediately to a double team from the top. If he was on the right wing, we went to a slow double team. He could hurt you equally from either wing—hell, he could hurt you from the hot-dog stand—but we just wanted to vary the look. And if he was on the box, we doubled with a big guy.

The other rule was, any time he went by you, you had to nail him. If he was coming off a screen, nail him. We didn't want to be dirty—I know some people thought we were—but we had to make contact and be very physical.}}

On offense, the Detroit players Jordan was guarding would pass the basketball, forcing Jordan to work hard on both ends of the court.<ref>{{cite magazine |author1=[[Jack McCallum]] |date=6 November 1989 |title=Mission Impossible |url=http://sportsillustrated.cnn.com/si_online/flashbacks/jordan/891106/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20020414043539/http://sportsillustrated.cnn.com/si_online/flashbacks/jordan/891106/ |archive-date=14 April 2002 |access-date=21 January 2023 |magazine=[[Sports Illustrated]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |author1=Jeff Zillgitt |date=27 April 2020 |title='The Last Dance': How the Pistons employed 'The Jordan Rules' against Michael |url=https://eu.usatoday.com/story/sports/nba/bulls/2020/04/27/the-last-dance-revisiting-the-jordan-rules-used-pistons/3032835001/ |access-date=21 January 2023 |work=[[USA Today]] |archive-date=December 7, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221207185037/https://eu.usatoday.com/story/sports/nba/bulls/2020/04/27/the-last-dance-revisiting-the-jordan-rules-used-pistons/3032835001/ |url-status=live }}</ref> "I don't think Chuck Daly wanted to hurt him; he was just looking to wear him out", former Bulls center Will Perdue said years later.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Bucher |first=Ric |title=How Michael Jordan Broke 'The Jordan Rules' |url=https://bleacherreport.com/articles/2888183-how-michael-jordan-broke-the-jordan-rules |access-date=2021-04-23 |website=Bleacher Report |language=en |archive-date=January 14, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250114165347/https://bleacherreport.com/articles/2888183-how-michael-jordan-broke-the-jordan-rules |url-status=live }}</ref> In an [[ESPN]] ''[[30 for 30]]'', [[Joe Dumars]] said, "It was like the [[Da Vinci Code]], the formula to [[Coca-Cola]], and the Jordan rules".<ref>{{cite news |author1=James Herbert |date=15 May 2020 |title=The Jordan Rules: What 'The Last Dance' documentary doesn't say about Pistons' defense against Michael Jordan |url=https://www.cbssports.com/nba/news/the-jordan-rules-what-the-last-dance-documentary-doesnt-say-about-pistons-defense-against-michael-jordan/ |access-date=21 January 2023 |work=[[CBS Sports]] |archive-date=January 21, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230121104408/https://www.cbssports.com/nba/news/the-jordan-rules-what-the-last-dance-documentary-doesnt-say-about-pistons-defense-against-michael-jordan/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

The Pistons sometimes used the strategy against other prolific scoring guards.<ref>{{cite web |title=PISTONS: Reliving the Pistons-Bulls Rivalry |url=http://www.nba.com/pistons/history/bullsrivalry_030314.html |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20050927205653/http://www.nba.com/pistons/history/bullsrivalry_030314.html |archive-date=September 27, 2005 |website=[[NBA.com]]}}</ref> 

The Jordan Rules were most effective for the Pistons during their first three playoff meetings with the Bulls.{{cn|date=August 2025}} In 1988, Detroit beat Chicago four games to one. then defeated the Bulls in six games in 1989 and seven games in 1990, both of the latter victories leading to championships.

=== Response ===
To counter the Jordan Rules, Jordan bulked up to 215 pounds to withstand the physical play of his opponents, and the Bulls began mastering the [[triangle offense]] behind head coach [[Phil Jackson]] and assistant [[Tex Winter]]. The new offense emphasized more ball movement and spacing, enabling Jordan to share the offensive load rather than shouldering it. In the [[1990–91 NBA season|1990–91 season]], the Bulls won a franchise-record 61 games and swept the Pistons in the 1991 Eastern Conference Finals. Soon after, the Bulls won their first NBA title, beating the [[Los Angeles Lakers]] in the [[1991 NBA Finals|NBA Finals]] 4 games to 1. The Pistons qualified for the playoffs again in 1992, 1996, 1997, 1999, and 2000, but did not advance to the second round until 2002.

The teams that faced the Pistons in the playoffs disliked the Jordan Rules. It was rumored that Jordan, [[Magic Johnson]], and [[Larry Bird]] lobbied for the Pistons' [[Isiah Thomas]] to be excluded from the [[1992 United States men's Olympic basketball team]] (also known as the "Dream Team"). Although Daly was named head coach of Team USA, Thomas was seen as the ringleader of the Pistons' implementation of the Jordan Rules.<ref>{{cite book |last1=MacMullan |first1=Jackie |last2=Johnson |first2=Magic |last3=Bird |first3=Larry |title=When the Game Was Ours |url=https://archive.org/details/whengamewasours00bird |url-access=registration |date=2009 |publisher=Houghton Mifflin Harcourt Publishing Company |page=[https://archive.org/details/whengamewasours00bird/page/262 262]|isbn=978-0-547-22547-0 }}</ref>

The Jordan Rules strategy was used by the [[New York Knicks]] from 1992 to 1998, under the tutelage of [[Pat Riley]] who served as their head coach from the 1991-92 to the 1994-95 seasons. However, the Knicks were not successful as Detroit in containing Jordan and the Bulls. Jordan faced the Knicks in the NBA Playoffs in 1991, 1992, 1993, and 1996, and won NBA titles in all four of those seasons.
